## Empirical narrative hazards

### “Mixed” does not mean underpowered

Section 7.2 now states this directly before the results table. The registered
aggregate required three simultaneous conditions. Transfer passed decisively;
boundary superiority and in-regime noninferiority failed decisively at their
registered margins. The intervals are narrow, and the sign of each difference
was shared by all eight fits. “Mixed” therefore names opposing effects. It does
not report inconclusive estimation.

The prose remains constructive about what survives: the reusable statistic
supported strong changed-tolerance generalization in this setting, while the
tested training/calibration/decoder pipeline did not turn that information
into good current-threshold fidelity or useful coverage. The reverse
cross-entropy comparisons remain descriptive because they were not registered
as reverse confirmatory propositions.

### Low false assertions remain adjacent to fallback

The false-support and false-refutation rows share one table with missed support,
missed refutation, target-weighted fallback mass, and four-outcome fidelity.
The next paragraph states that `0.9962` fallback mass is almost universal and
that the exact mask establishes nonreactivation of excluded plans without
establishing useful licensing or acceptable fallback risk. No isolated
low-error sentence can reasonably be read as an operational-usefulness result.

### The dead band is explained without being declared causal

The mechanism paragraph identifies the observable geometry: accepted intervals
were wider than oracle intervals on average; an interval crossing a threshold
decodes to Open; and endpoint overshoot at equality also converts support to
Open. This makes the miss/fallback pattern intelligible. The same paragraph
states that the experiment did not identify causal shares among objective,
fit, calibration, threshold construction, decoder, generator, or their
interactions. The account is therefore theory-consistent and testable, without
being either a mystery label or a proved causal decomposition.
