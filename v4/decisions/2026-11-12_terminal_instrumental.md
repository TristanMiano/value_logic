# Terminal and instrumental values: the author's clarification after W6

**Author:** Tristan Miano. **Date:** November 12, 2026, America/Los_Angeles.
Recorded by **Claude (Fable 5.2)**. Planning record; zero recurrence credit.

## What prompted it

The first version of the W6 reader's summary
([PDF](../reports/W6_cycle_summary.pdf), PR #40, by Claude) said that cycle
P4-R6-C001 "qualifies an essay claim instead of confirming it": the essay
[Is Utility All You Need?](../../posts/utility_all_you_need.txt) says the
distinction between terminal and instrumental values is "largely gone", and
the cycle found that entrenchment of a useful priority is conditional. The
summary called that "an informative correction".

The author replied that this misreads the claim. His words:

> For one thing, a "terminal" goal was something I allowed for the sake of
> argument: suppose a goal had been literally clamped or held fixed by some
> undefined means. But this is the more general situation: We do not know if
> any goals can be literally clamped, or what would clamp them if they could
> be. We then ask: what does "terminal" even mean in this context?

> Yes, the entrenchment is conditional -- it was stated as a caveat, or as a
> limitation, but rather this was in fact the mechanism I was looking for and
> proposed exists. So the conditional mechanism seems to be what is causing
> the "degrees". And those degrees are the blurring I was gesturing at.

> What happens when the entrenchment is not removed? As in, something all
> agents have to deal with for all time? I want to know what happens in that
> situation. Do these "entrenched" goals resemble goals we associate with
> "terminality" (what something ultimately wants)? And that last question
> doesn't seem to have been answered at all.

On the candidate meanings of "terminal", citing his project
[policy_value_isomorph](https://github.com/TristanMiano/policy_value_isomorph):

> It finds that a policy network could be imitated by a well-chosen value
> network; thus, a value function by itself (minus the choice of harness) can
> basically define an entire policy. The value function is a mapping from all
> input states to a value, so it opens the question of whether the terminal /
> instrumental distinction is erased or at least ambiguous. We haven't decided
> whether the entire value function is "terminal", or whether we consider just
> the maxima of it its "ultimate" goals. It could even apply only as an
> abstraction or high-level summary of what it seems to do.

On his project
[corrigibility-games](https://github.com/TristanMiano/corrigibility-games),
which studies agents that begin with terminal goals for the sake of argument
and are pressured or forced to update them:

> This project may be better left cited in the next module (meta-value),
> however.

## What this record fixes

1. **The reading of the essay claim.** A clamped terminal goal is a device
   granted for the sake of argument, not a premise. The claim is that lasting
   usefulness entrenches a value by degrees. Conditional entrenchment (R6.4)
   is a mechanism for the claim, and the degrees it produces are the blurring
   the essay describes. The summary's "correction" wording was Claude's
   misreading and is removed in the revised PDF. It was not a finding of
   Codex's cycle, whose own assessment does not use that framing.
2. **Two questions the author regards as open after W6.**
   - What "terminal" means when no goal is known to be clamped: the whole
     value function, its maxima, or a summary of what the agent does.
   - What happens when the entrenching pressure is never removed, and
     whether the resulting priorities resemble what something ultimately
     wants.
3. **How they will be handled** (the author's direction after Claude's
   advice, same day):
   - The W6 summary PDF is corrected.
   - A short W6 addendum settles a stated meaning of "terminal" and assembles
     what W6 already implies about permanent pressure. Codex (GPT-6) is to
     perform it; the
     [brief](../notebook/W6_terminality_addendum/BRIEF.md) says how.
   - The conflict and selection questions, and the corrigibility-games
     setting, are added to W7 as item W7.6 in [TODO_v4.md](../../TODO_v4.md).
