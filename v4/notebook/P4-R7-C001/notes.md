## W7.1 · A scarce pool of training places

A community needs two services. Agents with different encoded priorities choose different jobs even when the wage to the current worker is fixed. Their work changes the loads on each service. A fixed pool of apprenticeships is then allocated to mentors. A mentor's per-capita copying weight is proportional to (demand/share of providers) to a power $\alpha$. Treat mentoring slots as finite in supply, and normalize the weights across providers. Compare with genuinely inherited descendant counts having the same relative weights, and with an individual's weights on dispositions only if its update and actual action decoder agree. A teacher's prestige, personal income and its students' learning can differ. If an absent priority has no mentor, it must first arise by revision in transmission.

At equal demand, with positive initial shares, the odds of the first priority just after successful mentors are sampled equal its original odds to the power $1-\alpha$. Switching the copied priority with symmetric probability $\mu$ then scales the deviation of this share from one half by $1-2\mu$. Write $l$ for log odds. The update has form $h((1-\alpha)l)$ for $h(z)=2\operatorname{atanh}((1-2\mu)\tanh(z/2))$. Its derivative decreases strictly on positive arguments and $h$ is odd and bounded for $0<\mu<1/2$. At low gain all trajectories converge to balanced diversity. At $(\alpha-1)(1-2\mu)>1$ a unique attracting alternation in absolute log odds follows by strict concavity and the slope at zero. Initial exact balance remains balanced. For $\alpha=3,\mu=3/16$, the shares $1/4,3/4$ form the two-cycle. Test finite copying noise and compare identical time averages with incompatible trajectories. Examine whether delaying the scarcity signal changes the threshold and when another internal motive could yield identical actions.

Closest adjacent mechanisms include Tilman, Plotkin and Akçay's environmental feedback games, Chotibut et al.'s congestion-learning instabilities and Harper's algebraic comparison of replicator weighting and inference. Our service demand is fixed while each generation changes the per-provider service backlog. Check whether exact observations distinguish this mechanism from an externally imposed periodic schedule.

Realized service to clients is the sum of the smaller of capacity and demand in each job. It is $1-|x-1/2|$ and therefore averages only $3/4$ on the exact cycle although both average loads are exactly right. At known copying error, a test population initialized to a different interior composition separates scarcity-dependent allocation from a reward schedule replayed from the original path. For example the observed pre-error log odds of the next share divided by starting log odds equals $1-\alpha$ throughout the balanced-demand model. A phase-locked intervention keeps the calendar fixed while changing the mentor mix. The population samples effects of actions and transmission. An identical aggregate sequence could otherwise be achieved by externally changing rewards.

## W7.2 · Selection on when to change a priority

An adviser recommends whether to replace yesterday's installed job ranking. A change is useful with probability $p$, giving real gain $v$ when adopted and real loss $l$ for an inappropriate change. These returns fund later copies. The installed ranking determines the downstream action. Rigid ignores appeals. Open follows all appeals. Guarded pays $k$ per episode for independent evidence with true-accept probability $s$ and false-accept probability $f$. On a true occasion the informed adviser recommends the change. A false proposal incurs expected fee $h$ and yields the adviser $b$ only if accepted. An adviser learns the rule with probability $q$, otherwise knows its population frequencies. This types each internal value, the rule judging it, the adviser’s incentives and external return.

Assume $bf<h<b$. Known open types receive false offers, known guarded types deter them. With fraction $x$ open among open/guarded, hidden rules induce a false offer iff $x>(h/b-f)/(1-f)$, with mixing allowed at equality. Put $y$ for the probability of a false proposal conditional on a hidden client and an unchanged world. The open-minus-guarded copying payoff is $D_0-(1-p)l[q+(1-q)(1-f)y]$ with $D_0=p(1-s)v+k$. This gives a threshold in observability above which guarding pays even when the pool is honest. At $p=1/4,v=3,l=2,s=.9,f=.1,k=.15,h/b=.55$, a completely hidden equilibrium has half open and sends a false proposal on one sixth of unchanged occasions. Both earn .5 above baseline. Under full visibility guarded earns .525, open loses .75, rigid earns zero. At $p=.01$ and full visibility rigid beats both. Consider the public-good problem of individually costly verification, and compare a smooth heterogeneous reporting-cost model, neutral evidence, and a genuinely expanding menu of meta-rules. The example is distinct from R6.10's static commitment about a downstream division of wealth. Dekel, Ely and Yilankaya explicitly distinguish observable subjective preferences and material fitness and study stability against a broader mutant class.

## W7.3 · Who pays to discover the task partition?

Subjects see four task names. A local environment uses one of two crossed two-class partitions, equally likely. Within each class the same performer repeats its success bit; across classes its bits are independent fair draws. First performances have identical marginal laws. A judge buys repeated performances on two tasks grouped together by the current convention and separated by the other candidate. Each fresh performer supplies an independent pair. One discrepancy certifies the other partition; if all $d$ pairs match, accept the convention. The error is $2^{-d-1}$. Stop at the first discrepancy and expected purchased pairs are $d/2+1-2^{-d}$. In deployment, assign a worker who previously succeeded to one of the two potential transfer tasks. A correct partition gives certain success, the wrong one half. The improvement in earning a reward $B$ over default is $B(1-2^{-d})/4$. Distinctions are learned from judgments about actual reappearance.

Now transmit the investigative rule or an imitative rule according to net earnings. A nonpayer in a patch receives a local investigator's result with chance $\rho x$ when the investigator fraction is $x$, else uses the standing convention. Opportunity to copy and reward for doing good work are distinct. Paying $a$ for agreement with the public convention reduces the informational advantage to $\Gamma=(1-2^{-d})(B/4-a/2)$. Both types can use the same report. The investigator's advantage is $(1-\rho x)\Gamma-c(d/2+1-2^{-d})$. This supports loss, coexistence or near-fixation according to financing and disclosure. If next convention is majority among reports across patches, the chosen default remains a majority under either starting convention for finite $d$. Validate directions, finite populations, task change and welfare. Compare Rogers (1988) and Enquist, Eriksson and Ghirlanda (2007) on who pays for social knowledge; W1's recovery from recurrences and W6.16's payoff-guided acquisition supply complementary pieces.

## W7.4 · Why would the affected parties accept a selected rule?

Distinguish increase in copies, service to a specified end, and authorization by those who bear the costs. One positive normative premise is informed, voluntary ex ante acceptance by all affected parties with a feasible outside option and a shared understanding of the lottery. It is a limited justification for adopting that arrangement among them. Work with actual role-specific expected gains $g_{ri}$ of feasible arrangements $r$ over each role's outside option. They can choose mixtures implementable by one public lottery. The guaranteed joint surplus is $\gamma=\max_x\min_i(xG)_i$. Individual gains each attainable on a different infeasible joint promise cannot be added up.

Can environmental feedback actually find such a joint lottery? Let copied institutional arrangements grow in proportion to exponential weighted role gains, with review attention shifting toward roles poorly served. This is two-sided exponential weights in a finite zero-sum game. Freund and Schapire’s game-playing argument yields average true worst-role gain at least $\gamma$ minus the two regret terms. A reproductive bonus $b_r$ unrelated to anyone's welfare changes the game to $G_{ri}+b_r$. The role updater is unchanged as the average bonus cancels across roles. Thus the actual worst-role margin is at least $\gamma-\operatorname{range}(b)$ apart from finite learning error. Test whether that bound can be tight, affordable information and suppressed dissent. With gains $(3,-1)$ and $(-1,2)$ the mixture selecting the first rule $3/7$ gives both $5/7$ despite opposition to either pure rule. Independent conditional reasons for revision come from each person's own valued opportunities. Evolutionary origin of the rule alone supplies no such reason. Compare participation losses, risk and exclusion, plus a read of Street’s argument about an adaptive explanation of evaluative attitudes.
