## Alignment first: what moved

The cycle advances **C1 and C2**: evaluating a judgment by the recurrence it
predicts can select a representation of factual task structure, and that
representation can change together with the judge's influence. This is more
than assigning a value to an already accepted fact. An initially uninformative
representation can be replaced because a competing judgment captures a
repeatable difference. The environment can discipline this process even when
the judgments themselves partly cause what will recur.

**C3 gains a conditional mechanism**, not a truth theory: a specified learner
converges to a fixed environmental law under continuing informative exposure.
**C5 gains convergence results for recursive weights and forecasts**; logical
self-trust and a Löb-type statement remain untouched. C4, C6 and C7 have not
advanced in this W1 cycle. A decision-theoretic connection below prepares a
W6 question; it does not count as having completed W6 or W7.

The strongest faithful reading supported is:

> When a judge's forecasts are evaluated on repeatable, separating evidence,
> useful distinctions in those forecasts identify the corresponding factual
> distinctions. With a sufficient candidate vocabulary and sustained learning,
> an explicit coupled selection process can recover the environmental law even
> when its current judgments influence the observations it receives.

The qualifiers matter. Repeated evidence supplies information that isolated
performances lack; proper scoring makes that information valuable; a separating
channel fixes which facts can be recovered. Calibration, exploration and
representation proposals are presently designed into the learner. Explaining
why agents acquire and maintain those practices is the next environmental
selection question, rather than a reason to discard the construction.

## When a distinction changes a decision

The connection to value can be stated without treating log score as the only
purpose an agent might have. Fix a finite context X, a finite future outcome Y,
and a representation c(X). Keep the outcome and the available actions fixed.

**Elementary decision-sufficiency lemma.** If P(Y|X) depends on X only through
c(X), then seeing X instead of c(X) cannot improve the optimal expected payoff
in any finite action problem whose payoff is a bounded function of action and
Y. Conversely, if these conditional laws differ on a positive-probability
c-cell, there is a bounded two-action problem for which observing X strictly
improves expected payoff.

*Proof.* In the first case every action's conditional expected payoff is already
measurable from c(X), so maximizing it requires no extra context. In the second
case choose an event E whose probability p(X)=P(Y in E|X) is nonconstant on
such a cell K, and let t=E[p(X)|c(X)=K]. The actions have payoffs 0 and
1_E(Y)-t, both bounded. On K the best coarse expected payoff is zero. A judge
seeing X chooses the second action exactly when p(X)>t, earning the strictly
positive improvement

```math
P(c(X)=K)\,\mathbb E\bigl[(p(X)-t)_+\mid c(X)=K\bigr]>0.
```

Outside K it can imitate the coarse optimum. Nonconstancy and the definition
of the conditional mean give the strict inequality. QED.

This is a classical decision-sufficiency fact, derived here for the finite
setting, not a novelty claim. It makes the obligation precise: a sufficiently
rich family of valued decisions demands the predictive distinctions, while a
particular narrow objective may not use all of them. Additional correlated
side information must be part of the declared conditioning experiment; the
lemma does not license an unchanged comparison after adding arbitrary signals.
See the bounded [source comparison](review_s6.md#closing-source-comparison-november-11).

**A broad declared goal family.** There is also a quantitative version, so the
bridge need not rest on picking one convenient threshold after seeing the
model. Draw a target event y uniformly from the m possible outcomes and a price
t uniformly from [0,1], independently of (X,Y). Reveal the goal (y,t) before
the action. The available payoffs are 0 and 1{Y=y}-t. Write p_y(X)=P(Y=y|X)
and pbar_y(c)=E[p_y(X)|c(X)]. The expected advantage of the full context over
the representation, averaged across this family of goals, is exactly

```math
\frac{1}{2m}\mathbb E\sum_y
       \bigl[p_y(X)-\bar p_y(c(X))\bigr]^2.                 \tag{D.1}
```

Indeed, the optimal expected payoff at a fixed price is (p-t)_+, and
integrating it from t=0 to 1 gives p squared divided by two. The conditional
variance identity proves (D.1). If each event's price density is instead
bounded below by lambda>0 throughout [0,1], the advantage is at least lambda
times (D.1): at every fixed price, conditional Jensen makes the expected
full-versus-coarse advantage nonnegative. Thus losing at most epsilon value
on average in this family forces predictive squared error at most
2m epsilon/lambda. Exact optimality forces the predictive quotient.

A finite menu supplies a useful approximate version. Use J equally likely
prices (j-1/2)/J, j=1,...,J, instead of the continuum. Its average decision
advantage differs from (D.1) by at most 1/(8J squared). To see this, midpoint
integration of (p-t)_+ is exact except in the single interval containing p;
its underestimate is between zero and 1/(8J squared). The difference of the
fine and coarse errors is bounded by that same amount. Consequently average
decision loss at most epsilon on this finite menu implies

```math
\mathbb E\sum_y[p_y-\bar p_y]^2
\le 2m\epsilon+\frac{m}{4J^2}.                            \tag{D.2}
```

The finite menu cannot resolve every arbitrarily small difference: probabilities
lying between the same adjacent prices can induce identical actions. Increasing
the price resolution gives the explicit constructive approximation in (D.2).
These are optimal-action values given the information; neither bound pays the
cost of discovering or computing that information.

Coverage is the substantive condition. If prices occur only outside an
interval [l,u], and all p_y values being merged lie strictly inside it, every
observed price induces the same action for that event despite the different
probabilities. Restoring a positive density of prices inside the interval
restores strict decision value. This pairs a useful multi-goal route to
factual distinctions with the condition that makes it informative. It is an
elementary decision-theoretic synthesis; whether realistic environments or
selection pressures supply such goal coverage is left to W6/W7.
The close established result is the cost-loss mixture representation of proper
scores in [Gneiting and Raftery (2007), section 3.2, Theorem 3](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf).
Its uniform threshold mixture gives the quadratic score, up to scale and
outcome-only constants. The use here connects that established correspondence
to W1's recovered concepts and a precise next question about goal coverage.

**A compatible consequence of coupled recovery.** The same uniform-price
experiment gives an operational meaning to R1.9's convergence. For an arbitrary
probability forecast r(X), use the action "buy" exactly when r_y(X)>t. Its
average regret relative to the fully informed optimal action is
E[sum_y(p_y-r_y) squared]/(2m): for one event, mistakes occur only between
the prices p and r, and integrating their payoff loss gives (p-r) squared
divided by two. In R1.9 put p_y(x)=mu_y F_xy and r_y(x)=mu_y bar R_xy.
The average reference-environment decision regret is therefore at most

```math
\frac{\max_y\mu_y}{2m}\|F-\bar R\|_\mu^2
\le\frac{\max_y\mu_y}{2me}
       \bigl[e\|F\|_\mu^2-\mathcal P\bigr]
\longrightarrow0.                                         \tag{D.3}
```

This is a mathematical composition with declared compatibility: the goals
are independent of the context and outcome, contexts have the reference law
mu, and the decision pays a reference-outcome wager without changing F.
It evaluates the current forecasts while retaining R1.9's learning rule and
observation experiment; replacing its selection score by wager income would
require checking the resulting dynamics separately.
It is not an implemented universal controller or a certificate computed
without knowing F. If taking the action changes the environmental law, that
causal decision problem needs a new argument. The finite-menu bound above
concerns optimal actions from each representation, not arbitrary miscalibrated
forecast thresholds.

For a concrete example use R1.15's three-event directed cycle, with uniform
current events and next-event density F=1+0.6K. Its most likely successor
follows the preferred direction around the cycle. Bayes-optimal 0/1 prediction
has the following **population** accuracies, checked with rational arithmetic:

| Information supplied | Fixed prediction target | Best accuracy |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| No current-event label | Exact next symbol | 1/3 = 33.33% |
| Current symbol 1 versus {2,3} | Exact next symbol | 7/15 = 46.67% |
| Exact current symbol | Exact next symbol | 8/15 = 53.33% |
| No current-event label | Whether the next symbol is 2 | 2/3 = 66.67% |
| Current symbol 1 versus {2,3} | Whether the next symbol is 2 | 31/45 = 68.89% |

These are distinct prediction questions; compare rows only within a fixed
target. In particular, R1.15's zero mutual information for a **shared binary
label on both roles** does not say that a binary input is useless for the
original target. Its role-specific labels have positive information even at
smaller cycle strengths where they do not change the optimal 0/1 action.
Another payoff threshold can expose that information, as the lemma shows.
The calculations are in [directed_reappearance.py](directed_reappearance.py)
and [its output](directed_reappearance.json), including exact checks of
(D.1) across the five three-symbol partitions at four cycle strengths, and
100 finite-menu comparisons for (D.2).

## What is implemented together

There are three useful executable compositions, not one universal learner.

1. **Selected-task evidence to prediction (R1.10).** Generate a candidate
   partition, validate a dependence witness on fresh data, then train a
   forecast on another fresh sample. In the recorded two-component world,
   the expected-score certificate is positive (0.00238 nats). Exact population
   evaluation gives a learned gain of 0.32352 nats over the coarse forecast.
   A correctly specified two-component mixture estimator attains 0.32992;
   that stronger comparison is retained.
2. **Self-influenced evidence to adoption (R1.13, Corollary 28B).** A controlled
   probe creates an observable event that cannot have been caused by the
   learner's own feedback. Its confidence interval supports a choice between
   a coarse rule and a useful fine predictor; the adopted predictor then
   calibrates from ordinary observations. At environmental mean 0.6 and
   share 0.05, all 12 recorded paths adopted the fine predictor. At 100,000
   rounds their final mean squared error was 0.00889, compared with 0.32387
   for the passive coupled learner and 0.00868 for always using the fine
   learner. This tests the benefit of repairing passive discovery, not
   superiority to a learner already given the useful model. At the exact
   adoption boundary all 12 paths were still querying at the cap, showing
   why probe cost and an unresolved stopping disposition matter.
3. **Coalition evidence to recovered kinds (R1.14).** Using independent
   performers and controlled task pairs, empirical cross-performance moments
   recover four hidden task classes from eight labels in the recorded world.
   Every single judge is uninformative there, and even the one-performance
   full panel is marginally uniform. The implementation uses the known
   three-feature catalogue and a declared separation gap; it receives neither
   the latent traits nor the hidden class map as input.

The population selection theorem, the general coalition theorem and the
directed concept-birth model are additional mathematical constructions. Their
combination into an adaptive finite-data system has not been implemented or
proved. In particular, the coalition experiment uses controlled assignments;
it cannot simply inherit the selected-task bound without a new sampling
argument. The directed dynamics use a fixed reference context law, not an
arbitrary adaptively controlled Markov trajectory.

## Contribution and established alternatives

The contribution is chiefly a **characterization and useful synthesis** of
recursive judgment: explicit quotients, fixed points, coupled selection,
observation contracts and decision consequences that make the founding thesis
testable. Quantitative and boundary results are candidate mathematical additions
within that synthesis; priority over all related literature has not been
established. Recovery does not identify raw latent names beyond their observable
equivalence classes.

The enabling mathematics is substantial: proper scoring and sufficiency,
predictive-state representations, peer prediction, latent-class estimation,
attenuation correction, Perron convergence, composite likelihood, dependence
statistics, confidence sequences and performative prediction. The linked result
files name the relevant sources and read scopes. The work should be assessed
by what it makes precise and connects, alongside its actual empirical deltas.

An implementation of the same update under an ordinary statistical label is
an identical method, not an independent competing success. A full-label
estimator, a correctly specified mixture model, an always-fine predictor and
a full-joint temporal estimator are genuinely different alternatives. Some
outperform the bounded recursive constructions in the recorded settings.
Those comparisons locate the remaining discovery, representation and resource
costs; they do not erase the demonstrated link between successful judgment
and factual distinctions.

## Disposition and next questions

**W1: advance at the stated scope; available to recur.** The cycle gives enough
core feasibility to take the recovered objects and their explicit conditions
into the planned environmental modules. **M1 as a whole remains pending** its
W6/W7 environmental contracts; phase four remains open. No new recurrence is
started by this assessment.

The next authorized research cycle should normally be **W6, instrumental
convergence**, asking when different actual goals reward a shared separating
set of distinctions. The decision lemma gives a bridge to investigate, rather
than assuming every goal values all truth. Construct goal/environment families
that reward accurate distinctions and revision, and compare families where
coarse proxies suffice; seek conditions that enlarge the successful family.
W7 should then ask which environments select the needed exploration and
revision rules, alongside regimes that select stable conventions and the
changes that restore grounding.

The highest-priority return to W1 is **endogenous concept proposals with an
explicit budget**: construct an observed learner that can acquire a useful
joint distinction before any constituent split pays, and characterize the
conditions under which its expected decision value funds that search. Preserve
the full-joint and always-fine alternatives and price their representation
costs as well. Other named obligations are finite adaptive-catalogue guarantees,
fallible or changing feedback channels, strategic credit, nonstationary
environments and independent review of the principal proofs. C5's logical
self-trust target belongs to W4 and has not been quietly replaced by a
stochastic convergence theorem.
