## Main result and evidence

The implemented paid-feedback construction learns which factual distinctions
support different agents' purposes. At high overlap, both learn the common
hidden factor; at lower overlap they learn their respective private factor.
After all declared training and representation charges, selective purchases
raise whole-run expected net return from 0.131509 to **0.136466** and from
0.122723 to **0.127174**, versus a known constant forecast's **0.125000**.
The paired gains are 0.004957 and 0.004451 per opportunity over forced
purchases. These are two finite synthetic environments with 32 paired
replicas, not a general performance estimate. Near the shared/private tie,
finite disagreement remains visible and is inexpensive in decision value;
more evidence reduces loss. A separate known-law reuse calculation shows
when a one-time representation cost makes both facts worth retaining.

The broader portfolio gives explicit conditions for persistent but revisable
priorities, actual action improvement from survival-filtered examples,
conditional policy/value reconstruction, shared observation provision,
context-dependent adequacy and correcting an internal bonus that distracts
from the original purpose. Three- and four-state inverse-value witnesses
supply generic positive identification; an everywhere-positive ambiguous
family and a spanning-reward repair keep that conclusion correctly scoped.
R6.1--R6.16 and the two supplements are linked in the assessment and
[results index](../../results/README.md).

All 23 scientific programs were executed during the cycle and their small
outputs retained. The independent summary/algebra check adds 360 comparisons
recomputed from saved per-run data and 24 SymPy equalities for the two
inverse-value witnesses. Exact rational checks support the calculations;
