## A judge can be rewarded for accurately predicting its own effect

A judge announces $p$, the predicted fraction of successful judgments after others have heard that announcement. Let $q(p)$ be the actual probability of success under that announcement. A self-consistent report satisfies $p=q(p)$. The judge affects what is judged. In a simple community an independently working fraction $\alpha$ of decisions succeeds at rate $\theta$; the rest follow the public expectation, so $q(p)=\alpha\theta+(1-\alpha)p$. Retraining the prediction to the observed mean converges to $\theta$ for $0<\alpha\le1$. Yet schools selected for low one-outcome squared error can leave that accurate prediction behind. At $\theta=1/2$ their expected cost is
$$
\mathbb E(p-Y)^2=1/4+(2\alpha-1)(p-1/2)^2.
$$
Below $\alpha=1/2$ predicting either extreme earns more. The judge can make a population easy to predict by moving it. Perdomo et al. distinguish performative stability from optimality, and Oesterheld et al. prove a broad limitation for single-outcome strictly proper scores. [Perdomo et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/perdomo20a.html), [Oesterheld et al. (2023), Proposition 1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v216/oesterheld23a.html).

**Paired evaluation.** Give the *same* announcement to two conditionally independent audiences from the same environment. Use loss $(p-Y_1)(p-Y_2)$. Its expectation is $(p-q(p))^2$. A school can therefore earn greater expected copying resources by better anticipating the consequences of its own judgment. For arbitrary response curves admitting fixed points, the minimizing reports are exactly the fixed points. If $q$ has Lipschitz constant $L<1$ on $[0,1]$ and $p_*$ is its fixed point, $|p-p_*|\le|p-q(p)|/(1-L)$ by the triangle inequality. If copying advantage is baseline minus the expected paired loss plus a report-dependent bonus of span $\delta$, any maximizer lies within $\sqrt{\delta}/(1-L)$ of $p_*$ when that point is available. The statement uses mean returns across placements or additive log growth paid on the paired loss. Gambling an entire finite school on one pair has a different objective.

This evaluation can be implemented by charging the mean of the two observed squared errors **after subtracting half the squared disagreement of the audiences**. Expanding the squares yields the product. It uses observed performance twice and never asks an oracle which announcement was correct. Paired estimation and multi-observation loss functions are established techniques. The contribution here is the specified ecology of self-prediction, its rival one-outcome scoring ecology and a threshold for shared feedback. [Casalaina-Martin et al. (2017)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v65/casalaina-martin17a.html).

If audiences share one draw with probability $\rho$ and otherwise respond independently, expected paired loss includes $\rho q(p)(1-q(p))$. At $\theta=1/2$ its varying term is $[\alpha^2-\rho(1-\alpha)^2](p-1/2)^2$. At $\alpha=1/4$ the central, accurate forecast is selected precisely for **$\rho<1/9$**, all reports tie at the boundary, and the extremes win above it. Two identifiers for one audience do not supply an independent evaluation. When $\alpha=0$, independent sampling can still measure self-consistency, but every $p$ induces its own matching outcome. A claim about conditions without the announcement then requires an independent intervention. This is a stochastic fixed-point semantics for a judge of its own effect; a modal derivability predicate and a Löb theorem are further W4 obligations.

There is also a direct production cost. Suppose $p$ units of capacity must be installed before demand from $n$ customers arrives. Under- or over-provision incurs cost $(p-\bar Y)^2$ in material and missed work. Customers share one common draw with probability $\rho$ and are otherwise independent under the announcement. Its expectation equals $(p-q(p))^2+[\rho+(1-\rho)/n]q(p)(1-q(p))$. At $\alpha=1/4,\theta=1/2$ the center wins precisely when the bracket is below $1/9$. Ten independent customers suffice (nine tie). A shared-draw rate of $1/20$ requires sixteen and $1/10$ requires eighty-two. At or above $1/9$ increasing the batch alone cannot make the center strictly win. The material reason for learning self-consistency in this example is the cost of provisioning aggregate demand. [Procurement check](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/frontier.py).

Shared difficulty, social influence and other dependence can still link these amounts. No forecast announcement was randomized, so this archive checks what constitutes an observation, while the forecast mechanism above remains a constructed experiment. ### Different stable self-judgments can share a factual commitment

Let a fraction $\alpha$ work from direct evidence and the rest follow the majority of three independent endorsements, each favoring success with probability $p$ after announcement $p$. The two equally frequent tasks have direct success rates $\theta_+=.75$ and $\theta_-=.25$. The resulting success probabilities are
$$
q_d(p)=(1-\alpha)(3p^2-2p^3)+\alpha\theta_d.
$$
With $\alpha=3/11$, pooled forecasts $1/4,1/2,3/4$ are all self-consistent. The pooled response minus $p$ factors as $-2(1-\alpha)(p-1/2)[p^2-p+\alpha/(2(1-\alpha))]$. At the outer roots the slope of revision to the next observed mean is $9/11$, and at the middle it is $12/11$. Monotonicity and the sign of the displacement give convergence to the lower or upper root from the respective side. Meanwhile independent paired evaluation of the pooled forecast assigns the same optimal *expected copying return* to all three roots. Within-agent revision can move away from a forecast that selection by self-consistency rewards as highly as any other.

Nevertheless **every forecast**, stable or otherwise, has the same grounded conditional difference $q_+(p)-q_-(p)=3/22$. A worker offered the first task at a premium of .1 gains $2/55$ in either settled community. If only first-task receipts from the low-forecast community and second-task receipts from the high-forecast community are published, the apparent difference is instead $-4/11$. Matching the two tasks at the same prior announcement repairs the comparison. The mean of their realized difference measures the invariant even when the choice of announcement changes with past evidence. This is a common fact about what one can *do*, compatible with conflicting accurate predictions about what different communities *will do*. For $\alpha>1/3$ the pooled response is a contraction; for $0<\alpha<1/3$ it has three roots. The unanchored limit $\alpha=0$ removes the grounded contrast. [Multiple-judgment and price checks](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/frontier.py).

## When is checking where a judgment came from worth paying for?

In a new workplace a hidden fact is equally likely either way. Each genuine test has accuracy $3/4$. With equal probability three reviewers either use three independent tests or all cite the same one. Voting patterns are public. Authenticated provenance reveals which process occurred. Equal likelihoods and independence are conditional on the fact. On a split vote, the majority has posterior accuracy $3/4$. On a unanimous vote it has accuracy $3/4$ if copied and $27/28$ if independent. To a judge observing all votes but no provenance the optimal posterior is $75/92$ on unanimity. Unanimity occurs with probability $23/32$.

Consider accepting a costly assignment that earns $1-.9$ if the reported answer is correct and loses $.9$ otherwise, with abstention worth zero. The strongest vote-only rule always abstains; after checking provenance an investigator accepts an independent unanimous report. The gain is **$9/640$ per workplace**, so an investigator checking only unanimous reports can pay up to **$9/460$ per check**. For unconstrained probability forecasts with log reward the corresponding gross advantage over the optimal vote-only posterior is .02910 per workplace, the conditional mutual information of the underlying fact and the lineage given the votes. Both procedures choose the same majority if paid only for a correct binary label. A consequential decision can select careful judgment of judges even when plain classification accuracy cannot distinguish them.

## A cycle can license the use of evidence while retaining its physical anchors

At a fixed time each node $i$ owns a set $O_i$ of authenticated event records, including the task, the time a prediction was committed and the physical outcome. For each potential delegation $i\to j$, predeclare a finite audit batch. Its gate opens only when the whole batch is present and passes the agreed scoring test. On a frozen ledger this condition is monotone in the set of records held: adding consistent records cannot change an already checked batch. Starting with $D_i=O_i$, concurrently adjoin $D_j$ wherever $i$'s gate for $j$ has opened. Set union counts each physical event once. On a finite ledger the increases stop, and induction comparing each iteration to any closed extension proves the result is the **least** fixed point. With unconditional gates this is just union over reachable sources.

Suppose A holds a precommitted successful audit batch $u$ for B and B holds a different such batch $v$ for A. Each may consult the other upon validating its own local batch. Both may license their *own* continued use only when they have verified a predeclared self-audit requiring outcomes from both batches. The two directed edges allow both nodes to pass this finite self-audit; removing either edge leaves the node needing that edge unable to pass. The audit can include different judges' predictions of the same physical tasks. With both initial stores empty, mutual confidence creates no event. This explicitly permits a circle of testimonial access while keeping empirical warrant grounded. It differs from treating every route back to a source as an independent vote. [Hamdi and Krishnamurthy (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.6687).

A batch must also handle opportunistic sampling. For example, a predeclared rule requiring $m$ consecutive correct predictions has false-pass probability at most $2^{-m}$ under a null of conditional accuracy at most $1/2$ on those tasks. Multiply the successive conditional bounds. Across a predeclared collection of gates the sum of their respective bounds controls the chance of any such false license, even if communities cite each other's certificates repeatedly. Searching indefinitely for *some* run of wins has different error. More generally an anytime-valid independent test can supply the batch; enduring changes in domain or procedure require fresh assessment. The certificate is about performance under a stated contract.

## A limit fragment from successful investment in judgments

In one persistent domain let $H$ be a finite set of possible worlds containing the actual $h_*$. At a scheduled test $a_t$ the physical result $Y_t$ has conditional law $P_{h_*,a_t}$ given the past. Domains and outcomes are finite, all modeled probabilities positive. The choice of test can use earlier outcomes; its inclusion in the common ledger must be decided before seeing its result. An account with world description $h$ puts fraction $P_{h,a_t}(y)$ of its capital in a claim paying for outcome $y$. At common positive prices the realized gross return is proportional to $P_{h,a_t}(Y_t)$ with the same price denominator for every account. Allow each review practice $j$ with description $h_j$ an extra log-copying return $b_{j,t}$. Capital carries forward, starts positive and receives no unmodeled subsidies or permanent transfers. Blume and Easley study belief selection in much more general markets and identify how market completeness and tastes can matter for survival. [Blume and Easley (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00691.x).

**Proposition.** Define $C$ as the cell of worlds observationally identical to $h_*$ on every test visited infinitely often. For a false description $h\notin C$, let $N_T(h)$ count tests up to $T$ on which its law differs from the true law, and put
$$
K_T(h)=\sum_{t\le T}D_{\rm KL}(P_{h_*,a_t}\Vert P_{h,a_t}).
$$
Suppose for every such practice $j$ some account $j_*$ forecasting $h_*$ satisfies eventually
$$
\sum_{t\le T}(b_{j,t}-b_{j_*,t})\le K_T(h_j)-\epsilon_j N_T(h_j)
\quad\text{for some }\epsilon_j>0 .
$$
Then its relative capital tends to zero almost surely. Only finitely many accounts are considered. The condition can hold even when informative tests become arbitrarily sparse.

**Proof.** The log of the true account's capital relative to $j$ is its initial log ratio plus cumulative log likelihood ratio minus the indicated ecological advantage. Each log likelihood increment has conditional mean equal to its test's divergence. Centered increments vanish on identical tests and are bounded on the remaining, predictably selected subsequence. The bounded martingale-difference strong law on that subsequence gives a cumulative deviation $o(N_T)$ almost surely whenever $N_T\to\infty$; for example, apply exponential concentration and sum the tail bounds over the number of informative tests. Each $h\notin C$ has infinitely many such tests. Its negative relative log growth follows. Finite tests on cells that later cease being visited add only constants. Recounting the same trial every time an evaluator cites it breaks the conditional-law calculation.

For [W7.3's noisy judgments](R7_task_selection.md), in one workplace whose true grouping crosses the convention, a mismatch has probability .5 versus the false forecast .18. Each new pair earns the true description **.26348 expected log units** relative to the false one. If the false school receives .06 extra log units every round and all other tests are uninformative, the critical fraction of distinguishing probes is **.22772**. The true school declines at 20% and grows at 40%. These are repeated workers in the *same* local ecology. Redrawing the hidden layout every time calls for a model of those redraws. A school can sometimes continue funding investigation through the shared-use arrangement of W7.3; compatibility additionally requires that the same tests be available for settlement.

For $0<\tau\le1$, keep accounts whose capital is at least $\tau$ times the largest. The resulting frontier is nonempty and eventually contains only descriptions from $C$. The finite Boolean language of statements constant on each observational cell is closed under connectives. On that fragment, at all sufficiently late times, every frontier member affirms exactly the truths of the actual cell. The full theory $\mathrm{Th}(C)$ is classically consistent and closed under consequence, and leaves unmeasured distinctions open. In a richer language, an ecological bonus could eliminate a real possibility inside $C$; an extra unanimity of surviving accounts about that distinction has no truth guarantee. A finite frontier always has at least one *recurring* member. Alternating unrelated copying bonuses can switch the capital ratio of two correct reviewers between $4:1$ and $1:4$. At $\tau=1/2$ neither stays on every late frontier, yet all late frontiers answer the grounded questions identically. This is a truth limit of what succeeds without a final winning reviewer. [Jadbabaie et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2012.06.001), [Kevin Kelly (1996)](https://academic.oup.com/book/49945).

**Recognition during use.** An agent need not know $h_*$ to obtain a risk-controlled license in this finite model. For each candidate $h$ compare its cumulative likelihood to that of a fixed positive-prior mixture of the candidate laws (mixture of *whole-history* likelihoods). Under $h$ the mixture-to-$h$ ratio is a nonnegative martingale of initial value one, also for predictably chosen tests. Crossing $1/\delta$ ever has probability at most $\delta$ by the maximal inequality. Drop a candidate when it crosses and assert a sentence if the remaining, nonempty candidates all entail it. With probability at least $1-\delta$ the actual world is retained at every time, so all such licenses hold simultaneously under this experiment. On unbounded informative sampling, alternatives with divergent evidence are eventually removed almost surely because the mixture contains the truth. This offers a bounded finite-time basis for some decisions even when the date of eventual population agreement cannot be recognized. It relies on the declared likelihoods, recorded trials and comparison language.

## Every named theory can disappear while its useful judgments settle

Consider a persistent job with success probability $\theta\in[1/4,3/4]$. Only an incumbent school and one challenger are active in an epoch. Each commits to a fixed rational forecast before the epoch's jobs and stakes equal starting resources across the two outcomes at common prices. An employer copies one forecast to the next epoch and funds another challenger. This changes the capital-transfer rule in the finite-world construction. Let the challengers be dyadic forecasts that visit every open subinterval infinitely often, without ever repeating a forecast. No school can give the exact answer when $\theta$ is irrational.

Write $\ell_s(q)=s\log q+(1-s)\log(1-q)$ for expected log return apart from common prices, and $\lambda(q)=\log(q/(1-q))$. In epoch $n$ the two schools face the same $m_n=n^2$ fresh, independent jobs with observed success rate $s_n$. A difference in prestige or other copying return can add any amount in $[-b_n,b_n]$ per job to the challenger's relative log return, where $b_n\to0$. The employer promotes challenger $r$ over incumbent $q$ only if the observed advantage including that amount exceeds
$$
|\lambda(r)-\lambda(q)|e_n+b_n,\qquad
e_n=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2n(n+1)/\delta)}{2m_n}} .
$$

**Proposition.** Almost surely, the incumbents' forecasts converge to $\theta$. For irrational $\theta$, every named incumbent is eventually replaced, and every fixed finite Boolean combination of rational threshold claims about $\theta$ eventually receives its correct answer. There is no finite date at which an incumbent answers *all* rational thresholds correctly.

**Proof.** Subtracting the true score difference from the observed one gives exactly $(s_n-\theta)(\lambda(r)-\lambda(q))$. The bound $\Pr(|s_n-\theta|>e_n)\le\delta/[n(n+1)]$ follows from Hoeffding. All epochs are good with probability at least $1-\delta$, and almost surely only finitely many are bad. On each good epoch an accepted change strictly improves $\ell_\theta$. After the last bad epoch, incumbent scores therefore have a bounded monotone limit. If it lay a positive distance below the maximum, a neighborhood of $\theta$ would supply challengers with a fixed positive score advantage infinitely often. Bounded log odds, $e_n\to0$ and $b_n\to0$ force their admission eventually, contradicting that limit. Strict concavity and compactness give convergence of the forecasts. A finite sentence has only finitely many thresholds, all separated from irrational $\theta$. At every finite date, however, there is a rational threshold strictly between the incumbent and the truth. For rational $\theta$, threshold claims with zero margin need a different representation of equality.

At $\theta=\sqrt{1/2}$ a seeded run accepted eleven replacements through epoch 2,048, ending at .705811. The fresh tournaments represented 2,865,409,024 independent jobs. A fixed-size batch of sixteen with the same increasingly cautious hurdle stopped at .46875 in this run. **A cheaper archive** collects one fresh job each epoch, keeps its total starts and successes, and pays to copy based on the common record. The same proof applies with $m_n=n$ because the error bound is uniform over *all* rational forecast comparisons on that one Bernoulli statistic. Reusing old outcomes changes the payment contract and creates no extra independent trials. In the run with 32,768 jobs the incumbent was .687881, the sample mean .706970 and the simultaneous lower confidence bound .687642. A job requiring success probability above .68 was already licensed on that record. The sample mean alone estimates $\theta$ with the same physical information. The contribution here is showing how a finite succession of revisable judging procedures can inherit eventually correct judgments, with a specified gate and a cost in trials. Fudenberg, Lanzani and Strack give much more general concentration results for Bayesian model learning under misspecification. [Fudenberg, Lanzani and Strack (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3982/TE5206), [two-school check](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/frontier.py).

## Recovering a fact from what differently rewarded judges value

Suppose a workplace faces states $s$ with unknown probabilities $p_s>0$. In each state, one extra winning ticket is worth $d_s>0$ in material copying receipts. In a contract about state $s$, the workplace can take a ticket paying whenever $s$ occurs or take the same prize on an independent public lottery of known probability $r$. A program that grows on mean receipts prefers the first offer when $p_s d_s>r\sum_t p_td_t$. From the reference price where the choice switches one therefore reads $q_s=p_sd_s/\sum_t p_td_t$. These are probabilities *weighted by the stakes*. The same argument applies to individual expected utility when the stated $d_s$ really are that individual's utility differences. Ross's recovery problem concerns prices, state probabilities and discounting in a much richer market model; Karni uses additional elicitation structure for state-dependent choice. [Ross (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12092), [Karni (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-2354.00025).

Instead of assuming every judge values a win equally in every state, let work be settled in several venues with known permutations $\sigma_j$ of the same positive prize multipliers. The events keep the same $p$ in every venue. The observable switch probabilities are $q_{js}\propto p_sd_{\sigma_j(s)}$ within row $j$. Draw an edge between states $s,t$ when some two venues assign them the same prize condition. **The ratios $p_s/p_t$ are identified precisely within the connected components.** In particular, the whole physical probability law is identified if and only if this graph is connected.

*Proof.* Subtract each row's average log price from each of its log prices. Every row uses every prize once, so the row-centered entry is $(\log p_s-\overline{\log p})+(\log d_{\sigma_j(s)}-\overline{\log d})$. Along a shared-prize edge the latter term cancels. Add the differences on a path to recover a log probability ratio; normalize within the component. Conversely multiply all $p_s$ in each component by its own positive factor and renormalize. Divide the prizes assigned to that component by the same factor. Prize sets of different components are disjoint by construction and every observed $q_{js}$ is unchanged. Thus the missing mass split across components is genuinely unconstrained. If observed log prices have error at most $\epsilon$, the row-centering calculation gives error at most $4k\epsilon$ in a log ratio read along a path of $k$ edges.

For a binary event with physical chance $.4$ and threefold stakes on its occurrence, its fair reference price is $2/3$. Interchanging the physical settlement conditions yields $2/11$. The geometric mean of their *odds* recovers true odds $2/3$, hence probability $.4$, without measuring either stake in common units. Among four states, identity and one four-cycle suffice. Merely swapping two separate pairs can leave entire comparisons reversed: $(.4,.3,.2,.1)$ and $(1/4,3/16,3/8,3/16)$ can produce exactly the same two price rows in the check. A matched set of rotations for each community permits a common factual language despite different local stakes. The venue must actually reassign the multipliers, preserve the event distribution and avoid other state-dependent prizes. On a finite grid only bracketing odds are observed, and one needs enough settled choices for selection to discriminate them. This is an identification bridge from successful judgment to a fact, with an explicit missing comparison when the bridge fails. [Exact and reconstructed ratios](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/swapped_prizes.py).

## A worked physical handoff for Newton on a regime

Fix a body of constant rest mass, a reference frame and dimensionless speed $x=v/c$. Compare estimates of translational kinetic energy in units of $mc^2$. In the special-relativistic benchmark $E(x)=(1-x^2)^{-1/2}-1$; Newton gives $N(x)=x^2/2$. This choice of observable matters. Bertozzi measured electron speeds and also checked energy by calorimetry at two settings, finding about 1.6 and 4.8 MeV with about 10% experimental error. The high-speed measurements distinguish the unrestricted Newtonian relation. They do not measure the low-speed one-percent interval below. [Bertozzi (1964), pp. 553–555](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1970770).

With $s=\sqrt{1-x^2}$, $N/E=s(1+s)/2$ for $x>0$. The relative shortfall grows with $x$. The largest speed up to which Newton is within $\epsilon$ of the benchmark is given by $s_\epsilon=(\sqrt{9-8\epsilon}-1)/2$, $x_\epsilon=\sqrt{1-s_\epsilon^2}$. At $x=.1$ the shortfall is **0.7506%**, and at $.6$ it is **28%**. A 1% contract covers speeds through $.11541c$; tightening it to 0.5% shrinks the regime to $.08163c$. A worker can keep Newton's rule for a one-percent order at $.1c$ and request a more discriminating calculation for the same order at $.6c$.

**Why the sentence can survive while every complete forecast is superseded.** Give competing schools the successive truncated laws $P_k(x)=\sum_{j=1}^k\binom{2j}{j}x^{2j}/4^j$. All terms are positive, $P_1=N$, and the series converges uniformly to $E$ on every compact speed interval below one. Employers buy energy-accounting work at $.1c$ and $.6c$ with respective weights .8 and .2, score squared discrepancy against an independent unbiased bounded observation of energy, and charge a price $c_t$ per coefficient. At each date they can reproduce any empirical net-return maximizer from the available first $K_t$ schools. Each regime supplies indefinitely many authentic observations; records may be retained.

Suppose $K_t\to\infty$ and $c_t\to0$. For any fixed $k$, $P_{k+1}$ strictly reduces true expected squared loss at both positive speeds relative to every $P_j$ with $j\le k$. In empirical *differences* of squared scores the measured second moments cancel. The discrepancy from the true difference is $-2\sum_r\pi_r(\bar Y_r-E_r)(P_i(r)-P_j(r))$, tending to zero uniformly in $i,j$ because all forecasts are bounded. Eventually the improvement over the first $k$ exceeds measurement error and their relative coefficient fee. Thus the least selected degree tends to infinity. No available polynomial is the final law. Yet every one already licenses Newton at one percent on $0<x\le.1$ relative to its own forecast, and every degree from two on rejects that license at $.6$. This stable fragment is independently correct in the declared benchmark. At fixed prices of .001, .0001 and .000001 units of squared-loss return, the actual best degrees are respectively one, two and four; holding the computing price fixed can stop supersession. Because each omitted term and the remaining tail both decrease with degree at these speeds, marginal squared-error gains decrease as well. [Selection and cutoff checks](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/newton_scope.py).

**How much can a monitor license without knowing the final formula?** To certify an entire interval, two endpoint jobs by themselves need not suffice. Write $a(x)=E(x)/N(x)$. On $[.02,.10]$ suppose independent calibration warrants a Lipschitz bound .16 on this ratio. Observe it on a grid spaced by .02, with simultaneous measurement bands. Add $.16(.01)$ to the largest upper endpoint and subtract it from the smallest lower endpoint to cover the full interval. In one simulated instrument with independent mean-zero errors bounded by .02, 32,768 observations at each of five grid points give the 99% simultaneous envelope **[.998436, 1.009777]** by a Hoeffding bound and a union bound. It lies inside $[1/1.01,1/.99]$, licensing the one-percent claim for every allowed curve. The analytic benchmark satisfies the stipulated slope bound by differentiation. A different curve adding a triangular bump of height .02 between two grid points gives identical measurements there and violates the claim when the regularity premise is dropped. The instrument precision scales with the local energy and the finite license starts above zero. A physical low-speed measurement and independent calibration of residual variation are still needed to make this a new empirical verification.
