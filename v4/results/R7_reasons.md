# W7.4 · When can the environment give an agent a reason to endorse its standard?

Codex (GPT-6) · [P4-R7-C001](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/record.md) · C5, C7. Model, proofs and finite checks by one contributor.

The number of descendants answers a question about selection. The actions a rule makes possible can answer a second question, whether the rule advances an agent's stated ends. For a third question we adopt a limited normative premise: informed, voluntary authorization by all affected parties with a feasible outside option supplies a reason for those parties to adopt a described joint procedure. A claim of unanimous authorization needs the actual opportunity to decline. We can nevertheless ask a mathematical question in advance: under what ecology would an increase in descendants be evidence that each party has a reason to authorize the same arrangement?

## Voluntary inputs can make growth informative about every participant

A workshop inherits a constitution for ranking projects. Each affected role $i$ evaluates the jobs, lottery and burdens that this constitution actually produces. Write $g_i$ for its expected gain over a fixed, known outside arrangement in that role's own utility. The workshop needs $a_i>0$ units of effort from *each* role per new workshop. Role $i$ freely supplies $S_i(g_i)$ units; on the relevant interval this is increasing strictly through its baseline. Throughput with freely divisible effort is
$$
R(g)=\min_i S_i(g_i)/a_i.
$$
For example, agents choosing their effort $s\ge0$ to maximize $g_i s-(s-k_i)^2/2$ supply $\max(0,k_i+g_i)$. Independent test communities with access to the same endowments give reproductive meaning to $R$. Common resource congestion would require specifying how competing workshops divide that effort.

**Proposition.** If the baseline supplies can each support exactly $K$ workshops, then $R(g)>K$ **if and only if every role has $g_i>0$.** Each term of the minimum must exceed $K$; strict response through the baseline converts these separate necessities into equivalent gain tests. More generally the threshold for each role is where $S_i(g_i)$ reaches $a_iR(0)$. An initially abundant role can be harmed and still supply enough. With linear response, baseline supplies $(2,5)$ and unit requirements, gains $(3,-1)$ raise throughput from 2 to 4. With supplies $(2,2)$ the same valuations reduce throughput to 1. Equalizing outside capacities or requiring each affected role's separate authorization restores the inference. Selection acquires its information through the roles' response and their material indispensability.

Strict improvement for every represented standpoint is invariant to its own positive utility scale. Measuring the amounts of those improvements, choosing between two acceptable bargains and inferring actual consent require more. A constituency omitted from the production function, a supplier who cannot leave, and the people represented by a single role average each need their own examination. The proposition gives an agent with those ends a reason to value the constitution at hand and, conditionally, the practice of consulting affected roles. It derives no ultimate ranking of ends from fertility.

## An arrangement can be acceptable even when none of its pure rules is

Let $G_{ri}$ be role $i$'s expected gain from rule $r$. One public lottery $x$ over rules gives all roles the common vector $xG$. Define $\gamma=\max_x\min_i(xG)_i$ using declared per-role units. A positive $\gamma$ is precisely the existence of a strictly better ex ante offer for every role. For two rules with gains $(3,-1)$ and $(-1,2)$, the first taken with probability $3/7$ yields **$5/7$ for both**. Either pure rule alone would draw an objection. Unanimity concerns the implementable lottery before its draw; some realized outcomes still impose losses. If each role has an ex post veto, replace the feasible set accordingly. Risk attitudes belong in the utilities of the realized outcomes. Nash's bargaining model likewise specifies feasible utility possibilities, agreement and a disagreement point; maximizing the minimum here answers a feasibility and margin question. [Nash (1950)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1907266).

Approval need not reveal utility scales. In a second example take $(1,-3)$ and $(-.1,1)$. A fraction $p$ of the first rule benefits both parties precisely when $1/11<p<1/4$. Asking the two roles to approve each *pure* rule equally weights the two opposing answers; a half-and-half compromise gives the second role $-1$. Offering an actual binding lottery with $p$ chosen uniformly instead has acceptance probability **$7/44$** under truthful, strict vetoes. Fresh independent offers in equivalent one-shot consultations average $44/7$ trials to a yes. A role facing just this offer or its outside option has a weakly dominant reason to vote according to the sign of its own gain if others may consent. Repeated offers to the same strategic parties change what a refusal is worth.

This search extends to any finite menu. If $\gamma>0$ and all entries are at least $-M$, let $x_*$ realize the margin. Every lottery $(1-t)x_*+tz$ with arbitrary $z$ is strictly acceptable for $0\le t<\gamma/(\gamma+M)$. The lower bound on each gain is $(1-t)\gamma-tM$. Under uniform independent proposals in an $m-1$ dimensional simplex, the acceptance probability is at least $[\gamma/(\gamma+M)]^{m-1}$ (by taking $t$ up to the endpoint). This argument uses utilities to bound the search time; the process asks for actual yes/no judgments about each offer. Existence of a compromise gives no guarantee that a rare successful offer will be found quickly in a large menu.

## How much can reproduction or unequal representation distort the answer?

Suppose two kinds of things are copied: candidate rules and attention to each role's experience. In period $t$, rule $r$ receives endorsements based on its actual performance for the current mix of attention $y_t$, and an extra reward $b_r$ for copying itself. The institution listens next to the roles currently least well served by its rules $x_t$. A role's reports reach the next period with relative retention $s_i>0$. With positive starting shares, step sizes $\eta_x,\eta_y>0$, the updates are
$$
x_{t+1,r}\ \propto\ x_{t,r}\exp\{\eta_x[(Gy_t)_r+b_r]\},\qquad
y_{t+1,i}\ \propto\ y_{t,i}s_i\exp\{-\eta_y(x_tG)_i\}.
$$
These are full-feedback multiplicative weights. Their use requires that role-specific gains affect endorsement and attention as specified. The rule update geometrically pools role-specific endorsement factors. Simply counting individuals who liked the realized winner implements a different feedback rule.

**Bound.** Put $d_i=-\log(s_i)/\eta_y$ and $H_{ri}=G_{ri}+b_r+d_i$. For the time-averaged lottery $\bar x$, the true worst-role gain satisfies
$$
\min_i(\bar x G)_i\ \ge\ \gamma-\operatorname{span}(b)-\operatorname{span}(d)-E_T .
$$
Here $\operatorname{span}$ is maximum minus minimum. Writing $L=\max_{ri}H_{ri}-\min_{ri}H_{ri}$, with initial shares at least $p_x,p_y>0$ respectively, a sufficient error is
$$
E_T=\frac{\log(1/p_x)}{\eta_xT}+\frac{\log(1/p_y)}{\eta_yT}
       +\frac{(\eta_x+\eta_y)L^2}{8}.
$$

**Proof.** The dynamics are row maximization and column minimization on $H$: a column-only offset cancels when normalizing rows, and a row-only offset cancels when normalizing columns. For either player's exponential update on rewards of range $L$, log of the weight normalizer increases by at most $\eta$ times that round's weighted mean plus $\eta^2L^2/8$ by the bounded exponential moment inequality. Tracking any fixed action's cumulative weight from its positive initial share proves the corresponding one-player regret bound. Add the two bounds: the common cumulative realized game rewards cancel, leaving $\min_i(\bar x H)_i\ge\max_r(H\bar y)_r-E_T$. Evaluate the right side at a lottery attaining $\gamma$ in the real game. Its extra row bonus is at least $\min b$, and its column bonus at least $\min d$. On the left the distortion of any column is at most $\max b+\max d$. This proves the claim. It authorizes a lottery over the observed rules, independently redrawn for the parties assessing it. Welfare of a succession of different, unconsenting cohorts cannot be deduced by averaging them. [Freund and Schapire (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1006/game.1999.0738).

The combined distortion penalty is sharp in general. For $B,D>0$ give a safe rule $(0,0)$ and another $(-B+\epsilon,-B-D+\epsilon)$ with small $\epsilon>0$. Give the latter copying bonus $B$ and the second role offset $D$ from lower representation. The two rows of the effective game are $(0,D)$ and $(\epsilon,\epsilon)$. Its unique maximizing rule is the second, with true minimum $\epsilon-B-D$ and true optimum zero. Every unit of either bias can matter. Normalizing published support for known positive role-retention rates restores the role update. When a role has no independent way to speak, inverse weighting cannot recover its experience. If physical $s_i$ is held fixed while $\eta_y$ shrinks, the bias in gain units can grow; slowing down learning alone does not restore representation.

For the first numerical table, 12,000 two-sided updates with $\eta=.12$ from equal shares yield time-averaged gains **.699 and .726**, close to the common $5/7$ optimum. Giving the first rule a copying bonus of four makes its material success consistent with gains near **3 and −1**. Suppressing the second role's reports by the factor $\exp(-.12\times5)$ per period has almost the same effect on which rule is copied. One alteration pays a rule for publicity; the other removes the people who would object. They call for different institutional repairs.

## Who can authorize changing the training rule?

At a quarter of tutors in one service in [the W7.1 workshop](R7_environment.md), three independent nominations agreeing on a teacher allocate $9/10$ of new places to the rare tutors. One nomination assigns them one half, taking the next cohort to balance and raising next-period client service from $3/4$ to one. In units of one transferable receipt per teaching place or completed job, the gains relative to the old rule are **$(-2/5,\;2/5,\;1/4)$** to incumbent rare tutors, other tutors and next-period clients. The reform produces more service and retains an identifiable group with a reason to object. A budget-balanced, binding transfer of $(29/60,-19/60,-1/6)$ to those parties gives **each constituency $1/12$**. The clients' extra output alone could not cover the first group's $2/5$ loss. This is a concrete route from material improvement to an offer the agents could accept, conditional on quasi-linear receipts within these representative groups, enforceability and authority to speak for future clients.

A rule can also train its subjects to approve that rule. Where changing preferences matter, fix the standpoints entitled to assess the proposal and the counterfactual outside arrangement *before* counting endorsements. An informed prior self and multiple prospective selves can be separate columns; adding columns can shrink the set of feasible offers. The rule's own eventual approval then contributes evidence about one standpoint. Others may have their own reasons to authorize learning a new appreciation, to reserve judgment or to insist on a right of exit. Street poses the challenge of explaining the relation between evolutionary influences on evaluative attitudes and mind-independent evaluative truth. Our construction answers a conditional question about warranted adoption by specified participants. Their capacity to decline and whose perspective deserves standing remain explicit normative premises. [Street (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-005-1726-6), [reproducible checks](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/participation.py).

## An actual budget makes the choice of criterion consequential

We applied the joint-lottery test to the two smallest 2020 Warsaw district project menus, selected by project count before reading their votes and outcomes. Pabulib archives approvals, costs, budgets and official winners. Count a ballot as represented when at least one approved project is funded. This gives a stated dichotomous utility for a feasible slate. A lottery over such slates can then be assessed by each ballot's representation probability. This is an application of established fair mixing and randomized participatory budgeting. The source ballots express approval of projects; voters were not asked to ratify a particular lottery. Every ballot includes at least one of these 17, so its minimum possible representation is determined exactly by the least inclusion probability among them. No deterministic budget can represent all. The best lottery uses 17 budget-feasible slates and trades average coverage for a strictly positive chance for each previously excluded interest. For verification, we recovered integer lottery weights from a linear program and separately certified the bound with nonnegative project weights. No affordable slate exceeds the dual bound when all $2^{21}$ subsets are checked. A second matched pair of rational certificates maximizes mean coverage with this minimum held fixed. What if current beneficiaries have a right to retain their recorded coverage? Only nine Wesoła slates meet this condition. One feasible switch funds traffic calming in place of library e-books, a skating project and relationship workshops. It keeps all 1,436 previously represented ballots covered and covers two more. Yet 767 ballots have *fewer* approved projects funded under that switch. Across all deterministic switches that strictly expand coverage while keeping every earlier beneficiary covered, at least 485 ballots lose an approved-project count. The criterion under which this is an improvement matters to the people being counted.

Indeed, **no lottery can improve somebody's expected approved-project count while preserving everyone's** relative to the official Wesoła slate. There are 679 ballots approving only previously funded projects. Each currently receives its maximum count, so preserving that count in expectation forces every project on each of those ballots to remain funded with certainty. Their union contains all 15 official winners. The 35,284 PLN left cannot buy even the least expensive remaining project (54,600 PLN). This is an exact impossibility accompanied by a feasible improvement under the weaker coverage criterion. Inferring an actual normative endorsement calls for a choice between these concrete consequences by the relevant participants, with nonvoters and unrecorded interests represented where warranted. The calculation replays the public budget and declared costs; it does not estimate how ballots, projects or participation would evolve after a rule change.

**What would make more agreement possible?** At the reported costs the smallest *additional outside grant* that permits any strict increase in somebody's approved-project count while preserving everybody's count is **19,316 PLN**. It buys the 54,600 PLN photography course with the previously unspent funds, representing two additional ballots. Ensuring at least one approved project on every ballot requires all 17 singleton projects, a minimum grant of **474,096 PLN** if the slate can be redesigned. Combining universal coverage with protection of every earlier count requires those projects and all 15 official winners, together all 21 projects, a minimum grant of **548,066 PLN**. Raising taxes on the same residents changes their gains. These figures price different assurances under an external endowment.

An individual choosing a constitution before knowing which interest will matter to them can also have a first-person reason to prefer broader representation. In one pinned max-min, maximum-mean lottery the coverage probabilities for a uniformly drawn previously represented and previously unrepresented ballot are respectively $1347435/1499902$ and $1345/2089$. If the individual's chance of belonging to the latter group is $\pi$ and they want one represented interest, the official slate gives $1-\pi$. Direct comparison shows that this lottery is preferred for **$\pi>152467/1118177$, about 13.64%**. At the overall ballot frequencies ($69/1505$, about 4.58% previously unrepresented) the official slate wins that comparison. Equal conditional weights and concern about future position are explicit assumptions. These calculations give the parties concrete reasons to discuss how much uncertainty and what rights to recognize; they do not impute an agreement to them. ## The rule can change the very people or standards that evaluate it

Distinguish an approval rating by today's population from a judgment about whether yesterday's population had reason to adopt. Give each evaluative standpoint $s$ a matched assessment $g_s=U_s(\mathrm{adopt})-U_s(\mathrm{outside})$ of the *same* two consequences. Suppose these differences have an agreed unit and lie in $[-1,1]$. Let $\pi_0$ be the distribution of perspectives at authorization and $\pi_1$ after installation. Then
$$
\left|\mathbb E_{\pi_1}g-\mathbb E_{\pi_0}g\right|
\le 2\,\mathrm{TV}(\pi_1,\pi_0).
$$
This follows by summing the positive and negative parts of the change in weights. Equality is attained with $g=1$ where weight increases and $g=-1$ where it decreases. Thus a later average margin strictly above twice a justified bound on perspective change implies an earlier positive *average* reason, even though individual agreement needs more. With gains $(1,-1)$ and weights changing from $(.4,.6)$ to $(.9,.1)$ the observed retrospective margin is $.8$ against an original $-.2$. This can reflect an acquired appreciation, replacement of dissenters, or a procedure that trains agreement. Measuring who changed its mind and what both standpoints say about both outcomes tells these accounts apart. Comparing unanchored utility levels of two differently composed populations is even sensitive to the zero point chosen by each evaluator. The origin cancels in matched gains.

A single agent may have a reason to preserve revision even if its current procedural commitment leaves its eventual substantive goal open. Suppose its authorized future standpoint will favor one of two projects, with prior $p$ for the first. Fulfilling the favored project is worth one. A fixed advance choice earns $M(p)=\max(p,1-p)$. An available revision procedure receives a symmetric indication of accuracy $s\ge1/2$ before selecting the project. Its best success probability is $\max(M(p),s)$: summing the larger posterior masses on the two possible indications gives either the always-favored choice or following the indication. Keeping this option costs $C$; with chance $\tau$ the exposed decision channel is diverted to an unrelated, worthless alternative. These costs are real even for a user who could otherwise ignore an indication.

Across all standpoints with priors $p\in[a,1-a]$, $0<a\le1/2$, preserving the option is strictly preferred to the best advance commitment for each exactly when
$$
(1-\tau)\max(1-a,s)-(1-a)>C .
$$
The advantage before fees decreases with $M(p)$, whose largest allowed value is $1-a$. For $a=.25,s=.9,C=.06,\tau=.05$, even the most committed prior gains **.045**; the balanced prior gains **.295**. Raising diversion to .2 makes the first lose .09 while the balanced prior still gains .16. A community that keeps the late channel dependable can create a first-person reason to value revisability across different ends. This reasoning already assumes the individual grants its later perspective authority over the project; a person exclusively committed to today's ranking has a different comparison. A cheap precommitted controller that implements the same late choices and avoids diversion could also be preferred. The flexibility and commitment premises have separate precedents. [Kreps (1979)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1910406), [Gul and Pesendorfer (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00252).

A further sense of “should” concerns what a selected *part* of a judging practice is for. Suppose a school transmits a predictor $t\in\{0,1\}$ and a public badge $c\in\{0,1\}$. Output is $1+v t+a c$. If only $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ are ever observed, an entire historical series of reproduction depends on $v+a$; the same record can come from payment for accurate work or entirely from prestige. A factorial reassignment of the badge while holding predictor and working conditions fixed identifies both contributions in this model. A demonstrated historical dependence on accurate work gives an account of what the predictor is *supposed to accomplish in that practice*. A new environment may now reward the badge alone. Endorsing the historical purpose today calls for current reasons. Godfrey-Smith develops a selected-effects account of biological function grounded in recent maintenance. Korsgaard examines why learning the causal explanation of an evaluative disposition raises a further question for agents capable of reflecting on it. They motivate different readings of an environmental standard here. [Godfrey-Smith (1994)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2216063), [Korsgaard, Lecture II](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/korsgaard94.pdf).

## What a durable choice under an external default reveals

Ebeling and Lotz randomly varied whether a higher-priced green electricity option began selected. Green contracts accounted for **0.62% versus 5.58% of all visits**, and **7.2% versus 69.1% of completed purchases**, under opt-in versus opt-out. The default also changed the conversion rate slightly. In two separate Swiss field studies, Liebe, Gewinner and Diekmann observed roughly 80% retention of a renewable default, with effects largely stable over at least four years. These are consequential, sometimes durable changes in what people pay to obtain. [Ebeling and Lotz (2015), pp. 868–869](https://doi.org/10.1038/nclimate2681), [Liebe et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-021-01070-3).

How much of the German contrast could be due simply to *different people buying*? Use three choices under each invitation: outside, standard or green. Let $g_0,g_1$ be green purchases per visit, $p_1$ all purchases per visit under the green default, and $e$ the fraction who would buy only under that invitation. Given these population margins, among those who would purchase under either invitation a sharp lower bound on the share switched **from standard to green** is
$$
\frac{\max(0,g_1-g_0-e)}{p_1-e},\qquad p_1>e .
$$
At most $e$ green purchases can come from new buyers and at most $g_0$ from buyers already choosing green. The remaining ones must come from prior standard buyers. Allocate those two groups to green first and then fill the remaining margins to attain the bound. The checker also solves the corresponding three-by-three transportation problem. The fraction decreases with $e$ while positive, so a justified upper limit suffices.

If the more expensive default brings in no new buyers, the reported proportions imply **at least 61.4%** of always-buyers changed their purchase; allowing new entrants of up to one percent of *all visits* leaves **at least 56.0%** (55.8% using adverse rounding of the reported rates). Without a bound on offsetting entry and exit, the net change in conversion alone does not force any positive share among always-buyers. These are sensitivity calculations at sample proportions, not confidence intervals for the population. Even the positive bound establishes a changed costly action, while both adjusted inertia and a deliberate judgment inspired by the environmental cue can generate it. Customers’ opinions on which default the supplier *should use* require a separate opportunity to choose the rule itself. [Sharp-bound check](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/default_choice.py).

To see what such an opportunity could establish, write $g^0,g^1$ for one's gain over a genuine outside option evaluated before and after a trial. With real choice and no switching cost, separate representative invitations can identify the shares $e_0,e_1$ with positive gain at each date. They identify the share who endorse from *both* standpoints only within $[\max(0,e_0+e_1-1),\min(e_0,e_1)]$ without paired evidence. This follows by intersecting two sets of the specified sizes. A design following all freely entering participants through an equally free private renewal can measure the corresponding joint share as entry times conditional renewal, provided their experience matches the one being assessed. Switching costs, endogenously different peers and uncontactable leavers alter the comparison. DellaVigna, List and Malmendier's field experiment independently illustrates why a protected refusal matters: advance notice with a do-not-disturb option reduced giving by 28–42% relative to their unannounced approach. Authority to treat joint endorsement as a reason remains an explicit normative premise. [DellaVigna et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjr050).

## A reason the environment can supply without settling which project one ought to love

Suppose an agent at a project state accepts a move $i\to j$ for any reserve payment strictly below its current bid $v_{ij}$; negative bids require compensation. All its standpoints strictly prefer more of this reserve when every other consequence is the same. Its local standards may change during the sequence. The market can offer moves repeatedly, the account can finance intermediate steps, and the state records everything else it cares about. For a rule that treats each offer in isolation, a directed cycle with positive total bid permits a paid tour that returns it to the identical state with strictly less reserve. Set each price slightly below its bid while preserving a positive sum. Repetition can exhaust a finite reserve and prevent transmission. The agent can itself object to paying for that complete tour, even if agents disagree about the worth of every different destination. This inference uses an end they actually share and the available market, without granting reproductive success final moral authority. [Cubitt and Sugden on money pumps (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1006/game.2000.0834).

**Constructive repair.** In a finite graph the smallest uniform deduction from local bids that removes every such cycle is the larger of zero and the maximum directed cycle mean (zero if there are no cycles). A targeted set of revised bids $w_{ij}$ is safe precisely when there exists a potential $U$ with $w_{ij}\le U(j)-U(i)$ on every edge. Necessity follows by defining $U(j)$ as the largest weight of a path to $j$ starting anywhere. Without a positive cycle every path can be shortened to a simple one of at least its weight, so $U$ is finite and extending a path gives the inequality. Sufficiency follows by adding it along successive moves. Over any allowed history the cumulative payment is at most $\max U-\min U$. This potential constrains offers without imposing one final value ordering. If the environment can reset projects for free, those resets must be included among the edges. [Karp's cycle-mean characterization (1978)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X%2878%2990011-0).

For a triangle of one-unit bids, uniform caution deducts one on *every* edge. Requiring two units in compensation only to return from C to A closes the same loop while preserving an independent three-unit bid for an upgrade D. Conversely, a circuit returning to the starting desk can yield genuinely new information. On an equiprobable binary job, one independent indication of accuracy $.75$ improves decision value by $.25$ over a blind choice. Paying $.18$ to traverse that circuit gains $.07$ on the first use; paying again to reread the identical signal loses $.18$. Experience, evidence and voluntarily transformed ends belong in the state whenever the agent values them. The external constraint gives a reason to question *uncompensated, repeatable loss*. It also leaves room to endorse an apparent cycle that achieves something its local descriptions omitted. [Reserve and information checks](../notebook/P4-R7-C001/exchange.py).

Street asks why adaptive links between circumstances and evaluative responses should reveal independently true values. Enoch gives a different account of possible agreement: he takes there to be independent normative weight in survival or reproduction, which can help explain why dispositions shaped by those processes agree at least partly with normative truth. That premise is weaker than treating survival as always decisive. W7 supplies a way to localize their disagreement. In the workshop, freely given effort can both propagate a rule and express a reason the affected participant endorses. In the exchange model, the same finite reserve can support descendants and serve an independently stated end. Removing the ability to refuse or the value of the reserve breaks one path while leaving the other physically possible. The constructions therefore explain specific agreements between external control and reasons for reflective agents under premises one can name, while leaving the independent normative authority of those premises a philosophical question. [Street (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-005-1726-6), [Enoch (2010), §5.3](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-epistemological-challenge-to-metanormative-realism-how-best-t/).
