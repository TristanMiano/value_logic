VALUE LOGIC: HOW TO RELY ON MODELS WE EXPECT TO REPLACE

Tristan Miano

Newton’s laws are false, in one familiar sense of the word. They are not universally correct. Relativity and quantum theory exposed limits that Newtonian mechanics cannot cross. Yet Newton’s laws are still taught, still used, and still extraordinarily valuable. For a bridge, a machine, a ball in flight, or a spacecraft maneuver far from relativistic extremes, the older theory may be accurate enough, cheaper to apply, easier to inspect, and better matched to the available measurements than a more fundamental successor.

This combination is the starting point of value logic. A model can lose its claim to unrestricted finality without losing its value on every restricted domain. We can recognize that a theory is fallible, expect that even our best current theories may eventually be superseded, and still have good reasons to rely on particular models for particular purposes today.

The physical evidence supports the restricted part of that story more strongly than the philosophical part. Standard treatments recover Newtonian momentum and force in the low-velocity limit, and the Standard Model is both extraordinarily well tested and known to be incomplete, among other reasons because it omits gravity. That does not prove that every present theory will have a successor. The expectation of continuing supersession is my motivation, not a theorem of the project.

The practical problem exists either way. A bounded agent has to act before it knows whether its current theory is final. It needs a way to say more than “this model is true” or “this model is false.” It needs to represent a narrower judgment: this versioned plan may presently be relied on for this domain and purpose, under these constraints, on the basis of this evidence, with this fallback if the evidence does not suffice.

I call that judgment a license.

PRAGMATIC VALUE IS NOT A NEW TRUTH VALUE

“Value” here means task-relative usefulness. It is not a proposal to define truth as utility, nor to measure metaphysical falsity with a loss function. A model can be useful because it predicts accurately enough, because it is fast, because it is robust, because its inputs are available, because its errors are tolerable for the decision at hand, or because it improves on the best available alternative. Those are operational properties of a use plan in a context.

This is why the compact expression “model M is adequate on domain D within tolerance ε” does not emerge fully formed from the succession story. M is forced on us because we are evaluating a model or plan. D is forced on us because the motivating phenomenon is restricted-domain retention. But ε only becomes meaningful after we say what loss matters, how losses are aggregated over the domain, what units are being used, and what the threshold is for.

The paper calls that aggregation rule a domain functional. A local loss assigns a loss to each case. The domain functional takes the whole case-by-case loss rule and returns the domain-level quantity that will be compared with a threshold. It might be expected loss when average performance matters, worst-case loss when every case must be protected, or tail risk when rare severe failures dominate. Calling it a functional simply means that its input is a function. Choosing it is substantive because it determines which pattern of error the license treats as important.

Sometimes the threshold comes from an external safety or precision requirement. Sometimes it comes from the status quo. Suppose the declared fallback B has expected loss .35 on the domain, and switching is required to improve on it by at least .05. Then the fallback-relative ceiling is .30:

εB(D) = J(B,D) − Δ

Here B names what happens if no candidate is selected, Δ is the required improvement, and J is the declared domain-level loss or cost. The equation explains one origin of ε. It also reveals why “better than the fallback” and “absolutely adequate” are different requirements. A terrible fallback can be easy to beat while the candidate remains unacceptable. Conversely, a candidate can satisfy an absolute error limit without improving enough on an unusually good fallback. A serious license can require both conditions, along with latency, cost, robustness, traceability, or other constraints.

The object receiving the license may also be larger than a single equation or neural network. It may be a finite plan: choose a frame, run a fitted model, translate its output, estimate a loss, route between experts, and produce an action. Two plans can have the same final prediction while differing in cost, robustness, provenance, or error propagation. The project therefore keeps three things separate throughout: the ordinary computational payload, a quantitative grade or bound, and the evidence that makes that grade usable. It also keeps the task loss separate from a model that estimates the loss, and both separate from the optimization loss used to train that estimator.

The formal request separates four roles. The evidence state says what finite records and provenance are currently available. The executable plan says what would actually run. The reliance context fixes the domain, task, loss, aggregation, thresholds, constraints, fallback, units, and evidence modes. The profile is the checklist of requirements that must be supported for this kind of reliance. Writing these as four fields is not a claim that four is the shortest possible encoding; fields can always be bundled. The point is to let evidence updates, plan replacement, contextual changes, and stronger or weaker authorization standards vary independently.

The argument that follows has several evidential layers. The calculus constructively represents restricted reliance, revision, composition, and local update under explicit assumptions. ReLU supplies a finite representability witness for its numerical interface. In the synthetic study, the structured statistic pipeline transferred much better to a changed tolerance but performed much worse at the current boundary and in regime. The policy–value bridge has a finite behavioral encoding and conditional reconstruction bounds; its practical semantic reconstruction remains prospective.

OPEN LICENSES, OVERLAP, AND REVISION

Consider a small running example. In ordinary English the request is: “May we use this plan on the declared domain, given the records accepted now, if its loss is at most .20, it improves on the fallback by at least .05, it finishes within 50 milliseconds, and its version and evidence chain are traceable?” An older plan and a broader successor are both candidates. The fallback has loss .35, so the improvement clause requires candidate loss at most .30. The request deliberately keeps that comparative ceiling separate from the stricter absolute ceiling of .20.

Suppose the accepted loss interval for the older plan is [.14, .18] and its accepted latency interval is [43, 47] milliseconds. Suppose the successor has loss [.11, .16] and latency [45, 49] milliseconds. Both plans meet all three numerical requirements; assume that their trace records are also accepted. Value logic does not force a unique winner. Their licensed domains may overlap, and a later selection policy may choose between them by cost, speed, robustness, or some other declared criterion. If neither is licensed on a case, the system uses the named fallback or another explicit information-gathering action.

The public outcomes distinguish four importantly different situations. If the request itself is malformed—for example, its units do not match or no required fallback is specified—the result is Undefined. If the request is meaningful but some required evidence is missing, expired, conflicted, or crosses a decision boundary, the result is Withheld. If accepted counterevidence establishes that a requirement is violated, the result is Refused. Only when the request is well formed and every required condition is supported is it Granted.

Supported, Open, and Refuted describe individual requirements. Granted, Withheld, and Refused summarize a complete well-formed request; Undefined is reserved for a malformed request.

These outcomes were chosen because they preserve four different next actions. Undefined suggests repairing the request. Withheld suggests gathering or renewing evidence. Refused suggests revising the plan or choosing another one. Granted admits the plan to later selection. Four is not metaphysically privileged or minimal for every caller: a use-or-fallback controller could collapse distinctions, while an auditor may require much finer diagnostics. It is the coarsest public interface used here that retains those four responses.

The distinction also makes revision intelligible. If the older plan’s latency certificate expires, the plan becomes Withheld rather than retroactively false. If later accepted loss evidence moves to [.23, .25], its adequacy condition becomes Refuted and the complete request becomes Refused. If the absolute threshold changes from .20 to .16, the old interval [.14, .18] crosses the new boundary and becomes Open, while the successor’s [.11, .16] remains Supported at equality. A later specialist may become preferred on one subregion without erasing the earlier plans from other regions or from the archive.

Several tempting global rules had to be refined, and each correction suggests a more useful replacement. These corrections bear on comparison, domain splitting, and retention policy. They leave the underlying motivation for scoped reliance intact.

A profile is simply a typed requirement checklist. A basic reliance profile asks whether the candidate is adequate, improves on its fallback, satisfies its constraints, and has a usable trace. A preferred-use profile can additionally ask whether an exact finite evaluated set contains no certified dominator. A dominator is a candidate that is no worse on every declared comparison coordinate and strictly better on at least one. Keeping reliance and comparative preference in different profiles lets a bounded search support an exact finite claim without posing as a proof of global optimality.

Comparative defeat is profile-relative for the same reason. Adding a superior model can defeat a preferred-use grant while leaving an ordinary reliance grant unchanged. Partial domination does not automatically carve a domain into clean child regions. A split is available only when the system can actually reconstruct the child domains, their evidence, samplers, fallbacks, and provenance. Otherwise the unresolved region remains withheld or goes to fallback.

The library retained in memory is not necessarily its Pareto frontier—the eligible set whose members are not dominated by another eligible plan. An archive can preserve dominated models for history, explanation, recovery, or use on another profile. Adequacy, comparative undefeatedness, frontier membership, archival retention, and current selection are distinct states. A Pareto-frontier characterization becomes valid only after the eligible finite library, resource coordinates, comparison completeness, and retention policy are all fixed appropriately.

Open-ended libraries also require attention to direction. A currently supported claim that no known model dominates this one can be overturned by adding a later dominator. But a comparison already refuted by an existing certified dominator may remain refuted as the library grows. “Open-ended comparison is never stable” was too broad. Positive non-domination and persistent refutation behave differently.

WHAT A RELU REPRESENTATION SEEMED TO PROMISE

The neural motivation was unusually concrete. A threshold gives a signed margin. ReLU turns the positive part of that margin into a nonnegative feature. Finite continuous piecewise-linear computations can be represented exactly by finite feed-forward ReLU networks under standard architectural conventions. If a network retains a numerical statistic rather than only the current three-way label, the same statistic may be useful when the tolerance changes.

This suggested a possible bridge between logic and computation. A network could propose losses, intervals, grades, or payloads. Exact external machinery could validate the evidence, apply inclusive boundary rules, preserve missingness and provenance, combine the required conditions, mask unlicensed candidates, and invoke fallback. Named positive-margin channels could then serve two roles: they could record room inside a supported boundary and provide numerical input to a later declared computation.

The exact result is representability. Learnability, architecture choice, alignment between activation regions and scientific domains, and hidden-unit semantics remain empirical questions. Many activation cells may refine one licensed region, or one activation cell may contain several simultaneously licensed models.

The architecture-neutral result is more basic. A consumer family is the set of downstream questions the representation must answer. A representation is sufficient when every declared consumer can recover the observation it needs. The coarsest exact code lets two states share a code exactly when no declared consumer needs to distinguish them. Four public outcomes are minimal only for a caller that truly needs to distinguish malformed, refuted, open, and supported requests. A Boolean grant consumer needs less. An audit consumer that needs witnesses, signed margins, validity, and provenance needs more. Counts over the realizable set of query states give a lower bound for a fixed-length discrete code; a neural-width bound additionally needs precision and robustness assumptions.

The formal results matter because they turn the opening intuition into a controlled practice rather than leaving it as an analogy. Separating evidence from the world preserves the fallibilist point: present warrant need not pretend to be final truth. The composition theorem says when certified components can support a composite plan through explicit interfaces, grade propagation, evidence, and checked-root conditions. The read-footprint theorem identifies exactly which later changes leave an earlier assessment intact. The routing and transfer results say what must be checked before restricted-domain usefulness may be carried into a selected subdomain or a successor plan. The fixed-point boundary marks where the finite calculus ends and a genuinely recurrent theory would have to begin.

The formal work also established boundaries that matter outside the neural implementation.

Operational support is not automatically a fact about the world. The same finite evidence state can be paired with worlds in which the target claim is true or false. A world-level conclusion requires a named evidence mode and a soundness bridge: explicit assumptions and validation connecting the finite record to a stated population or world claim. A license is a finite-stage warrant, not a truth oracle.

Component grants do not grant a composition. Two components can each have certified error .06 under individual .10 limits while their same-sign errors add to .12 at the root. A finite acyclic plan can be certified when its interfaces match and its constructors transform payloads, grades, and evidence correctly, but the root needs its own checked certificate or a valid propagation rule. Upstream error may be amplified by downstream sensitivity, so the correct global bound uses products of sensitivities along paths and must remain valid on the perturbation tube—the full region of intermediate values reachable under the certified upstream errors. Latency, peak memory, energy, caching, and contention also require their own typed aggregation rules rather than being thrown into one scalar sum.

Append-only history does not by itself preserve a grant. A new model can leave every old record intact while changing a comparison requirement. The useful persistence theorem is local: if an update writes outside the complete read footprint of the observed diagnostic, that diagnostic is preserved. A read footprint lists every record, collection, and index lookup that can affect the diagnostic, including a query whose present answer is “nothing found.” Otherwise a newly inserted record can create a “phantom” change. Absence of an impact path is sufficient for invariance; the reverse direction requires a realizable status-changing path for the exact update class and observable.

Fallback is not synonymous with safety. An empty active set prevents the system from using an unlicensed library plan, which is valuable, but the declared status quo can itself be harmful. Router risk therefore depends not only on how often routing or fallback happens, but on the severity of those outcomes. A low parent-domain average cannot simply be copied to every selected subdomain: loss can be concentrated on exactly the subset a router chooses. Routing accuracy alone is not a safety metric. Evaluation needs selected-scope loss, misroute frequency and severity, fallback mass and loss, and coverage.

Likewise, two models being close in their raw predictions does not automatically make them close in task risk or make a blend safe. A small prediction discrepancy transfers to a small task-loss discrepancy only through a common frame and an appropriate regularity condition, such as a certified Lipschitz bound: on the reached range, task loss changes by at most a fixed multiple of prediction change. A blended model is a new use plan and needs evaluation of its own.

Finally, genuine cycles are not licensed by pretending they are finite composition. If a grant changes the very evidence or loss used to determine that grant, equations can have no fixed point or several fixed points. A recurrent extension would need an explicit state space, operator, convergence or fixed-point rule, selection convention, and evidence-to-world bridge. The ordinary core stays finite and well founded.

THE CORRECTED ACTIVATION-TO-LICENSE LADDER

The canonical three-requirement example starts with ordinary sentences:

“The plan’s expected loss on this domain is at most .20.”

“The plan improves on the declared fallback by at least .05; because the fallback loss is .35, the plan’s loss is at most .30.”

“The plan finishes within 50 milliseconds.”

The paper calls one typed requirement an atom. Call these atoms A for absolute adequacy, I for improvement, and C for the latency constraint. Their numerical subprofile is P = A ∧ I ∧ C: the conjunction symbol ∧ means that all three must be Supported, rather than that one may compensate for another. The complete request also requires its trace record and well-formedness checks. These atoms expose three different sources of a reliance threshold—an absolute task limit, a comparison with the status quo, and a resource constraint—while keeping the arithmetic readable. They are a pedagogical choice, not a universal three-condition template.

For the candidate plan M, the accepted loss interval is [.14, .18] and the accepted latency interval is [43, 47] milliseconds. The scales convert unlike quantities into dimensionless units: (.20 − .18)/.01 = 2 for A, (.30 − .18)/.01 = 12 for I, and (50 − 47)/1 = 3 for C. The .01 loss scale and 1-millisecond latency scale are transparent pedagogical normalization choices, not universal constants.

For a smaller-is-better threshold, that arithmetic is the support slack—the threshold minus the accepted upper endpoint—followed by normalization and rectification, which replaces a negative normalized margin with zero. The surplus vector is therefore 2, 12, 3. All three numerical atoms are Supported and, assuming the request is well formed and its trace is accepted, the request is Granted. The same loss interval answers A and I under different thresholds. A compact implementation can reuse that numerical information while separately retaining the support and refutation directions, exact state, evidence validity, missingness, provenance, and the address: the typed identity binding a requirement to its scope, criterion, parameters, and evidence mode.

Now hold the adequacy threshold at .20. The interval [.14, .18] gives strict support and surplus 2. The interval [.17, .20] is also supported because equality is included, but its positive surplus is zero. The interval [.18, .22] crosses the threshold and is Open, also with zero surplus. The interval [.23, .25] is Refuted, again with zero support surplus, but with a distinct positive refutation margin. Missing evidence and expired or rejected evidence are Open for different diagnostic reasons; any stored numerical zero is only a masked placeholder and has no margin meaning.

This is why a negative support margin is not the same thing as refutation, and why a rectified zero is not a logical state. Boundary support, unresolved crossing, refutation, missing evidence, and invalid evidence can all collide at zero. Even a pair of positive support and positive refutation channels does not distinguish supported equality from an open or missing case unless the exact state travels beside it.

The interpretation therefore climbs a ladder.

At the first level, an arbitrary positive ReLU activation means only that its preactivation is positive.

At the second, a positive value from a named learned point or interval margin means predicted favorable slack. The predictor can be wrong, and the number is not yet evidence.

At the third, after an envelope—a validated uncertainty region—and its evidence mode have been accepted, a positive normalized value means strict certificate-relative surplus for one addressed condition, scope, version, and polarity, meaning the support-versus-refutation direction that the evidence is permitted to establish.

At the fourth, the full license still requires a well-formed request and exact support for every required condition. One positive adequacy feature cannot compensate for missing latency evidence, a failed improvement condition, invalid provenance, or a malformed request.

At the fifth, a later selector may use surplus only after restricting itself to the exactly licensed active set. For the vector 2, 12, 3, one illustrative declared ranking rule is max(0, .5 × 2 + .1 × 12 + .2 × 3 − 1) = 1.8. Relative to a unit of normalized improvement surplus, the coefficients give adequacy five times the weight and latency twice the weight, then charge a fixed entry cost of 1. They are transparent demonstration choices, not logical constants or fitted normative weights. The resulting 1.8 ranks an already licensed candidate; it cannot grant the candidate. If latency evidence disappears, the adequacy and improvement channels can still make a downstream score positive, just as a network bias or bypass can remain active when one ReLU is zero. Authorization and quarantine come from the exact state, profile, active mask, and fallback—not from the sign of one neuron.

This is the surviving dual-use result. A named, normalized, certificate-relative surplus can be both a meaningful grade and an input to a declared downstream computation. Arbitrary hidden units and arbitrary payloads do not inherit that interpretation. Positive rescaling of units also changes raw margins, so cross-channel use requires a registered normalization that rescales with the units or a downstream consumer that changes its coefficients inversely, leaving the score unchanged when units are rescaled.

WHAT THE FROZEN EXPERIMENT FOUND

The experiment compared two complete pipelines on a synthetic model-succession task. The structured arm predicted centers and radii for loss and latency, used held-out calibration data that were not used to fit the model to expand its proposed intervals, and then applied the exact evidence and license decoder. The comparison arm used a capacity-matched ReLU trunk with direct three-class cross-entropy heads for the meaningful atom states, followed by the same exact nonlearned operations. Both received the declared tolerance as an input. Neither received oracle intervals, latent world intercepts, oracle labels, outcomes, routes, or target-derived certificate information.

The preregistered aggregate hypothesis required the structured arm to win by at least .05 on changed-tolerance transfer and by at least .05 on a boundary panel while remaining within .02 on ordinary in-regime fidelity. The result was mixed because it contained decisive effects in opposite directions, not because the study lacked power.

Changed-tolerance transfer was the clear positive result. Without retraining, structured fidelity was .9436 and direct cross-entropy fidelity was .7570, for a paired difference of +.1866 and a 95 percent paired interval from .1860 to .1873. The scorer saw the new tolerance and was evaluated again for the changed query. Its demonstrated scope is changed-tolerance generalization against this frozen direct classifier; literal reuse of one invariant region and comparisons with other tolerance-conditioned classifiers remain untested.

The other two registered components went the opposite way. On boundary-state fidelity, the structured arm scored .5196 and direct cross-entropy scored .7808, a difference of −.2612 with interval [−.2636, −.2587]. This decisively missed the registered +.05 criterion and landed in the opposite direction. On ordinary in-regime fidelity, the structured arm scored .7764 and direct cross-entropy scored .8773, a difference of −.1009 with interval [−.1022, −.0997]. The registered .02 noninferiority guard was also missed. The direct classifier’s observed advantages are strong descriptive findings, but they were not preregistered as new confirmatory reverse-superiority hypotheses.

The structured proposals did pass their separately registered marginal target-in-proposal coverage checks. Loss coverage was .9098, with a 95 percent interval from .9085 to .9111 and a Holm-adjusted lower bound of .9085. Latency coverage was .9044, with interval [.9031, .9058] and lower bound .9033. Both exceeded the registered .88 margin.

That is a real calibration result at its exact scope: marginal containment of the target by the proposed interval in two frozen exchangeable target groups. Conditional, full-profile, selected-route, deployed-loss, safety, and target-world claims require separate evidence. Calibration of proposed intervals and useful licensing behavior are different achievements.

The adjacent use statistics make that difference impossible to miss. The declared target distribution contained .35 true Granted mass and .65 true non-Granted mass. Target-weighted fallback mass was .9962 for the structured pipeline and .9139 for direct cross-entropy. The corresponding target-weighted issued-Granted masses were .0038 and .0861, but issued-Granted mass may contain false grants and is not recall. Both pipelines often declined to decide; the tested structured pipeline fell back almost everywhere.

Under the unweighted design-distribution traces, the structured pipeline’s false-support and false-refutation rates were low, .0087 and .0146, while its missed-support and missed-refutation rates were large, .4611 and .3248. Accuracy conditional on the reference outcome being Granted was .0124 for the structured arm and .1811 for direct cross-entropy. Those .0124 and .1811 values are unweighted design-distribution conditional accuracies. They are not target-weighted recall, and the compact traces omitted the weights needed to decompose target-weighted fidelity or reconstruct target-weighted conditional Granted accuracy.

The exact active mask did its narrow job: inactive selection was zero in both arms. This establishes exclusion integrity; useful coverage and fallback risk are separate measurements.

One plausible explanation is conservative dead-band geometry. The structured intervals were wider on average than the oracle intervals, and the decoder maps an interval that crosses a threshold to Open. At inclusive support equality, any upper-endpoint overshoot also changes the state from Supported to Open. That mechanism is consistent with low false assertion, high miss rates, and near-total fallback.

That explanation is consistent with the theory and traces, but the experiment estimates only a complete-pipeline difference. Assigning cause among the training objective, center fit, radius fit, calibration expansion, threshold construction, decoder, generator, and their interactions requires a factorial design. Marginal target coverage does not imply case-by-case endpoint overshoot. The tolerance input also carries information about how the synthetic query was constructed, so the difference is not an identified “wrapper effect.”

There was also a design drift worth preserving. An earlier plan called for comparing boundary behavior at matched coverage, but the frozen primary endpoint compared raw macro fidelity while the two arms abstained at very different rates. The endpoint remains a valid comparison of the two frozen complete pipelines. It does not isolate representation quality at equal answer rates. A future study should freeze full risk–coverage curves or common answer-rate comparisons prospectively and retain target and design weights, evidence mode, polarity, diagnostics, route, fallback loss, and misroute severity.

The main empirical lesson is therefore asymmetric and useful. The pipeline that retained a numerical statistic transferred better when the rule changed: on changed-tolerance fidelity, the structured arm led the direct classifier by .1866. This supports the information-retention interpretation without isolating it as the cause. The same pipeline lost .2612 at the boundary and .1009 in regime, missed the noninferiority allowance by .0809, and sent .9962 of target-weighted mass to fallback. Those are large operational effects, not cosmetic misses.

The impact is nevertheless localized. It strikes the tested combination of center-and-radius outputs, marginal calibration, and exact conservative decoding, and it blocks a claim that this complete structured pipeline was operationally competitive on the frozen target population. The formal reliance calculus and finite representability results remain intact, as does the observed changed-tolerance advantage. The experiment did not factor the pipeline finely enough to identify one culprit.

That diagnosis suggests concrete alternatives: retain a statistic together with an explicit state head; use asymmetric or boundary-aware regions; train with losses that price misses and fallback as well as false assertions; tune decoder conservatism on a separate declared role; or compare systems on risk–coverage curves at common answer rates. A factorial study could vary objective, interval parameterization, calibration, and decoder separately. These are contingent choices that can be changed without abandoning the motivating idea.

Information preservation, marginal calibration, cautious withholding, and operational decision quality must therefore be measured separately.

THE OPTIONAL POLICY–VALUE BRIDGE

The project also began with a broader black-box motivation. Given a capable but opaque policy, perhaps an environment-relative value-like surrogate could provide a high-level semantic foothold: not a complete explanation of the policy, but a readable model of rankings, expected outcomes, counterfactual choices, domains of validity, and uncertainty. Value was the first semantic object I could see how to recover directly enough to motivate the project; beliefs, for example, did not yet offer an equally direct reconstruction route. The scope is fixed here: the target is an environment-relative surrogate whose practical fidelity and domain of validity can be assessed. Whether an arbitrary policy has a uniquely true utility function, and whether such a surrogate would recover it if one exists, are questions this project leaves open.

One formal existence result survives cleanly. For finite states and legal actions, a deterministic policy can be encoded as a one-hot action-score table: at each state, the chosen action receives 1 and every other legal action receives 0. With a fixed tie-specified decoder, the encoder and decoder are inverse on the encoder’s image. That is a genuine finite isomorphism or lossless correspondence once the objects and preserved structure are stated.

This finite existence result coexists with stricter limits on natural return-based reconstruction. A policy by itself does not determine the usual return-based value function, because standard value also depends on the environment, rewards or preferences, state or history representation, horizon or discount, perspective, terminal convention, and decision rule. A Q function assigns a return-based score to each action at a state; greedy decoding chooses the highest-scoring action. Applied to a policy’s Q function, that decoder reproduces the source policy only where the policy is already greedy under compatible conventions; elsewhere it may improve the policy rather than invert it. A scalar state value requires a transparent action-and-transition harness. Rollout reconstruction is therefore about environment-relative semantics, identification, and practical fidelity, while finite behavioral encoding remains an existence result.

Two counterexamples impose further design requirements. State visitation frequency is not utility in general: an agent may visit an unavoidable or absorbing bad state frequently, while a preferred terminal state may have low occupancy because arrival ends the episode. Occupancy remains available as a behavioral statistic, but a return interpretation needs supplied rewards or preferences, adequate support, and a declared environment and horizon. Likewise, a value representation need not be at least as complex as the policy under every accounting convention. If all actions have equal return, value can be constant while an arbitrarily complicated policy breaks ties. A constructive complexity comparison must therefore count the decoder and environment harness, fix the approximation tolerance, and assume enough action separation to recover the chosen action.

Likewise, an improving agent need not revise its terminal values. It can improve its world model, estimator, search, or policy while keeping its objective fixed. Value change is possible under some architectures; it is not forced by generic improvement.

The recursive-judgment result is narrower than “judgment recovers facts.” Let R be a pre-outcome report, Y a held-out outcome, and N the declared nuisance context—the background variables a baseline may use without R. The true N-conditioned Bayes predictor is the population-optimal probabilistic forecast based on N alone. If a predictor using R and N beats it in population log loss by δ nats, where a nat is a natural-log unit of information, then R contains at least δ nats of conditional information about Y. Against a merely convenient baseline, the baseline’s regret must be subtracted. Under mediation—all remaining report–outcome dependence passes through the latent task given N—the bound transfers to the outcome-identifiable task quotient, which groups task labels that induce the same outcome distribution.

The theorem establishes partial outcome information and, under mediation, partial information about outcome-identifiable task distinctions. Ontology, factual measurement, mechanism, human transparency, and deployment require additional identification and evidence. Omitted nuisance variables, non-Bayes baselines, direct outcome leakage, duplicated task labels, unstable outcome kernels, and recursive copying are failure modes that must be ruled out before making stronger claims. A later recursive report adds evidence only to the extent that it contributes information beyond all earlier reports; a copy can agree perfectly while adding none.

Behavioral fidelity, value fidelity, outcome or task-quotient information, domain validity and useful coverage, representational alignment, causal faithfulness, and human inspectability therefore remain separate evidence axes. Success on one is not an average score that silently upgrades the rest.

WHAT VALUE LOGIC ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES

The final architecture is deliberately hybrid. Learned modules may propose statistics, uncertainty regions, payloads, grades, or ranking utilities. Exact machinery keeps each requirement’s typed address—its scope, criterion, parameters, and evidence mode—along with units, profile, evidence identity, permitted support/refutation direction, validity, provenance, and fallback. The external decoder decides Supported, Open, or Refuted for each required condition, computes the public assessment, constructs the active mask, and permits selection only from the licensed set.

A finite ReLU network is one explicit reference witness when the requested numerical map is globally finite and continuous piecewise affine, or has an explicit extension of that kind. This is a representability theorem; width, efficiency, learnability, scientific-domain alignment, architectural choice, and mechanistic interpretation remain open. Another architecture is compatible if it preserves the same typed interface and exact authorization boundary.

The formal calculus establishes a way to make present reliance explicit without claiming final truth. It distinguishes current permission from permanent stability and from semantic finality. It gives constructive stability when dependencies are frozen or statistical intervals become and remain separated from a threshold under declared assumptions. It makes updates local through complete typed read footprints. It supports finite proof-carrying plan composition when payload, grades, and evidence are transformed correctly. It treats overlap, gaps, routing, and fallback as first-class objects rather than forcing every case into one winner.

The opening intuitions now have different evidential statuses. Restricted-domain usefulness, overlap between old and new models, and revisable present reliance have been given a constructive formal realization. Local persistence, finite composition, and representation sufficiency have conditional theorems whose hypotheses identify what must be built or checked. The structured statistic pipeline has strong changed-tolerance evidence consistent with numerical reuse. For current-threshold operation, the evidence instead shows substantially worse performance and near-total fallback in that frozen pipeline. The policy–value program has a finite lossless encoding result and a clarified reconstruction agenda, while its broader practical fidelity remains future work.

The negative results strike different layers. Countermodels to global closure, automatic composition, copied parent risk, and unqualified prediction-to-loss transfer constrain theorems and license rules; they replace broad shortcuts with explicit premises. The ReLU counterexamples constrain the proposed implementation interface; exact state and evidence must travel beside numerical margins. The pipeline losses constrain a contingent experimental design and are large enough to withhold an operational-success claim, while leaving the formal calculus and changed-tolerance finding in place. The policy–value counterexamples constrain identification and complexity claims; they motivate an explicit environment, reward or preference convention, decoder, support condition, and accounting rule. Properly baselined predictive gain establishes partial information, with stronger semantic conclusions requiring stronger mediation and identification assumptions.

The practical warning is that retained information and useful authorization are separate achievements.

That is the core idea of value logic. Supersession need not force unrestricted endorsement or total discard. An older model and its successor can both be licensed on an overlap. A gap can invoke an explicit fallback. Expiry can withhold without refuting. Counterevidence can refuse. A later specialist can become preferred without erasing the history that justified earlier use.

The aim is modest in one sense and ambitious in another. It provides a rigorous language for a decision we repeatedly have to make before deeper questions about final truth and model semantics are settled: what may we rely on now, for this purpose, on this evidence, with these limits, and what should happen when the answer changes?
