VALUE LOGIC: HOW TO RELY ON MODELS WE EXPECT TO REPLACE

Tristan Miano

Newton’s laws are false, in one familiar sense of the word. They are not universally correct. Relativity and quantum theory exposed limits that Newtonian mechanics cannot cross. Yet Newton’s laws are still taught, still used, and still extraordinarily valuable. For a bridge, a machine, a ball in flight, or a spacecraft maneuver far from relativistic extremes, the older theory may be accurate enough, cheaper to apply, easier to inspect, and better matched to the available measurements than a more fundamental successor.

This combination is the starting point of value logic. A model can lose its claim to unrestricted finality without losing its value on every restricted domain. We can recognize that a theory is fallible, expect that even our best current theories may eventually be superseded, and still have good reasons to rely on particular models for particular purposes today.

The physical evidence supports the restricted part of that story more strongly than the philosophical part. Standard treatments recover Newtonian momentum and force in the low-velocity limit, and the Standard Model is both extraordinarily well tested and known to be incomplete, among other reasons because it omits gravity. That does not prove that every present theory will have a successor. The expectation of continuing supersession is my motivation, not a theorem of the project.

The practical problem exists either way. A bounded agent has to act before it knows whether its current theory is final. It needs a way to say more than “this model is true” or “this model is false.” It needs to represent a narrower judgment: this versioned plan may presently be relied on for this domain and purpose, under these constraints, on the basis of this evidence, with this fallback if the evidence does not suffice.

I call that judgment a license.

PRAGMATIC VALUE IS NOT A NEW TRUTH VALUE

“Value” here means task-relative usefulness. It is not a proposal to define truth as utility, nor to measure metaphysical falsity with a loss function. A model can be useful because it predicts accurately enough, because it is fast, because it is robust, because its inputs are available, because its errors are tolerable for the decision at hand, or because it improves on the best available alternative. Those are operational properties of a use plan in a context.

This is why the compact expression “model M is adequate on domain D within tolerance ε” does not emerge fully formed from the succession story. M is forced on us because we are evaluating a model or plan. D is forced on us because the motivating phenomenon is restricted-domain retention. But ε only becomes meaningful after we say what loss matters, how losses are aggregated over the domain, what units are being used, and what the threshold is for.

Sometimes the threshold comes from an external safety or precision requirement. Sometimes it comes from the status quo. Suppose the declared fallback B has expected loss .35 on the domain, and switching is required to improve on it by at least .05. Then the fallback-relative ceiling is .30:

That equation explains one origin of ε. It also reveals why “better than the fallback” and “absolutely adequate” are different requirements. A terrible fallback can be easy to beat while the candidate remains unacceptable. Conversely, a candidate can satisfy an absolute error limit without improving enough on an unusually good fallback. A serious license can require both conditions, along with latency, cost, robustness, traceability, or other constraints.

The object receiving the license may also be larger than a single equation or neural network. It may be a finite plan: choose a frame, run a fitted model, translate its output, estimate a loss, route between experts, and produce an action. Two plans can have the same final prediction while differing in cost, robustness, provenance, or error propagation. The project therefore keeps three things separate throughout: the ordinary computational payload, a quantitative grade or bound, and the evidence that makes that grade usable. It also keeps the task loss separate from a model that estimates the loss, and both separate from the optimization loss used to train that estimator.

OPEN LICENSES, OVERLAP, AND REVISION

Consider a small running example. An older plan and a broader successor are both candidates for one domain. The declared fallback has loss .35, and the candidate must improve on it by .05, so improvement requires loss at most .30. There is also a stricter absolute adequacy requirement of loss at most .20 and a latency requirement of at most 50 milliseconds.

Suppose the accepted loss interval for the older plan is [.14, .18] and its accepted latency interval is [43, 47] milliseconds. Suppose the successor has loss [.11, .16] and latency [45, 49] milliseconds. Both plans meet all three requirements. Value logic does not force a unique winner. Their licensed domains may overlap, and a later selection policy may choose between them by cost, speed, robustness, or some other declared criterion. If neither is licensed on a case, the system uses the named fallback or another explicit information-gathering action.

The public outcomes distinguish four importantly different situations. If the request itself is malformed—for example, its units do not match or no required fallback is specified—the result is Undefined. If the request is meaningful but some required evidence is missing, expired, conflicted, or crosses a decision boundary, the result is Withheld. If accepted counterevidence establishes that a requirement is violated, the result is Refused. Only when the request is well formed and every required condition is supported is it Granted.

These outcomes are not merely four names for “yes” and “no.” They imply different next actions. Undefined suggests repairing the request. Withheld suggests gathering or renewing evidence. Refused suggests revising the plan or choosing another one. Granted authorizes consideration by the selector, not compulsory use.

The distinction also makes revision intelligible. If the older plan’s latency certificate expires, the plan becomes Withheld rather than retroactively false. If later accepted loss evidence moves to [.23, .25], its adequacy condition is Refused. If the absolute threshold changes from .20 to .16, the old interval [.14, .18] crosses the new boundary and becomes open, while the successor’s [.11, .16] remains supported at equality. A later specialist may become preferred on one subregion without erasing the earlier plans from other regions or from the archive.

Several tempting global rules had to be rejected to make this work.

A license does not always have to include a claim that no better model exists. Reliance and comparative preference are different profiles. A basic reliance profile can ask only whether the candidate is adequate and usable. A preferred-use profile can additionally require that no certified dominator exists in an exact finite evaluated set, or that every relevant pairwise comparison has been resolved. This finite, profile-indexed replacement is stronger and more honest than pretending that a bounded search proves global optimality.

Comparative defeat is profile-relative for the same reason. Adding a superior model can defeat a preferred-use grant while leaving an ordinary reliance grant unchanged. Partial domination does not automatically carve a domain into clean child regions. A split is available only when the system can actually reconstruct the child domains, their evidence, samplers, fallbacks, and provenance. Otherwise the unresolved region remains withheld or goes to fallback.

The library retained in memory is not necessarily its Pareto frontier. An archive can preserve dominated models for history, explanation, recovery, or use on another profile. Adequacy, comparative undefeatedness, frontier membership, archival retention, and current selection are distinct states. A Pareto-frontier characterization becomes valid only after the eligible finite library, resource coordinates, comparison completeness, and retention policy are all fixed appropriately.

Open-ended libraries also require attention to direction. A currently supported claim that no known model dominates this one can be overturned by adding a later dominator. But a comparison already refuted by an existing certified dominator may remain refuted as the library grows. “Open-ended comparison is never stable” was too broad. Positive non-domination and persistent refutation behave differently.

WHAT A RELU REPRESENTATION SEEMED TO PROMISE

The neural motivation was unusually concrete. A threshold gives a signed margin. ReLU turns the positive part of that margin into a nonnegative feature. Finite continuous piecewise-linear computations can be represented exactly by finite feed-forward ReLU networks under standard architectural conventions. If a network retains a numerical statistic rather than only the current three-way label, the same statistic may be useful when the tolerance changes.

This suggested a possible bridge between logic and computation. A network could propose losses, intervals, grades, or payloads. Exact external machinery could validate the evidence, apply inclusive boundary rules, preserve missingness and provenance, combine the required conditions, mask unlicensed candidates, and invoke fallback. Named positive-margin channels could then serve two roles: they could record room inside a supported boundary and provide numerical input to a later declared computation.

The proposal was never that every hidden activation literally means “adequate.” A ReLU network has its own activation regions, but those regions need not align with the scientific domains on which Newtonian, relativistic, or other models are useful. Many activation cells can refine one licensed region, or one activation cell can contain several simultaneously licensed models. Exact piecewise-linear representability is an existence result, not a theorem that gradient descent recovers the intended construction, not a proof that ReLU is uniquely suitable, and not evidence that hidden layers possess an intrinsic proposition-like semantics.

The architecture-neutral result is more basic. A representation is sufficient when every declared consumer can recover the observation it needs from that representation. The coarsest exact code depends on the consumer family. Four public outcomes are minimal only for a caller that truly needs to distinguish malformed, refuted, open, and supported requests. A Boolean grant consumer needs less. An audit consumer that needs witnesses, signed margins, validity, and provenance needs more. Counts over the realizable set of query states give a lower bound for a fixed-length discrete code; they do not give a neural-width lower bound without additional precision and robustness assumptions.

The formal work also established boundaries that matter outside the neural implementation.

Operational support is not automatically a fact about the world. The same finite evidence state can be paired with worlds in which the target claim is true or false. A world-level conclusion requires a named evidence mode and a soundness bridge with an explicit population, scope, procedure, and assumptions. A license is a finite-stage warrant, not a truth oracle.

Component grants do not grant a composition. Two components can each have certified error .06 under individual .10 limits while their same-sign errors add to .12 at the root. A finite acyclic plan can be certified when its interfaces match and its constructors transform payloads, grades, and evidence correctly, but the root needs its own checked certificate or a valid propagation rule. Upstream error may be amplified by downstream sensitivity, so the correct global bound uses products of sensitivities along paths and must remain valid on the perturbation tube actually reached. Latency, peak memory, energy, caching, and contention also require their own typed aggregation rules rather than being thrown into one scalar sum.

Append-only history does not by itself preserve a grant. A new model can leave every old record intact while changing a comparison requirement. The useful persistence theorem is local: if an update writes outside the complete read footprint of the observed diagnostic, that diagnostic is preserved. Those footprints must include negative collection reads such as the exact evaluated set or search index, or a newly inserted record can create a “phantom” change. Absence of an impact path is sufficient for invariance; the reverse direction requires a realizable status-changing path for the exact update class and observable.

Fallback is not synonymous with safety. An empty active set prevents the system from using an unlicensed library plan, which is valuable, but the declared status quo can itself be harmful. Router risk therefore depends not only on how often routing or fallback happens, but on the severity of those outcomes. A low parent-domain average cannot simply be copied to every selected subdomain: loss can be concentrated on exactly the subset a router chooses. Routing accuracy alone is not a safety metric. Evaluation needs selected-scope loss, misroute frequency and severity, fallback mass and loss, and coverage.

Likewise, two models being close in their raw predictions does not automatically make them close in task risk or make a blend safe. A small prediction discrepancy transfers to a small task-loss discrepancy only through a common frame and an appropriate regularity condition, such as a certified Lipschitz bound on the reached range. A blended model is a new use plan and needs evaluation of its own.

Finally, genuine cycles are not licensed by pretending they are finite composition. If a grant changes the very evidence or loss used to determine that grant, equations can have no fixed point or several fixed points. A recurrent extension would need an explicit state space, operator, convergence or fixed-point rule, selection convention, and evidence-to-world bridge. The ordinary core stays finite and well founded.

THE CORRECTED ACTIVATION-TO-LICENSE LADDER

The canonical three-requirement example makes the neural correction precise without requiring a table of every channel.

The candidate plan M must satisfy absolute adequacy A, meaning loss at most .20; improvement I, meaning loss at most the fallback-relative ceiling .30; and constraint C, meaning latency at most 50 milliseconds. The accepted loss interval is [.14, .18], the accepted latency interval is [43, 47] milliseconds, the registered loss scale is .01, and the latency scale is 1 millisecond.

For a smaller-is-better threshold, the support slack is the threshold minus the accepted upper endpoint. After normalization and rectification, the three positive support-surplus values are 2 for adequacy, 12 for improvement, and 3 for latency. The same accepted loss interval supplies the first two conditions under different thresholds. The system therefore does not need an independently learned status head for every logical atom. It does need enough separate information to retain the interval or center and radius, the support and refutation directions, the exact state, evidence validity, missingness, address, and provenance.

Now hold the adequacy threshold at .20. The interval [.14, .18] gives strict support and surplus 2. The interval [.17, .20] is also supported because equality is included, but its positive surplus is zero. The interval [.18, .22] crosses the threshold and is Open, also with zero surplus. The interval [.23, .25] is Refuted, again with zero support surplus, but with a distinct positive refutation margin. Missing evidence and expired or rejected evidence are Open for different diagnostic reasons; any stored numerical zero is only a masked placeholder and has no margin meaning.

This is why a negative support margin is not the same thing as refutation, and why a rectified zero is not a logical state. Boundary support, unresolved crossing, refutation, missing evidence, and invalid evidence can all collide at zero. Even a pair of positive support and positive refutation channels does not distinguish supported equality from an open or missing case unless the exact state travels beside it.

The interpretation therefore climbs a ladder.

At the first level, an arbitrary positive ReLU activation means only that its preactivation is positive.

At the second, a positive value from a named learned point or interval margin means predicted favorable slack. The predictor can be wrong, and the number is not yet evidence.

At the third, after a named envelope and evidence mode have been accepted, a positive normalized value means strict certificate-relative surplus for one addressed condition, scope, version, and polarity.

At the fourth, the full license still requires a well-formed request and exact support for every required condition. One positive adequacy feature cannot compensate for missing latency evidence, a failed improvement condition, invalid provenance, or a malformed request.

At the fifth, a later selector may use surplus only after restricting itself to the exactly licensed active set. For the vector 2, 12, 3, one illustrative declared ranking rule produces 1.8. That number ranks an already licensed candidate. It cannot grant the candidate. If latency evidence disappears, the adequacy and improvement channels can still make a downstream score positive, just as a network bias or bypass can remain active when one ReLU is zero. Authorization and quarantine come from the exact state, profile, active mask, and fallback—not from the sign of one neuron.

This is the surviving dual-use result. A named, normalized, certificate-relative surplus can be both a meaningful grade and an input to a declared downstream computation. Arbitrary hidden units and arbitrary payloads do not inherit that interpretation. Positive rescaling of units also changes raw margins, so cross-channel use requires a registered normalization that rescales with the units or a downstream consumer that explicitly transforms covariantly.

WHAT THE FROZEN EXPERIMENT FOUND

The experiment compared two complete pipelines on a synthetic model-succession task. The structured arm predicted centers and radii for loss and latency, used a disjoint calibration role to expand its proposed intervals, and then applied the exact evidence and license decoder. The comparison arm used a capacity-matched ReLU trunk with direct three-class cross-entropy heads for the meaningful atom states, followed by the same exact nonlearned operations. Both received the declared tolerance as an input. Neither received oracle intervals, latent world intercepts, oracle labels, outcomes, routes, or target-derived certificate information.

The preregistered aggregate hypothesis required the structured arm to win by at least .05 on changed-tolerance transfer and by at least .05 on a boundary panel while remaining within .02 on ordinary in-regime fidelity. The result was mixed because it contained decisive effects in opposite directions, not because the study lacked power.

Changed-tolerance transfer was the clear positive result. Without retraining, structured fidelity was .9436 and direct cross-entropy fidelity was .7570, for a paired difference of +.1866 and a 95 percent paired interval from .1860 to .1873. The scorer saw the new tolerance and was evaluated again for the changed query. This supports strong threshold-conditioned, no-retraining generalization by a statistic-output interface. It does not prove that one tolerance-invariant prediction region was literally reused, and it does not prove universal superiority over classifiers that also receive the tolerance.

The other two registered components went the opposite way. On boundary-state fidelity, the structured arm scored .5196 and direct cross-entropy scored .7808, a difference of −.2612 with interval [−.2636, −.2587]. The registered claim that the structured arm would be superior by .05 was refuted at its margin. On ordinary in-regime fidelity, the structured arm scored .7764 and direct cross-entropy scored .8773, a difference of −.1009 with interval [−.1022, −.0997]. The registered .02 noninferiority guard was also missed. The direct classifier’s observed advantages are strong descriptive findings, but they were not preregistered as new confirmatory reverse-superiority hypotheses.

The structured proposals did pass their separately registered marginal target-in-proposal coverage checks. Loss coverage was .9098, with a 95 percent interval from .9085 to .9111 and a Holm-adjusted lower bound of .9085. Latency coverage was .9044, with interval [.9031, .9058] and lower bound .9033. Both exceeded the registered .88 margin.

That is a real calibration result at its exact scope: marginal containment of the target by the proposed interval in two frozen exchangeable target groups. It is not conditional coverage, full-profile coverage, selected-route coverage, deployed-loss coverage, system safety, or truth about a target world. Calibration of proposed intervals and useful licensing behavior are different achievements.

The adjacent use statistics make that difference impossible to miss. The declared target distribution contained .35 true Granted mass and .65 true non-Granted mass. Target-weighted fallback mass was .9962 for the structured pipeline and .9139 for direct cross-entropy. The corresponding target-weighted issued-Granted masses were .0038 and .0861, but issued-Granted mass may contain false grants and is not recall. Both pipelines often declined to decide; the tested structured pipeline fell back almost everywhere.

Under the unweighted design-distribution traces, the structured pipeline’s false-support and false-refutation rates were low, .0087 and .0146, while its missed-support and missed-refutation rates were large, .4611 and .3248. Accuracy conditional on the reference outcome being Granted was .0124 for the structured arm and .1811 for direct cross-entropy. Those .0124 and .1811 values are unweighted design-distribution conditional accuracies. They are not target-weighted recall, and the compact traces omitted the weights needed to decompose target-weighted fidelity or reconstruct target-weighted conditional Granted accuracy.

The exact active mask did its narrow job: inactive selection was zero in both arms. That proves that an excluded plan was not reactivated by a high score. It does not prove that a useful plan was licensed or that fallback was safe.

One plausible explanation is conservative dead-band geometry. The structured intervals were wider on average than the oracle intervals, and the decoder maps an interval that crosses a threshold to Open. At inclusive support equality, any upper-endpoint overshoot also changes the state from Supported to Open. That mechanism is consistent with low false assertion, high miss rates, and near-total fallback.

But it is an explanation consistent with the theory and traces, not an identified causal decomposition. The completed experiment did not isolate how much came from the training objective, center fit, radius fit, calibration expansion, threshold construction, decoder, generator, or their interactions. Marginal target coverage does not imply case-by-case endpoint overshoot. The tolerance input also carries information about how the synthetic query was constructed. It would be incorrect to label the complete-pipeline difference as a measured “wrapper effect.”

There was also a design drift worth preserving. An earlier plan called for comparing boundary behavior at matched coverage, but the frozen primary endpoint compared raw macro fidelity while the two arms abstained at very different rates. The endpoint remains a valid comparison of the two frozen complete pipelines. It does not isolate representation quality at equal answer rates. A future study should freeze full risk–coverage curves or common answer-rate comparisons prospectively and retain target and design weights, evidence mode, polarity, diagnostics, route, fallback loss, and misroute severity.

The main empirical lesson is therefore asymmetric and useful. Remembering the number helped when the rule changed. The particular structured objective, calibration procedure, and conservative decoder did not turn that retained information into competitive current-threshold fidelity or useful answer coverage. Direct state classification had the opposite profile on those endpoints. Information preservation, marginal calibration, cautious withholding, and operational decision quality must be measured separately.

THE OPTIONAL POLICY–VALUE BRIDGE

The project also began with a broader black-box motivation. Given a capable but opaque policy, perhaps an environment-relative value-like surrogate could provide a high-level semantic foothold: not a complete explanation of the policy, but a readable model of rankings, expected outcomes, counterfactual choices, domains of validity, and uncertainty. This was the first semantic object I could see how to recover directly enough to motivate the project. It is an authorial research motivation, not a claim that value is metaphysically prior to belief or that every policy has a true utility waiting to be discovered.

One formal existence result survives cleanly. For finite actions, a deterministic policy can be encoded losslessly as a fixed score vector that assigns the chosen action its distinguished value, with a fixed tie-specified decoder. Restricted to the encoder’s image, the encoder and decoder are inverse. That is a genuine finite isomorphism or lossless correspondence once the objects and preserved structure are stated.

Failures of stronger constructions do not refute that existence result. A policy by itself does not determine the usual return-based value function, because standard value also depends on the environment, rewards or preferences, state or history representation, horizon or discount, perspective, terminal convention, and decision rule. Greedy decoding of a policy’s Q function reproduces the source policy only on the subset where that policy is already greedy under compatible conventions; otherwise it may improve the policy rather than invert it. A scalar state value requires a transparent action-and-transition harness. Rollout reconstruction is therefore about environment-relative semantics, identification, and practical fidelity, not about whether some finite policy/value-like encoding can exist.

Two other strong claims were removed. State visitation frequency is not utility in general: an agent may visit an unavoidable or absorbing bad state frequently, while a preferred terminal state may have low occupancy because arrival ends the episode. Occupancy can be a behavioral statistic under suitable support conditions, but reward must be supplied to turn it into expected return. And a value representation need not be at least as complex as the policy under every accounting convention. If all actions have equal return, value can be constant while an arbitrarily complicated policy breaks ties. Any serious complexity lower bound must fix what machinery is counted, the approximation tolerance, and the conditions that make the chosen action recoverable.

Likewise, an improving agent need not revise its terminal values. It can improve its world model, estimator, search, or policy while keeping its objective fixed. Value change is possible under some architectures; it is not forced by generic improvement.

The recursive-judgment result is narrower than “judgment recovers facts.” Let R be a pre-outcome report, Y a held-out outcome, and N the declared nuisance context. If a predictor using R and N beats the true N-conditioned Bayes predictor in population log loss by δ nats, then R contains at least δ nats of conditional information about Y. Against a merely convenient baseline, the baseline’s regret must be subtracted. Under an explicit mediation condition, the information bound transfers to the quotient of task labels that are distinguishable by their outcome distributions.

This establishes partial outcome information and, under mediation, partial information about outcome-identifiable task distinctions. It does not establish complete latent ontology recovery, factual measurement in general, mechanistic interpretability, human transparency, or a license to deploy the judging system. Omitted nuisance variables, non-Bayes baselines, direct outcome leakage, duplicated task labels, unstable outcome kernels, and recursive copying all defeat stronger readings. A later recursive report adds evidence only to the extent that it contributes information beyond all earlier reports; a copy can agree perfectly while adding none.

Behavioral fidelity, value fidelity, outcome or task-quotient information, domain validity and useful coverage, representational alignment, causal faithfulness, and human inspectability therefore remain separate evidence axes. Success on one is not an average score that silently upgrades the rest.

WHAT VALUE LOGIC ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES

The final architecture is deliberately hybrid. Learned modules may propose statistics, uncertainty regions, payloads, grades, or ranking utilities. Exact machinery keeps the request address, units, profile, evidence identity, polarity, validity, provenance, and fallback. The external decoder decides Supported, Open, or Refuted for each required condition, computes the public assessment, constructs the active mask, and permits selection only from the licensed set.

A finite ReLU network is one explicit reference witness when the requested numerical map is globally finite and continuous piecewise affine, or has an explicit extension of that kind. This establishes representability under stated hypotheses. It does not establish minimal width, efficient size, optimization success, scientific-domain alignment, architectural superiority, or mechanistic interpretability. Another architecture is compatible if it preserves the same typed interface and exact authorization boundary.

The formal calculus establishes a way to make present reliance explicit without claiming final truth. It distinguishes current permission from permanent stability and from semantic finality. It gives constructive stability when dependencies are frozen or statistical intervals become and remain separated from a threshold under declared assumptions. It makes updates local through complete typed read footprints. It supports finite proof-carrying plan composition when payload, grades, and evidence are transformed correctly. It treats overlap, gaps, routing, and fallback as first-class objects rather than forcing every case into one winner.

Its negative results are part of the contribution. A finite search is not global closure. A grant is not automatically a world fact. Component grants do not grant their composition. A parent average does not certify a selected subdomain. Routing and fallback need severity as well as frequency. Close predictions do not imply close task loss without a regularity bridge. ReLU positivity is not authorization, and ReLU zero is not quarantine. A policy/value encoding can exist without being a unique natural return representation. Properly baselined predictive gain proves partial information, not transparency.

The frozen experiment adds the most important practical warning. Retaining a reusable statistic substantially improved changed-tolerance transfer, but the tested conservative pipeline lost boundary and ordinary-case fidelity and almost always fell back. Marginal proposal calibration still passed. The result is neither “structured learning worked” nor “structured learning failed.” It shows that useful information can survive inside a representation without producing a useful complete decision system under the chosen objective and decoder.

That is the core idea of value logic. Supersession need not force unrestricted endorsement or total discard. An older model and its successor can both be licensed on an overlap. A gap can invoke an explicit fallback. Expiry can withhold without refuting. Counterevidence can refuse. A later specialist can become preferred without erasing the history that justified earlier use.

The aim is modest in one sense and ambitious in another. It does not decide final truth, prove that neural activations naturally mean what we hope, or recover true utility from arbitrary behavior. It does provide a rigorous language for a decision we repeatedly have to make before those deeper questions are settled: what may we rely on now, for this purpose, on this evidence, with these limits, and what should happen when the answer changes?
