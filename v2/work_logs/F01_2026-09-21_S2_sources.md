# F01 S2 — Targeted primary-source check

Accessed September 22, 2026 UTC (September 21 in America/Los_Angeles).
This is a narrow check prompted by the reconstruction, not the F03 foundations

## Sources and exact use

1. **Radu Mardare, Prakash Panangaden, Gordon Plotkin, “On the Axiomatizability
   of Quantitative Algebras” (2018), arXiv:1804.01682v1.**
   Primary HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/1804.01682v1 . Checked section 2.1,
   Table 1, and section 2.2's definition of quantitative algebra. The distance
   can be extended-real-valued; operation interpretations are required to be
   nonexpansive. This is an explicit assumption, not permission to ignore
   the amplification in E04. A future import must match the selected metric,
   operation, and typing. Only these definition-level facts are used here;
   the variety and quasivariety proofs were not audited.

2. **Matteo Mio, Ralph Sarkis, Valeria Vignudelli, “Universal Quantitative
   Algebra for Fuzzy Relations and Generalised Metric Spaces” (2024 journal
   version), arXiv:2304.14361v6.**
   Primary HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14361v6 . Checked introduction
   sections 1–1.1. The framework permits arbitrary operation functions and
   uses a relation with range [0,1]; equality and quantitative judgments are
   distinguished. Section 1.1 explicitly discusses specifying Lipschitz
   operations with constants greater than one. The bounded relation range
   does not by itself bound the elements of the carrier. This is a relevant
   F03 lead, not an adopted semantic foundation or imported theorem.

3. **“Quantitative Algebraic Reasoning” (LICS 2016).** The authors' University
   of Edinburgh research record confirms the separate earlier paper, DOI
   10.1145/2933575.2934518, pages 700–709:
   https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/quantitative-algebraic-reasoning/ .
   Metadata/abstract checked only. Do not conflate its title with the 2018 paper.

## Consequence for this task

The concrete F01 arguments remain self-contained. The source check prevents
an unqualified assumption that all quantitative-algebra frameworks require
nonexpansion or that a bounded numerical relation forces bounded value objects.
