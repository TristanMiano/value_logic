## RLL and the inherited finite-linear interface

Giorgio Bacci, Radu Mardare, Prakash Panangaden and Gordon Plotkin,
*Rational Lawvere Logic*, CSL 2026, LIPIcs 363, 3:1–3:21,
DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3.

Primary HTML:
https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol363-csl2026/html/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3.html

The introduction's distinction between linear inequalities, Farkas/Motzkin
methods and a richer arithmetic language was checked, together with the numerical
operator interface. S3's independently reconstructed finite nonempty-polyhedron
certificate is the actual starting point for this pass. Nothing here transfers
the whole RLL completeness or complexity result to an altered language. Proof
size, proof search, and checking an externally supplied certificate stay separate.
This is not a new reading of every proof or every page of the paper.

## Boundary lead: linear versus general conic certificates

Minghui Liu and Gabor Pataki, *Exact duals and short certificates of infeasibility
and weak infeasibility in conic linear programming*, arXiv:1507.00290.
Primary abstract: https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.00290

Only the abstract/metadata and its contrast between linear and general conic
programming were checked. It is a **lead**, not an imported theorem or independently
verified full paper. This pass's polyhedral alternative is not asserted for an
arbitrary nonlinear uncertainty set. The disk example has its own explicit
quadratic argument and does not rely on an unverified general conic theorem.

## Relationship to existing mathematics and project claims

Sparse dual witnesses, finite parametric linear pieces, basic feasible solutions,
set cover, shortest paths and support functions are established mathematical
patterns. The note reconstructs its exact instances and restrictions rather than
claiming to have discovered those patterns. The source/quantifier distinctions,
reflective-policy application, and neural-certificate characterization are
project-level candidates for further comparison, not verified literature gaps.
