## RLL and the linear-certificate antecedent

Giorgio Bacci, Radu Mardare, Prakash Panangaden and Gordon Plotkin, *Rational
Lawvere Logic*, CSL 2026, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3.
Primary HTML: <https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol363-csl2026/html/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3.html>.
Rechecked the introduction's Affine Lawvere comparison and Related Work.
It explicitly connects the affine calculus to Farkas/Motzkin linear-inequality
methods. The general arithmetic deduction system is a separate interface.
Our finite signed template certificate is a scoped application of that classical
linear-certificate mechanism, not a newly imported RLL proof-search theorem.
The proof of the real finite-polyhedron claim is reconstructed in section 5.

## Linear versus more general conic certificates

Igor Klep and Markus Schweighofer, *An exact duality theory for semidefinite
programming based on sums of squares*, arXiv:1207.1691v2, November 11, 2012;
Mathematics of Operations Research 38 (2013), 569–590.
Primary record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1691v2>.
Only the abstract and bibliographic record were inspected. Its contrast between
linear and semidefinite certificates supports treating the finite linear result
as a restricted baseline. None of its sums-of-squares theorems is imported or
reported as independently verified.
