https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol363-csl2026/html/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3/LIPIcs.CSL.2026.3.html

The arithmetic/residual and finite-theory interface remains the relevant
comparison. F06's signed difference, explicit empirical source versions and
proof-local update contracts are not automatically instances of the source's
whole completeness theorem. The row-violation residual and min/max arithmetic
are established kinds of mechanisms; this checkpoint implements specific
proof expansions and distinguishes what their current source interpretation
licenses. It does not claim invention of residual implication or linear
constraint relaxation. The already checked F03 locators remain authoritative;
no new PDF figure or whole-paper independent review is claimed.

https://docs.mosek.com/modeling-cookbook/linear.html

The primary documentation explicitly gives max/min inequality modeling,
finite Farkas alternatives and linear dual certificates. These are the correct

proofs rather than acting as an arbitrary linear-program solver. The forward
proof expansions are reconstructed in the project note. No conic, infinite-
dimensional, or unqualified attainment theorem is imported.

## Alternative supports and withdrawal

**Johan de Kleer, An assumption-based TMS. Artificial Intelligence 28(2),
127–162 (1986).** Publisher abstract/metadata:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0004370286900809
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(86)90080-9

The publisher's abstract identifies manipulation of assumption sets and
simultaneous potential solutions as central mechanisms. This is a substantive
antecedent for retaining alternative supports rather than treating one currently
chosen proof as the entire reasoning state. Only metadata and abstract were
checked; no detailed ATMS correctness theorem is imported. A late direct DOI
open returned HTTP 403; the indexed publisher abstract remained readable.

**Johan de Kleer, A General Labeling Algorithm for Assumption-Based Truth
Maintenance, AAAI 1988.** Official abstract:
https://new.aaai.org/Library/AAAI/1988/aaai88-034.php

The official abstract discusses the basic Horn-justification restriction and
its generalized labeling interface. It was read as a boundary/antecedent,
not used as proof that the project's signed support/bound antichain is complete
for arbitrary logical theories. A late direct open returned HTTP 502; the
indexed official abstract was available. No failed fetch is described as a
successful full-paper inspection.

**Kathryn Blackmond Laskey and Paul E. Lehner, Assumptions, beliefs and
probabilities. Artificial Intelligence 41(1), 65–77 (1989).** Additional lead:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0004370289900787

Its publisher abstract describes an equivalence between a belief-theoretic
inference network and assumption-based truth maintenance with probabilities on
assumptions. This is a useful warning against claiming that merely combining
symbolic supports and numerical uncertainty is novel. The full paper and its
hypotheses were not inspected, and no theorem from it is used here. The present
availability labels do not themselves calculate statistical confidence.

## What this pass actually contributes to the working project

The new artifact gives checked, finite transformations for source substitution,
proof-local repair, numerical assumption discharge and derived-rule expansion.
Its original-work status is a project implementation/reconstruction, not a
publication-priority conclusion. The loss-grounded opportunity is the explicit
connection between a particular inference's assumptions, their residual
violation magnitudes, and a useful cost-comparison conclusion—alongside the
counterexamples showing when dependence, source meanings or observations must
still be retained. F10 should revisit the closest literature once the eventual
rule set and claimed characterization are fixed.
