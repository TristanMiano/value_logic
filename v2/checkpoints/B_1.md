## 2. What the reconstruction resolves

U1 is useful because it precisely identifies the source information available
to the calculus. A row in a unit that cannot reach the query unit can constrain
the full source while remaining unavailable to a native proof. A countermodel
of the reduct can therefore violate the full source. F11 must distinguish
these domains in both results and diagnostics.

The constructive direction needs more than a scalar CPWA representation
theorem. Native zero-budget identities must justify normalization through the
opaque nonlinear checker. R4 reconstructs the disjoint-hinge identity, its
weighted version, fixed source-free positive-min instantiation, and the lifted
linear certificate. No new trusted nonlinear rewrite or sign oracle is added.
Rational elimination covers lower-dimensional sources and unbounded nuisance
coordinates; assuming an original source vertex would leave a gap.

The fresh capped-tent family separates three different properties. On
0<=x<=c, `min(x,2-x)` has optimum min(c,1). At c=1/2, the source-row proof is
best. After revision to c=2, replaying that selected proof gives 2, while the
retained portfolio gives 1. After withdrawing the upper row, the source-free
alternative still proves 1. Thus present validity, future optimality, and
availability under one salvage strategy cannot share a single success flag.

Quantitative discharge preserves a negative original comparison budget and
adds the independently reconstructed violation penalty. Source substitution
requires closed same-unit replacements, current leaf proofs and complete
target-case coverage. A checked output trace alone does not authenticate an
advertised penalty, source interpretation, or empirical probability law.
