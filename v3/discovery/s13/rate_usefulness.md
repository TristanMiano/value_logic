# What a channel-specific rate theorem would let a reasoner decide

Codex (GPT-6), DEVELOPMENT, October21,2026. Scientific interpretation of
I13-R's proposed useful mathematical delta, not a deployment demonstration.

The invoice theorem would replace an unrestricted search over partial private
programs by a nearly sharp resource certificate for one global property.
Let S be parity of the n hidden fair coordinates, let B bound expected
query-plus-reset expenditure, and let beta be invoice_rate.md's coefficient.
Every finite public transcript T has conditional-row separation

    q=TV(P(T|S=+1),P(T|S=-1)) <= min(1,B/(beta*n)).

For equal prior signs its best achievable correctness probability is
(1+q)/2. Its mutual information about S is at most q bits: the common-part
construction makes T a garbling of an independent parity-erasure experiment,
whose mutual information is exactly q. For a nonuniform sign prior, that
information bound becomes q*H(S), but the expenditure premise must still
use the uniform-source bill or the explicitly robust ambiguity contract.
These are ordinary channel consequences of the proposed new price bound.

For n100,H4,f1/2, beta*n=130. A budget13 therefore limits parity guessing
success to55% and mutual information to0.1 bits. Buying the constructed
complete decoder of expected fee about130.265 with probability13/130.265
achieves guessing success about54.9898%. This is an exact constructed
near-match, not an observed security experiment or a claim about all bits.

The same statement can be read from either side of a bounded-information
service. A user can rule out worthwhile acquisitions whose best possible
decision benefit is below their fee. A provider can bound leakage of the
stated global property through an exact billing-only interface. Neither
interpretation assumes that conventional probability is unable to represent
the channel. The conventional construction and the Value Logic description
refer to the same mechanism and tie.

The useful mathematical content lies in the extra *realizability restriction*:
the private encoder must purchase the bits needed to choose its bill. Ordinary
unequal-symbol coding usually starts with a message already known to the
encoder; ordinary bit-query complexity gives the answer directly to the
controller. Neither gives this coefficient merely by changing notation.
An exact reduction from existing work could still subsume the result, so the
closest-prior obligation remains substantive.

This is a candidate theory of a narrow observation channel. Small average
parity leakage does not protect other input properties or prevent rare
complete disclosure. It is not differential privacy, and it does not apply
when query identities, intermediate timings or private state persist outside
the stipulated channel. A measured hardware/application claim would need
those premises checked and every differing resource paid. The proposed
moderately novel mathematical contribution can be worthwhile without that
stronger claim, if its proof and prior-work delta survive verification.
