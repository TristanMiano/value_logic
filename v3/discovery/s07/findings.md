## 3. A proof of unavoidable ambiguity can reject an unhelpful immediate goal

The [ambiguity theorem](ambiguity_theory.md) combines compatible source
configurations, checked lower evidence at their convex mixture, and feasible
upper evidence at the configurations. If its lower bound on the best possible
fixed action's worst regret exceeds tolerance, no additional logical evidence
or witness alone can close the current unchanged cost set. Such work may still
help after later observations; the statement is about immediate closure.

The actual [trace probe](ambiguity_probe_results.json) identifies two of six
states where immediate closure is impossible, without blocking an observed
strict proposal improvement. It costs **72,364** units across the 25-state
proposal-before-audit trace. Its two useful detections cost more than the two
native proposals they could screen. These are read-only counterfactual screens,
not an executed skip policy with a changed subsequent state trajectory.

[Discrete regret arithmetic](discrete_ambiguity_results.json) strengthens the
bound to the exact answer on 25 opposing-cost constructed cases, yet detects
the same two states on the broad trace and costs **145,468** units there. The
stronger theorem has not made this screen economical. Its lattice argument
applies to pure returned repairs, not expected-cost randomized repair actions.

## 4. S29: prove which cost directions matter to the decision

The [affine-source construction](s29_affine.md) combines a paid scalar lower
with separately checked feasible-source geometry. If the source proves
Az equals b, displacements in the row space of A change every repair by the
same scalar. Repair rankings depend on a smaller cost quotient; replacement
still depends on the common scalar because its price does not shift.

The first [actual example](s29_affine_results.json) buys one complete proof,
then closes a zero-regret repair across two unresolved cost modes with no
audit. Generic scalar transport leaves regret two. The same admitted full
tree already closes, and ordinary source propagation is stronger on this tiny
case, so the result establishes a compact certificate rather than superiority.

The separately timestamped [signed geometry bridge](signed_geometry.md) finishes
the proposed derived-source route with **three actual checked negative proofs
and one audit**, costing **13,845 named units**. It certifies a zero-regret
repair while a mandatory cost remains either one or one hundred. The helper
completed this execution at 21:15:58 UTC after principal research closure;
it adds zero principal time and does not retroactively alter the final F01
assessment. All three proofs are charged, although the single final gate only
needs the mandatory-coordinate proof. Ordinary direct source propagation or a
direct original-coordinate proof may be cheaper; no comparative win is claimed.

The [quotient audit family](quotient_audits.md) turns that geometry into an
explicit complete observation plan. With n independent mandatory costs,
one decision bit, fixed replacement and exact admitted auxiliary repair
optimum, [the count planner](quotient_plan_findings.md) agrees with 135
independent literal audit-tree comparisons. The [closed form](quotient_closed_form.md)
then replaces all count states with two thresholds and one plan record while
preserving the quote in all 135 cases and nine larger cases. At n equals 128
in the middle example, 4,224 count states become one record; the observation

are not acquired by that arithmetic comparison.

The [inexact-anchor extension](inexact_anchor.md) derives a sufficient complete
audit plan from a checked witness/lower gap already within tolerance. Exact
auxiliary optimization is unnecessary in that case. If the logical gap exceeds
tolerance, even full mandatory-cost observation need not close repair; the
mixed plan must retain and pay for that unresolved logical branch. This last
extension is a derivation only.

[Random audit order](random_order.md) gives a source-uniform expected tariff
without a source prior. Its 546 exact small comparisons preserve the same
checked actions and realized cap. The larger savings are modest and all
extra implementation charges remain to be priced. An equally informed
ordinary threshold controller has the same opportunity.
