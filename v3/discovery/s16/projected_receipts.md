# Cross-target values as a partial response-vector receipt

Codex (GPT-6), October 23, 2026. DEVELOPMENT; same-model derivation.
This continues the small-information contract, with a new way to acquire a
second-moment bound. It does not claim a new robust least-squares principle.

Let S=(I+MM^T)^(-1), ||v||_2=1, s=v^T S v, and m=v^T S^2 v. Old scalar
value computations on a target subspace can give a lower bound on m even
when no old solve vector is retained. Suppose W has orthonormal columns,
v=W*c with ||c||=1, and the retained response Gram matrix is C=W^T S W.
Then

    s=c^T C c,       m>=||C*c||^2=:l,       s^2<=l<=s.       (1)

The inequality is orthogonal projection of S*v. For a nonorthogonal target
matrix V, retained h=V^T S v and G=V^T V give m>=h^T G^dagger h. The
receipt must warrant h in range G and the exact Gram/pseudoinverse contract.
Unknown/rounded matrices cannot be silently treated as exact.

For k old basis targets, k(k+1)/2 quadratic values determine C by polarization,
including cross terms. Their acquisition, storage and target geometry are
paid. Once C is retained, (1) applies to every subsequently chosen c on the
same deterministic receipt event. This differs from separately certifying
a norm of the old solve vector for each new target. It does not make the
old quadratic solves free, or imply that retaining C beats retaining S*W.

## Two explicit envelopes from a lower moment receipt

Let ||N-M||op<=e, e>=0. Write w=S*v and z=M^T*w. Then

    ||w||^2=m, ||z||^2=s-m, M*z=v-w.

Using z as a feasible new primal variable and w as a dual direction gives

    F(N;v) <= s+2e sqrt(m(s-m))+e^2(s-m),
    F(N;v) >= s^2/[s+2e sqrt(m(s-m))+e^2 m].              (2)

Here F(N;v)=min_x(||v-Nx||^2+||x||^2). Both formulas use only old
information and a separately proved edit warrant. They are valid bounds;
sharpness conditional on an arbitrary whole C is not asserted.

For e>0 put

    t_minus=(1-e/sqrt(e^2+4))/2,
    t_plus =(1+e/sqrt(e^2+4))/2,
    m_U=max(l,s*t_minus), m_L=max(l,s*t_plus).

The two maxima of the added terms in (2) over m in [l,s] occur at m_U
and m_L respectively. This follows by differentiating sqrt(t(1-t)),
or optimizing a linear function over its upper semicircle. Consequently

    U_proj=min(1, s+2e sqrt(m_U(s-m_U))+e^2(s-m_U)),
    L_proj=s^2/[s+2e sqrt(m_L(s-m_L))+e^2 m_L].           (3)

For e=0 use s exactly. The interval can be intersected with any inherited
one-value or richer valid envelope. This avoids claiming that the simple
primal upper rule dominates every existing rule on every parameter choice.
For partial receipts use certified enclosures or an optimization over the
joint feasible receipt set, never midpoint substitution.

## A strict decision from three old quadratic values

Take two orthonormal target vectors with old quadratic values1/10 and9/10,
and old value1/2 at their normalized sum v. Polarization gives

    C=diag(1/10,9/10), c=(1,1)/sqrt(2), s=1/2, l=41/100.

An example realizing these receipts is M=diag(3,1/3). The argument also
covers every larger-dimensional M compatible with the three receipts.
Let e=1/10. Since l/s=82/100 exceeds both t_minus and t_plus, (3) uses
m_U=m_L=l. The elementary bound sqrt(369)<193/10 gives

    F(N;v) <= 5009/10000 + sqrt(369)/500 < 1079/2000 < 27/50,
    F(N;v) >= 2500/(5041+20*sqrt(369)) > 2500/5427 > 23/50. (4)

Thus all compatible edits have revised value strictly between .46 and .54.
The one-scalar sharp upper at s=1/2,e=1/10 is100/181>.54. This inability
to settle .54 is substantive: the scalar pair M=1,N=9/10 has exactly the
same old value1/2 and allowed edit, and attains100/181. It violates the
additional cross-target receipt constraints. An identical ordinary receipt
implementation derives (4) too. No CPU, audit-fee or complete-cost gain is
measured by this information separation.

## Scalar bounds cannot simply be applied spectrally

An attractive but invalid shortcut replaces S by U_e(S), applying the
one-value scalar upper to each eigenvalue and asserting a Loewner bound
S_new<=U_e(S). The above S=diag(.1,.9) refutes this at first order.
For v=(1,1)/sqrt(2), s=.5 and m=.41. A unit rank-one edit decreasing M in
the old primal/dual directions has response derivative

    2*sqrt(m*(s-m))=sqrt(369)/50 > .32.

The derivative of v^T U_e(S) v at e=0+ is(.1+.54)/2=.32. Choose
M=diag(3,1/3) and H=-a*b^T with a=(1,9)/sqrt(82), b=(1,1)/sqrt(2).
Then ||H||=1 and N=M+e*H gives that larger derivative. For sufficiently
small positive e the spectral inequality therefore fails. Coordinatewise
upper bounds omit correlations generated by the edit. This is why the
projection-moment derivation, rather than spectral substitution, is needed.
