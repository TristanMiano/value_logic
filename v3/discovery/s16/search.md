## Pass 10: reject two tempting greedy value-of-computation rules

Task-specific model acquisition need not be submodular. Let the task be the
first coordinate, and let two unit-price models provide the exact rows
(1,1) and(0,1). Neither row alone identifies the task; together they do.
The function dim(span(acquired rows) intersect span((1,0))) therefore has
zero singleton gains and positive joint gain. A greedy immediate-gain rule
can buy a direct task measurement costing M instead, although the pair costs2.
The gap is unbounded as M grows. Full-rank coverage is submodular, but solving
for all coordinates can purchase irrelevant information on larger examples.

Joint proof refinement has the same elementary obstruction. For Boolean
unknowns y1,y2, initially maximize y1 over the whole cube. A proof of
y1<=y2 alone and a proof of y2=0 alone each leave the maximum1; together
they reduce it to0. If each proof costs1 and a direct proof of y1=0 costs M,
immediate-gain selection can again waste arbitrarily much. All certificates
are compatible with the true state(0,0); the issue is complementarity, not
unsoundness. Ordinary planning, code-circuit search and value-of-information
methods are the relevant controls. No new greedy approximation guarantee
or benchmark is warranted from the present structure.

A useful retained LP dual still quantifies a completed refinement: if an
upper bound max w^T y<=b^T rho is certified by rho>=0,A^T rho>=w, and a
new proof tightens b to b-Delta with Delta>=0, the same dual certifies a
decrease of rho^T Delta without resolving the optimization. The resulting
joint-proof wealth lower bound increases by exp(rho^T Delta). Predicting
whether the proof will complete remains a separate fallible forecast;
dual sensitivity does not supply its success probability. This is ordinary
weak duality applied to the proof interface, retained as a possible adapter
rather than claimed as a new value-learning algorithm.
