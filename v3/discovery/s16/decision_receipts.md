## A shared-edit certificate settles an otherwise unresolved choice

Let M=I_n,n>=2, ||N-M||_F<=1/10, and S_N=(I+N N^T)^-1. Two actions have
losses L1=(S_N)_11 and L2=(S_N)_22+2/25. The exact certificate in
joint_allocation.md establishes

    |(S_N)_11-(S_N)_22| < .070884160.

Consequently L1-L2<-.009115840 for every admitted N. Action1 is robustly
better; the actual edited matrix response need not be evaluated to choose it.
The signed allocation argument extends from two dimensions by giving every
other spectral direction objective weight0, hence no useful edit budget.

Each separate target has sharp interval[100/221,100/181]. Subtracting those
intervals leaves L1-L2 in[-4000/40001-2/25,4000/40001-2/25], whose upper
endpoint is positive. Thus those individually sharp answers fail to settle
the same choice. A conventional joint robust optimizer with the same source
and edit information obtains our answer too; the gain is from retaining
the shared edit constraint, not from excluding a conventional comparator.

The known0.08 action offset is part of the task, not tuned evidence of
real-world usefulness. Reading or certifying the edit budget and obtaining
the original source information still have costs. This example establishes
a capability under a stated contract, not an observed saved computation.

## A certified upper bound on the value of recomputation

For the ridge objective J_M(x)=||y-M^T x||^2+||x||^2, let x be a retained
approximate solution, j=J_M(x), and suppose the normal-equation residual
g_M=(I+M M^T)x-My satisfies ||g_M||<=beta. For any ||N-M||_op<=e,
the posterior_reuse.md residual argument gives

    J_N(x)-min_z J_N(z) <= (beta+e*sqrt(j))^2.      (1)

Here is a direct optimization proof without a probabilistic interpretation.
Set r=y-M^T x, Delta=N-M and S_N=(I+N N^T)^-1. The new residual is
g_N=g_M-Delta*r+N*Delta^T*x. Since

    ||S_N^(1/2)||_op<=1,
    ||S_N^(1/2)[I,-N]||_op=1,

the triangle inequality yields ||S_N^(1/2)g_N||<=beta+e*sqrt(||r||^2+||x||^2).
Completing the quadratic square gives J_N(x)-min J_N=g_N^T S_N g_N,
proving(1). For an exact old solution beta=0, the bound is e^2*r_M,
where r_M=min J_M. This is sharper in its use of old information than
charging the whole possible change in the optimum as a failure of reuse.

Suppose computation costs and objective losses have an explicitly declared
common utility unit, and buying a new solve costs at least c, regardless
of its outcome. Even a perfect solve cannot improve the combined loss if

    c >= (beta+e*sqrt(j))^2.

This is a robust sufficient rule to retain x. It does not require a belief
over possible edits or a forecast of the solver's runtime. If the inequality
fails, it says only that recomputation might be worthwhile; it does not
authorize buying it. A randomized solver, a changing task, warm-start costs,
or other information produced by solving can change that decision problem.

For an exact retained solution with r_M=100 and e=.01, the maximum objective
improvement is .01. A solve costing .02 in the declared common unit cannot
pay for itself on this objective. The units and tariff are illustrative,
not measured fees. The extra rule has content because the old residual
receipt lets us bound a computation's value before executing it.

## Relation to the overall contribution

These examples use established robust decision and value-of-computation
principles. The possible contribution is a specific, checkable receipt
that supplies their previously unavailable input, together with its sharp
information and shape boundaries. They address changing assumptions,
fallible numerical models and reasoning costs. They do not learn arbitrary
mathematical truth, and identical ordinary implementations tie. A useful
moderately novel receipt or synthesis remains a worthwhile target even
though the surrounding decision principle is conventional.
