## Relation to the original five questions

1. **Logical uncertainty:** incomplete proofs and optimization runs can supply
   truthful partial information. The current leading construction can act on
   such intervals before full optimization is resolved. It does not learn
   probabilities of arbitrary unsettled mathematical propositions.
2. **Logical counterfactuals:** source edits, proof dependencies and model
   replacement now have explicit finite semantics and transfer conditions.
   The sharp block theorem bounds all warranted edits of all sources compatible
   with retained information. Genuine counterpossibles remain outside its scope.
3. **Several useful, fallible models:** model proposals can guide queries or
   repairs, while independent mathematical warrants control reliance. Common
   information and conventional robust/correction methods often give an
   equivalence rather than strict superiority. N16 alone is not a multi-model
   advantage theorem.
4. **Learning usefulness and paying for reasoning:** partial evidence can improve
   the set of certified decisions; reuse bounds can limit the possible value of
   another solve. Better certificates do not automatically mean a better paid
   learning policy. That broader empirical and metareasoning duty is unresolved.
5. **Probability information in values/costs:** the invoice constructions expose
   exactly which interface can reveal a hidden parameter, and the numerical
   constructions expose which targets a value can determine. A spectral measure
   is not automatically an epistemic probability over mathematical claims.

Value Logic organizes these questions around what a bounded reasoner can use,
retain, pay for and revise. Conventional mathematical methods also address
those concerns. They are appropriate ingredients and controls. The contribution
test is a nonredundant useful mechanism or guarantee, not philosophical distance
from ordinary methods or universal superiority over a class containing itself.

## What the returns suggest changing

The repeated bottleneck is the relation between a mathematically valid bound
and the actual information/cost contract. Several seemingly promising gains
disappear when a stronger control gets the same source access. Future generation
should specify that contract early, including cheap returned vectors, known
rank, shared solves, public repairs and source/edit warrants. This is a search
heuristic from the observed failures, not a proven optimal allocation.

For a bounded mathematical contribution, a natural exact information problem
and a nonroutine analytic solution can be worthwhile without an application
benchmark. N16 now has a reconstructable candidate of that form. Its remaining
novelty/usefulness judgment must explicitly compare the analytic collapse with
the existing exact SDP. Adding unrelated extensions or more passing grids
would not settle that judgment.

For a service or cost contribution, choose a credible proof-producing or
expensive-truth workflow first. Reject it early if direct interpolation, a
returned full vector, or an ordinary lazy policy dominates under the actual
prices. The private-repair and partial-proof routes remain available for
this purpose. The numerical reuse guarantee is a useful component, but its
standard Kantorovich/duality ancestry makes a standalone novelty claim less
promising without a specific new decision duty.
