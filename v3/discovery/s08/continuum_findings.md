# Continuum policy certificates: capability gained, bill still substantial

**DEVELOPMENT — Codex (GPT-6), October 14, 2026 UTC.**

[cont01](cont01/results.json) checks 105 expected-fee polynomial identities
against 7,620 complete policy/world combinations, 5,775 exact restriction
identities/range checks, and four altered-certificate controls. It buys no
physical audits. The degree bound, independently checked policy values and
exact interpolation support the continuum identity; a sampled graph alone
would not do so.

[cs01](cs01/results.json) completes 46,080 exact-action outputs across nine
off-menu environments and five arms. Three preparation attempts terminated
in the unstable host/interpreters; their costs remain unknown. Completed
units and all failures were preserved, and remaining units finished in
separate Python processes. The author's explicit stochastic-retry authority
is recorded alongside the failures. Separate reconstruction checks every
durable output/receipt, all 180 policy polynomials, 1,728 region tables,
learning counts and decision epochs; see the nine *_reconstruction.json
records. Table totals here mean individual bins, not complete 64-bin banks.
Thirty-six changed-cover/endpoint/mask/fee controls reject. Reconstruction
buys zero new physical observations and earns no new empirical sample count.

The ordinary learner exactly matches the warranted learner. With 1,024
requests per arm, the learner's fee advantage over cached static robust
selection appears only in n6/rho=.2: 25,704 versus 27,035, at 627,295 versus
583,832 named units. The learner stops at request 248 after 128 pair samples.
In the remaining eight environments it buys the same realized fee as the
static control. It does certify a sufficiently good policy in four of those
environments, but this certificate does not itself reduce their purchases.
No empirical true-parameter exclusion is observed; this is not a coverage
calibration study.

The plug-in policy comparator is much cheaper to prepare because it omits
region certificates. It uses point estimates and has no corresponding
per-request interval fee claim. Its action certificates are nevertheless
exact. In these runs its cumulative conditional expected bank-fee regret is
small, including approximately 1.13 units in n16/rho=.2 against approximately
1,153.58 for the conservative static and interval controllers. This is a
decision rule comparison, not evidence that statistical guarantees are free
or that a point estimate warrants an interval claim. In n16/rho=.4 its
realized fee exceeds the static fee despite lower expected bank-fee regret;
expected and realized comparisons must remain distinct.

The fixed 64-bin representation retains the original physical source and
removes the old finite-menu membership assumption: the old menu now selects
policies only. Its preparation grows substantially with path size, and a
conservative Hoeffding interval can keep an expensive policy selected after
useful evidence has arrived. The current cost model records named operations
and end-to-end arm timings, with three unknown failed prefixes. Neither a
complete-cost gain nor P3-POS01 readiness follows.

The next cheap repair is a sharper **exact Bernoulli mixture confidence
interval** at the same sparse count epochs. It should retain the continuum
coverage claim, unchanged source and strong ordinary mirror, while making
the additional integer-power calculation explicit. If that cannot justify
its overhead, retain the result and return to a different mechanism instead
of endlessly optimizing this small source family.
