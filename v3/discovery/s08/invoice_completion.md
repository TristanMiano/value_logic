# Counterfactual invoice bounds from incomplete receipts

**DEVELOPMENT — Codex (GPT-6), October 14, 2026 UTC.**
Constructive response to [the adequacy countermodel](adequacy_countermodel.md).
The aim is a fee comparison that does not assume a Markov, independent or
otherwise known within-row source distribution. Fresh rows remain identically
distributed for the expected-fee learning statements below.

## An exact completion interval for a supplied policy

Fix an interval-root acquisition policy pi and a partial receipt assignment
z from the current original-source request. Let C_pi(x) be the physical fee
pi would pay **from scratch** in complete indicator world x. Previously paid
receipts may be reused to evaluate this hypothetical fee, but do not erase
queries from the definition of C_pi. Define

```math
L_\pi(z)=\min_{x\supseteq z}C_\pi(x),\qquad
U_\pi(z)=\max_{x\supseteq z}C_\pi(x).
```

For the path policies, both endpoints are computable by the same O(n^2)
ordinary/left-forced/right-forced interval states used for execution. Charge
the selected node's fee. If its indicator is known, take that continuation;
otherwise minimize or maximize over E and M. Ordinary E continuations split
into two ordinary subintervals; M continuations split into two forced ones.
Forced endpoint continuations recurse on the remaining interval. The child
variable sets are disjoint, so minima and maxima add exactly. Keep an attaining
indicator witness for each endpoint and an independent recurrence check.

For receipts generated by pi itself, the interval collapses to its realized
fee: every compatible completion follows the same query history. For another
policy, an interval width is a concrete missing-information quantity. It can
be nonzero even when the current repair action is already exact.

## Distribution-free partial identification and a necessary ordinary control

For any current-row source law, pointwise L_pi(z)<=C_pi(x)<=U_pi(z).
Under fresh stationary rows and any predictable logging policy, conditional
expectations therefore bracket the same unknown mean fee mu_pi. Bounded
martingale concentration on observed lower and upper endpoints gives valid
mean-fee intervals, even when logging policies adapt. The endpoint means
need not be constant across logging policies; the pointwise ordering is the
reason the concentration statement remains valid.

However, **the executed policy's own physical invoice is always observed**.
A conventional stochastic cost-bandit controller can learn the best fixed
bank policy directly, without fitting any latent source model or buying
special pilot audits. It needs the same admissible policy bank, exact action
checker and actual invoices, but not the Markov polynomial certificates.
This is a material additional comparator to the earlier ordinary mirrors.
The models' shared-parameter feedback may help, but its expensive preparation
and family assumptions cannot be compared only with static controls.

Do not replace a policy's own exact-invoice confidence interval by a pooled
partial-completion interval. Intersect the two valid statements. A simple
warning case has constant policy fees A=3/4 and B=1, with every unexecuted
policy's completion interval [0,1]. Optimism using only average lower
endpoints balances (3/4)N_A/T against N_B/T, spending about 3/7 of requests
on the inferior B. The valid but perpetually incomplete bounds can produce
linear regret. Exact on-policy samples immediately contain information that
must not be discarded by that pooling operation.

## A priced residual correction when partial identification is insufficient

After observing z, set m_pi=(L_pi+U_pi)/2. Choose a pilot indicator R with
known probability q(z)>0, before observing any additional values. On a pilot,
replay pi against the existing receipt cache, buying only missing nodes needed
to determine its from-scratch invoice C_pi. The estimator

```math
Y_\pi=m_\pi+\frac{R}{q(z)}[C_\pi-m_\pi]
```

is conditionally unbiased for C_pi given the full original world, because
the pilot coin is independent of its unobserved values conditional on z.
No importance correction for the original logging policy is needed: every
request receives this explicit randomized completion opportunity. If width
w_pi=U_pi-L_pi, its conditional pilot variance is at most
(1/q-1)w_pi^2/4. At width zero, no pilot is needed and its exact known invoice
can be used directly. Actual replay purchases, failed probes, computation,
retention and random-bit production must all enter the bill.

With a public upper quote c(z) for the additional pilot fee, minimizing the
bound E[w(z)^2/q(z)] subject to E[q(z)c(z)]<=B yields the usual clipped
allocation q(z)=min(1,w(z)/sqrt(lambda*c(z))) when costs are positive.
This is ordinary control-variate inverse-probability estimation and allocation,
not a new estimator. The repository's earlier paid selective-feedback and
observable-performance constructions already contain purchase probabilities
and bounded martingale guarantees. The new local object is the **checked
completion interval for another acquisition policy's invoice**, and an actual
cache-aware replay route that makes its missing residual observable.

Prospective first checks: exact interval DP versus all small completions,
attaining witnesses and changed-certificate rejection; the P/Q countermodel's
sharp receipt-law bounds; and the naive-pooling failure. Then compare a small
source-free on-policy cost bandit with the prepared-model services, naming
differences in their statistical fee-guarantee duties. Paid replay is a later
experiment only if its information/cost calculation supports that step.
