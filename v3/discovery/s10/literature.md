Read the primary HTML of Hellerstein, Kletenik and Parthasarathy,
[A Tight Bound for Stochastic Submodular Cover](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.01149),
sections 1--3, especially section 2 Assumptions 1--4. Its independent item states,
observable pointwise polymatroid utility and universal coverability matter.
Our single fixed dual yields a capped additive observable reduction. A guaranteed
fallback supplies coverability. Overlapping group queries have dependent states,
even if their underlying coordinates are independent, so this theorem's greedy
ratio cannot be imported for that grouped menu. No approximation factor is
claimed there. The elementary conditional-probability policy improvement is
separate and does apply when its conditional completion probability is valid.

The existing [s06 cut-dual derivation](../s06/cut_dual_derivation.md) already
contains source-bound rational multipliers, slack-corrected lower functions and
the binary fixed-action box bound. s10's direction is its repeated-action
expression, explicit audit-score compilation and actual query service on partly
recognized CNF. Reproving the old dual inequality is not a new research result.

Read Deshpande, Hellerstein and Kletenik,
[Approximation Algorithms for Stochastic Boolean Function Evaluation and
Stochastic Submodular Set Cover](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.0726), sections 2,
4.1--4.3 and 5, especially Lemma 1's disjunctive utility and Theorem 5.
Sequential threshold testing and certificate stopping are established ordinary
alternatives. Their 3-approximation concerns binary linear-threshold evaluation;
it is not automatically a factor for our grouped, multivalued, one-sided duty
with a separately executable fallback. The disjunctive construction does give
a way to combine fixed certificate utilities: multiply their residual quotas,
then subtract from the product of initial quotas. This avoids the invalid
arbitrary-maximum construction, but multiplies the goal value and can worsen
numerical and planning costs. s10 should use that known construction if it needs
a genuine portfolio cover potential, while distinguishing it from a probability
computation or an optimal query policy.

Read Boutilier, Patrascu, Poupart and Schuurmans (2006),

Read Gilbert and Spanjaard (2017),
[A double oracle approach to minmax regret optimization problems with interval data](https://arxiv.org/html/1602.01764),
sections 3.1--3.3 and 4--5. Proposition 1, explicitly credited there to Mastin,
Jaillet and Chin (2015), gives the induced objective
`q_i = l_i + (u_i-l_i) E[x_i]` for a mixed action's adversarial response.
With `E[x_i]=a_i/m`, this is exactly the normalized s08/s10 repeated-action
formula. **That formula is prior art, not a novelty of this program.**
Deterministic integer repetition restricts implementable marginals, but does not
make the algebra new. The mixed-game value bounds pure minimax regret; do not
confuse it with an integral three-copy action. Their polynomial-oracle premises
do not apply to arbitrary SAT. Improving the action menu and retaining source
computations are required conventional comparisons before claiming audit gains.

Read the authors' [Mastin, Jaillet and Chin manuscript](https://www.mit.edu/~jaillet/general/rand_mmr_final.pdf),

in the convex hull of feasible actions. Its polynomial result is conditional
on polynomial nominal optimization. Our finite triple search below is integral
and can be exponential; it does not inherit that result.

Read Padmanabhan and Natarajan (2019),
[Tree Bounds for Sums of Bernoulli Random Variables](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.06321),
sections1.2--1.3,3 and4.2. Extremal probabilities with specified marginals and
their affine dual certificates are established ordinary optimization. Their
polynomial tree result requires specified Bernoulli pair marginals on a tree;
it does not justify assuming conditional independence. Our three-score,
identical-marginal count LP instead uses permutation symmetry and direct finite
primal/dual checking. Its weighted sum of separately sharp prefix bounds need
not be jointly sharp. No novelty or tree-complexity theorem is imported.
