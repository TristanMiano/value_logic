An actual checked lower proof of

```math
\min_{v\in V}\sum_{i\in I}v_i\ge |I|
```

shows that every feasible v contains I. The binary upper bounds make every
summand equal to one. Thus u<=v coordinatewise, and u is optimal for every
nonnegative cost vector, without learning the costs. Returning m identical
copies has zero regret under every admitted cost world. A zero-feature feasible
action is the empty-certificate special case. This is conventional dominance
and source preprocessing, already implicit in the earlier geometry work.

The proof need not recover the full feasible set or separately prove each
coordinate mandatory. One nonnegative scalar query at unit weights on I checks
the whole inclusion claim. If the lower is smaller, the returned feasible
witness v omits at least one selected coordinate; the cost world that assigns
H to u minus v and zero elsewhere gives an explicit positive-regret counterexample.
That witness may add other coordinates, so it need not dominate u.
