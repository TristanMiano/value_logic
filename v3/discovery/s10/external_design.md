# A stronger external-source comparison

**Working DEVELOPMENT reasoning; Codex (GPT-6), October 14, 2026.**

Before spending on larger paid requests, compare sparse cuts with exact initial
induced-objective proofs. A source can have few feasible models or many fixed
variables. In that case a tighter one-time proof may show that a supplied action
already meets a tolerance chosen from a weak packing bound. Paying repeated
audits to meet that weak certificate would not demonstrate a useful saving.
Do not hide this by excluding the exact ordinary source solve or changing the
tolerance after the request outcomes.

For each proposed m-copy action, the initial exact regret is its upper cost
minus the optimum of q_i=4 a_i. The actual complete provider can acquire and
check this optimum once. Its preparation bill may repay at a repeated horizon.
Compare the exact value with the three extraction budgets in uf01. A paid
external-source declaration should use a tolerance fixed before costs and give
ordinary controllers the same available proof mechanisms.

A second issue is a proved source backbone. If feature v_i is identically 0 or
1 over all feasible source models, its cost contributes equally to every
same-copy-count repair and cancels from repair-only regret. A complete-information
baseline should be allowed to audit only coordinates not proved constant,
then solve with weight zero on the constants. This requires actual source-bound
proofs of the constants; inspecting the supplied models alone is insufficient.
Checking only model agreement would miss a feasible alternative with another
value. Fixed-price replacement would require retaining absolute common costs;
the present external trial concerns repair-only regret.

Positive and negative derived unit clauses can establish that geometry through
the existing resolution checker. Bounded extraction may miss units even when a
standard failed-literal SAT check finds them cheaply. The latter is therefore
another possible ordinary preparation route, with its real proof invoice.
The earlier s07 source-geometry result already justifies common-cost cancellation;
this is an implementation/comparison requirement, not a new cancellation theorem.

Finally, the source's 20 Boolean variables are newly interpreted as uncertain
cost features. This evaluates a synthetic repair task on an external logical
source. It does not make the arbitrary cost model an empirical SATLIB property,
or establish generality for correlated costs or arbitrary soft-clause features.
