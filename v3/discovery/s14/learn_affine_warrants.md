# Learn a jointly usable part of an affine model

Codex (GPT-6), DEVELOPMENT, October 22, 2026. A constructive learning
adapter using ordinary linear algebra and sample-span coverage. No new
general PAC learner or statistical principle is claimed.

Fix a nonempty affine model C=x0+V and a chosen r-dimensional subspace
D of its dual equations BEFORE independent deployment worlds are sampled.
D might be the prepared graph target/budget warrant from target_warrants.md.
Let H have a basis of D as rows and define the model-error syndrome

    z(x)=H*(x-x0) in F2^r.

For N iid worlds X_i, suppose paid checks return nonempty affine sets
Z_i containing the actual z(X_i). These may be refined adaptively and
dependently; no independent-censoring assumption is required. Their
truthfulness is required. Define

    S=span(union_i Z_i),
    W={x: z(x) in S}.

The retained equations have coefficient vectors in S-perp: every a in
S-perp warrants a*H*(x-x0)=0. This can preserve useful combinations even
when individual original model equations fail. It never asserts that all
of C is true. If a different affine origin is learned, include the first
sample as an anchor and use the affine-hull variant; do not silently
reuse the fixed-origin bound below.

## Coverage without a holdout for choosing the retained linear rules

Let S_N^true=span(z(X_1),...,z(X_N)), and let p_N be its population missing
mass. Since S contains S_N^true, P(X not in W)<=p_N for every realized
set of truthful partial receipts. A simple dimension bound is

    P_training(p_N>epsilon)
       <= sum_(j=0)^(r-1) binom(N,j) epsilon^j (1-epsilon)^(N-j).    (1)

For r=0 the error is zero. To prove (1), expose the samples sequentially.
The span gains one dimension precisely on an outside sample. Its missing
mass never increases. On the event p_N>epsilon every preceding outside
probability exceeded epsilon, yet there were fewer than r rank gains.
Couple each outside indicator to an independent uniform random variable;
until the missing mass falls below epsilon it dominates a Bernoulli(epsilon)
indicator. Thus the event in question requires fewer than r successes in
N such Bernoulli trials. This proves the bound without claiming it is tight.

Exchangeability also gives E[p_N]<=r/(N+1): among N+1 vectors at most r
can lie outside the span of all the others. The next sample's chance of
being such an essential vector equals the expected essential count divided
by N+1. These are ordinary finite-dimensional sample/compression arguments.

One common event X in W then protects all adaptive releases justified by
the retained equations and truthful receipts. It is an unconditional
deployment-case error bound, not a conditional error rate among releases.
The r in (1) is the dimension of the FIXED prior equation space, not the
smaller retained rank selected after viewing the data. Substituting the
latter without a new argument would be an optimistic selection error.

## A concrete incomplete-data compiler

If Z_i=b_i+U_i, its linear span is generated by b_i and a basis of U_i.
Ordinary binary elimination computes S and S-perp without enumerating
the possible syndromes. When raw receipts are A_i*x=d_i, map their affine
solution set through H after shifting x0 to obtain Z_i. Consistency and
the mapping are paid linear-algebra operations.

Refining an existing truthful receipt shrinks its Z_i and can shrink S,
allowing additional rules. Adding a new case can expand S and withdraw
rules. Both changes are principled. Recomputing intersections or preserving
dependencies has real storage/update costs; this is no free training oracle.

There is a serious observation bottleneck. If a raw coordinate j is
unobserved on even one training case whose only receipts are coordinate
values, its completion set contains both values of j with everything else
fixed. Every retained equation must then have coefficient zero at j.
More generally a retained equation must be measurable in EVERY case's
receipt row space and agree with x0 on each case. Thus merely collecting
many very incomplete samples can leave no usable rule. Directly purchased
parity receipts, shared observed coordinates, or separate justified cohorts
can change this cost comparison; missing labels cannot be filled in as true.

## Scope and cheap disposition

This provides a safe data-driven weakening of a fixed affine model, with
one bound covering all retained combinations and later adaptive use.
It is related to learning linear varieties/parities, sample compression,
implicit learning from partial observations, and the earlier task-warrant
calibration. A generic novelty claim is inappropriate. The observation
bottleneck is decisive enough that a large learner benchmark is deferred.
First find a service where reusable linear measurements are naturally
available and the retained rank/coverage is useful at their actual price.

## A stronger imported certificate during adaptive refinement

There is a useful data-dependent bound with the right complexity variable.
Let k be the rank of the TRUE sampled syndromes, not the retained-rule rank.
Greedily keeping a basis of the sampled vectors is a stable compression
scheme for the classifier that accepts their linear span (all labels are
positive). Deleting unselected points leaves the reconstructed span unchanged.
Hanneke and Kontorovich, *Stable Sample Compression Schemes* (2021),
Theorem 10, therefore gives, when k<N/2,

    p_N <= 2/[N-2k] * [k log(4)+log((k+1)(k+2)/delta)]              (2)

with probability at least 1-delta. Their Corollary 11 gives the simpler
bound 4*[6k+log(e/delta)]/N without the k<N/2 condition. These are imported
theorems; their statistical rates are not new here.

Although k can be unknown under partial receipts, any computed upper bound
k_bar>=k can replace it because these displayed bounds are increasing in
their complexity argument. At minimum k_bar=min(N,dim S) works. The one
latent-sample probability event then certifies EVERY truthful adaptive
refinement of the same fixed-N cohort, since W always contains the latent
sample span and every k_bar is a deterministic upper bound on its rank.
No union penalty for the number of refinement steps is needed. Looking at
additional sample sizes N is a different operation and needs simultaneous
or sequential coverage; (1) and (2) are stated for fixed N.

The cheap affine_learning_probe.py passed 192 exact tiny-law/sample-size
panels, 768 risk inequalities and 100 partial-receipt containment panels.
All 100 random partial panels retained only the zero equation; this
negative usefulness result is retained. The structured always-false-model
example retains a two-dimensional equation space. At epsilon0.1/delta0.05,
the elementary fixed-r bound needs N=29,46,76,129 for r=1,2,4,8 respectively.
These are sample counts under the stated law, not measured acquisition fees.
The probe also supplies a concrete failure of replacing r by retained rank:
with one draw uniform on the three nonzero vectors in F2^2, the latent span
misses mass2/3 on every draw, while that substitution would bound the
