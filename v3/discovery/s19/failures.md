# Failures, negative results and evidence limits

Codex (GPT-6), DEVELOPMENT. Historical executed files remain unchanged.

The first perceptron checker reported a false strongly connected component
because its DFS traversal was defective. [Failed source](perceptron_v1.py),
[plan](perceptron_v1_plan.json) and [failure](perceptron_v1_failure.json) are
preserved. The separate [repaired checker](perceptron_check.py) and
[result](perceptron_result.json) pass their declared finite cases. This was
a deterministic checker bug, not evidence of CPU damage. No failed attempt
was overwritten or retroactively counted as passing.

The actual shared-proof integration is unfavorable overall: uniform and
both predeclared useful-advice banks exceed the fallback bill. Failed and
unfinished proof work, verification and controller counters remain visible.
The hindsight bank is an oracle comparator, not a deployed saving. See
[integration](proof_integration.md) and its frozen result.

Several promising-looking constructions reduced to prior methods: binary
and multiclass fusion, generic probability recovery, score-paid search,
matroid receipt connectivity and parts of subset convolution. These are
scientific dispositions, not implementation failures. No novelty claim is
rescued by renaming the ordinary equivalent.

Some primary pages timed out or refused direct fetching. Literature notes
distinguish inspected sections from abstract/snippet leads; inaccessible
material was not treated as a completed comparison. Shell path lookups and
atomic patch-context errors during note preparation changed no executed
scientific evidence and do not warrant hardware attribution.

Twenty-one whole research intervals are excluded by explicit dispositions
because of context compaction, mixed modes or an administrative lookup.
Explicit O/context recovery is also excluded. The research floor is met
only after these corrections. All research is same-model DEVELOPMENT;
finite probes, shared helpers and targeted corruptions are not independent
confirmation or proof of generic solver completeness.

During publication, pub/recover_inputs.py materialized the two proof-service
generators' deterministic input bytes without rerunning their policies or
tests. Both hashes match the digests in the original results. These files
are publication-time reconstructions, not falsely backdated saved inputs.
