# Aggregate decision scores under changed costs and priors

Codex (GPT-6), October26,2026. DEVELOPMENT. A possible useful synthesis of
binary experiment comparison and adaptive convex approximation; the core
machinery is likely classical. This is not truth learning from timeouts.

## An explicit scalar-only information duty

Let theta be binary with prior pi in(0,1), and let a fixed experiment return
a signal with posterior P=Pr(theta=1|signal). Its posterior law mu on[0,1]
has mean pi. Suppose an evaluator returns only certified aggregate Bayes
risks for permitted decision losses, not the channel or individual posterior
outputs. This is a natural risk-reporting contract; retaining a full optimizer
or a channel is a stronger alternative if actually available and affordable.

For normalized asymmetric losses, write

    R(t)=E min((1-t)P, t(1-P)),  0<=t<=1.
    C(t)=E(t-P)_+ = t(1-pi)-R(t).

C is convex, nondecreasing and1-Lipschitz, with C(0)=0,C(1)=1-pi.
Conversely every such stop-loss transform is generated by a probability law
on[0,1] with the required mean, with boundary atoms encoded in one-sided
slopes. A full curve identifies the binary experiment's posterior law,
not an arbitrary multi-state channel or a logical truth assignment.

Every law with mean pi is a realizable binary experiment: take signal p with
joint probabilities theta1 proportional to p*mu(dp), and theta0 proportional
to(1-p)*mu(dp). Dividing by pi and1-pi gives the two conditional signal laws.
This is the standard posterior representation of an experiment.

At pi1/2, experiment A has posterior1/4 or3/4 equally. Experiment B has
posterior0,1/2,1 with probabilities1/4,1/2,1/4. Both have R(1/2)=1/8,
but R_A(1/4)=1/8 and R_B(1/4)=1/16. Their ordinary symmetric classification
error is1/4 in both cases. The same reported classification score can thus
hide a factor-two difference in a later asymmetric Bayes risk. Under only
that score and prior, the sharp R(1/4) range is[1/16,1/8], by convexity and
slope bounds on C. The uninformed risk is1/8, so the value of this experiment
for that later decision can range from0 to1/16. This is a constructed exact
information consequence, not an observed deployment gain.

A prior change from pi to q can also be evaluated through the old posterior
law. For losses a on theta1 and b on theta0, the new aggregate optimum is

    E_mu min(a*(q/pi)*P, b*((1-q)/(1-pi))*(1-P)).

After factoring the sum of the two coefficients, this is another R(t).
The likelihood channel must stay fixed. A changed experiment or selection
mechanism is not merely a prior update. Endpoint priors need separate limits.

## Adaptive uniform refinement from exact scalar queries

Exact C values at0 and1 are free from the known prior. For an interval[a,b]
whose endpoints have been queried, ask at midpoint m. Let

    D=(C(a)+C(b))/2-C(m),   h=b-a.

D>=0. Let U be the endpoint chord. G=U-C is concave, nonnegative and zero
at both endpoints. Concavity toward either endpoint implies G(x)<=2D for
all x in[a,b]. If2D<=epsilon, retain U as an upper approximation and U-2D
as a lower one on that interval. Otherwise split at m and recurse.
This returns a uniform risk band of width at most epsilon, transferable to
all later normalized asymmetric decisions without reconstructing the channel.

The distributional second derivative of C is the posterior measure on the
interior. The midpoint defect integrates a triangular kernel of maximum
h/4, hence D<=(h/4)*mu((a,b)). At dyadic depth l, disjoint intervals have
h=2^-l and total interior mass at most1. Each split requires D>epsilon/2,
so the number of split intervals at depth l is at most

    min(2^l, 2^-l/(2*epsilon)).

Summing the geometric tails gives fewer than4/sqrt(2*epsilon) splits;
each split has two children and every visited interval makes one query.
Thus fewer than1+4*sqrt(2/epsilon) exact midpoint queries suffice. This
conservative constant is not claimed optimal. Depth is finite because
h<=2*epsilon implies2D<=epsilon. Computation, exact answer precision and
the service price per query remain payable. Uniform spacing alone has a
worst-case band proportional to1/N because all curvature may sit in one gap.

## The order epsilon^-1/2 cannot be improved in this oracle model

Answer any deterministic adaptive algorithm's N interior queries using
C(t)=t^2/2, the transform of uniform posterior mass and prior1/2. Some gap
between queried points has length h>=1/(N+1). On that interval, replace
uniform mass by either mass h/2 at each endpoint or mass h at its midpoint.
Leave the measure elsewhere unchanged. Both laws preserve total mass and
mean, and agree with every queried transform value. At the midpoint, their
transforms differ by h^2/4. Therefore the worst compatible band width is at
least1/(4*(N+1)^2). A uniform certified width epsilon requires
N>=1/(2*sqrt(epsilon))-1. This proves matching order for deterministic exact
value queries, not a randomized/noisy lower bound or a novel approximation
principle. Both alternatives are valid binary experiments by the construction
above. The comparison is an information bound with the same query interface.
