This maps the fixed contribution to the author's goal; it does not replace
unresolved broad questions with an easier assertion of general success.

The organizing goal asks for mechanisms by which a bounded reasoner makes
materially better use of unresolved mathematics, fallible models, reasoning
costs or counterfactual changes. The disjunction is substantive: one useful
positive construction can advance this goal without solving every component.
V19 concerns what a reasoner can infer from several retained scalar optimum
reports about a shared but incompletely known source. Its exact output and
costs specify a finite inference service, rather than a metaphysical account
of value or a general learner of mathematical truth.

There is no principled opposition between the spirit of value logic and
ordinary mathematics. Here the value-logic motivation selects what to retain:
shared-source constraints, attainable values, costs and explicit conditions
under which a conclusion survives. Ordinary disjunctive programming, LP
duality and inside-outside computation implement that motivation. The intended
moderate contribution is the explicit guarantee at this interface, not a
claim that conventional methods cannot represent fallible models.

## A direct probability-envelope interpretation of the fixed example

This is a mathematical interpretation of the already selected example, not
new empirical evidence. The embedding was already developed in
[s19's credal-receipt note](../../discovery/s19/credal_receipts.md); this is
its confirmation-stage reconstruction, not a new contribution. For either
two-coordinate response x=(x1,x2) from
robust_gap.md with a=b=10 and epsilon=delta=1/10, define

    p(x)=(x1/30, x2/30, 1-(x1+x2)/30).

All coordinates are strictly positive because each response coordinate is
positive and the total is at most22.2<30. Each original response polygon
maps affinely to a compact rational polygon in the three-state simplex,
with nonempty relative interior. The two source blocks remain independent.
For losses L1=(30,0,0), L2=(0,30,0) and Lnew=(30,30,0), their expectations
are x1, x2 and x1+x2 respectively. Thus the observations can be read as two
known-loss lower-envelope reports from an unknown pair of candidate laws.
The future report is another lower-envelope expected loss, with the exact
two-component prediction already proved.

Equivalently, divide by30 and interpret the first two losses as indicators
of disjoint singleton events. The future quantity is the lower probability
of their union under the unknown two-point credal hull. Knowing only the
two lower singleton probabilities does not identify that union value; the
individual model-region restrictions supply additional structure. Removing
those restrictions or allowing more generators can change the prediction.
This makes the connection to probability information concrete while keeping
known expectation identities and imprecise-probability machinery credited.

The embedding also exposes a limitation: these are possible reported risk
models, not a proof that the real data-generating law belongs to their hull.
A discrepancy identifies joint inconsistency among source premises and reports.
It cannot diagnose empirical truth from algebra alone.
