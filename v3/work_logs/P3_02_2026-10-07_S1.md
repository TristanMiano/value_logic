## Result and disposition

The scientific work is complete at the **finite probability-information**
scope. The [principal derivation](../derivations/02_probability_information.md)
contains the assumptions, proofs, examples and attribution for PI-1–18. Its
central answer is conditional: a value observation contains probability
information only relative to its payoff meaning, source family, observation
contract and required service. Full-law recovery, a specified family of
expected losses, certified intervals, all preferences and some optimal action
have different information requirements.

The finite full-simplex case has an exact row-space criterion. Calibration
changes that criterion: unknown positive scale removes free use of probability
normalization, and an unknown common offset removes the absolute level of the
observed rows. Constructive query repairs, including positive and bounded-payoff
restrictions, expose the consequences. The decision-only analysis includes
cases where action selection requires fewer queries than probability or cost
recovery, and a coefficient-alphabet example where exact rational representation
costs more but a stated regret tolerance permits approximation.

The note also gives sharp compatible-source intervals, missing-correlation
and unknown-stakes counterexamples, noisy-recovery witnesses and ordinary
affine baselines, conditional-ratio boundaries, distinctions among proper-score
objects, and explicit limits of nonlinear unit changes. An algebraic decoder
does not automatically become a native probability-unit proof: the inherited
language needs the declared conversion premises, and does not acquire arbitrary
uncertain products or variable division from this analysis.
