## Candidate object and criterion

The candidate object is a bounded computation-selection service connected to
versioned mathematical forecasts and explicit evidence acquisition. It has
complete-policy profiles, task/resource feature prices, exact numerical lower
bounds, paid setup and selection, and bounded audits of its own fixed procedure.
A companion adapts the P3-06 potential to four action roles and an explicitly
paid sampler. A further ordinary finite planner makes a supplied uncertain
performance law operational without assuming its value of information.

The plausible contribution types are a modest formal adaptation, an informative
limitation, or a useful implementation synthesis. The assessment follows the
[author's broad criterion](../../RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md#4-lanes-comparisons-and-contribution).
It does not require worldwide priority, a new general logic, or superiority on
every benchmark. It does require an identified delta and evidence of its
magnitude within a named comparison scope. The ability of an ordinary controller
to execute the same algorithm is informative and does not alone disqualify a
useful synthesis.

## What the evidence actually changes

The principal paired-profile construction is now a real finite program with
independently reconstructed boundaries. Its high-stakes selected computation
has a positive lower continuation value, and additional samples change the
expensive-storage decision once. This supports a specific account of paid
computation under acquired uncertainty. It is a substantive improvement over
leaving expected benefit as an undefined oracle.

The evidence does not support a broad efficiency advantage. A positive audit
against fallback coexists with a much cheaper charged exact solver. The
analytical ordinary profile is far cheaper than the sampled profile on this
transparent arithmetic population and makes better asymmetric-price choices.
The four-action full-feedback protocol pays for every exact label and loses
to its always-BUY service-fee control despite valid mixture-regret statements.
The dependency example and the finite acquisition planner both show that a
favorable continuation value can coexist with an unfavorable total bill.

The strongest local theoretical improvements are the sharp finite-action
constants, the represented-context/known-shift extensions, and the exact
adaptive-acquisition envelope. They are independently checkable and useful
for delimiting this implementation. Their proofs are short specializations
of established mathematical methods. At present there is no supported claim
that one of them materially expands a useful reasoning service beyond the
credible ordinary combination, or that the particular integration's practical
benefit justifies its added machinery. That is the missing magnitude evidence;
it is not a demand for universal superiority.
