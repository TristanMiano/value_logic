## Section 3.3: represented context prices

The first extension is mathematically valid under the fixed-feature and fixed-policy conditions explicitly stated in the section. Write the raw paired coordinate as `d_j(X)` and fix bounded measurable context functions `g_r(X)` before profiling. The retained coordinates are the products

\[
h_{jr}(X)=g_r(X)d_j(X).
\]

For every represented schedule `lambda_j(X)=sum_r theta_jr g_r(X)`, linearity gives

\[
\mathbb E\left[\sum_j\lambda_j(X)d_j(X)\right]
=\sum_{j,r}\theta_{jr}\,\mathbb E[h_{jr}(X)].
\]

On simultaneous product-coordinate intervals, choose the lower endpoint for `theta_jr >= 0` and the upper endpoint for `theta_jr < 0`. The signs used are those of **the expansion coefficients**. The argument remains valid when the represented price function itself changes sign across requests. Since the event already covers every product coordinate, the coefficients may be selected from the acquired data without an additional union bound over their continuum. Conditioning on that data fixes the selected rule for future evaluation under the applicable law.

The expanded profile requires valid product ranges and its own simultaneous-event cell count. For `g_r` in `[c_r,e_r]` and `d_j` in `[a_j,b_j]`, safe endpoints are the minimum and maximum of the four products `c_r*a_j`, `c_r*b_j`, `e_r*a_j`, `e_r*b_j`. No independence between context and task features is needed. Constant product coordinates can be treated exactly.

These are qualifications of acquiring the proposed expanded profile, not missing claims of the current implementation. Section 3.3 explicitly says the existing fourteen-coordinate profile is not silently reused. It charges computing/admitting context features and treats new price-reactive policy behavior as a different complete map. The claim is for the represented family of expected comparisons; it does not provide arbitrary query-dependent pricing or an individual-query truth guarantee.

## Section 3.3: total variation transfer

The second extension also passes. With the explicitly stated convention

\[
\operatorname{TV}(\nu,\mu)=\sup_A|\nu(A)-\mu(A)|,
\]

a measurable coordinate `f` in `[a,b]` under both laws obeys

\[
|\mathbb E_\nu f-\mathbb E_\mu f|
\le\operatorname{TV}(\nu,\mu)(b-a).
\]

For `a < b`, normalize to `[0,1]` and integrate the differences of level-set probabilities. Each integrand has absolute value at most total variation, and the integration interval has length one. A constant coordinate has zero change directly. A binary indicator attains the coefficient, so no extra factor of two is required. For a symmetric coordinate in `[-R,R]`, its width is already `2R`.

Consequently, on an old simultaneous coverage event and under a separately justified bound `TV(nu,mu) <= rho`, expanding each coordinate interval by `rho*(b_j-a_j)` and clipping it to its analytic range is valid. Applying the signed constant-price lower score yields

\[
G_\nu(\lambda)\ge L_\mu(\lambda)
-\rho\sum_j|\lambda_j|(b_j-a_j).
\]

The population law `mu` must be the law underlying the profile, rather than just its empirical distribution, and the analytic ranges must hold on both laws' supports. If the separately justified TV certificate is itself probabilistic with failure probability `eta`, its event must be combined with the profile event; a union bound gives failure at most `delta+eta`, without requiring independence between those events. The current section is conditional on a supported TV bound and does not claim that such a probabilistic certificate has been obtained.

The section correctly keeps feature-map and implementation changes separate from population drift. It asserts no empirical drift certificate for the development runs. Both the primary reviewer and the delegated same-model mathematical reviewer found no substantive defect in the stated extensions.
