## Finding

**The proposed extension is sound under the restrictions below.** An active
simplex support of size three, or any other size bounded by fixed `A`, does
not itself create a denominator recursion. The decisive property is that
features depend on the prior residual only through a dyadic forecast `p`.
The residual enters the score, but no residual-dependent rational ratio is
fed directly into the next feature coefficients.

The completed P3-06 source is
[CF-11](../../../../derivations/06_cost_forecast_refinement.md#cf-11-restricted-finite-numeric-state).
This addendum checks the additional simplex support factors and inverse-
polynomial tolerance; it does not reopen the completed two-action theorem.
The associated multi-action proof is
[multi_action_reconstruction.md](../multi_action_reconstruction.md).

## 1. Required restrictions

Fix independently of the horizon:

- The action count `A`, total feature dimension `d`, tent resolution and
  feature-block counts.
- The rational feature scales and a fixed positive rational `eta_0`.
- A common integer divisible by the denominators of all admitted action-row
  coefficients, weights and supplied expert values. Their numerators are
  bounded by fixed polynomials in the round index. Fixed here means one
  denominator for the entire stated family, not a new denominator at every
  round or a horizon-dependent integer of unreported size.

Use

```math
\eta_t=\eta_0 2^{-h_t},\qquad
h_t=\lceil\log_2(t+1)\rceil.
```

Choose reports only at the endpoints or by dyadic bisection. Let the requested
positive score tolerance be a dyadic/rational quantity with `O(log(t+1))`
encoding size, bounded below by an inverse polynomial. Store rationals in
reduced/canonical form or normalize to the stated common denominator. The
cost of this reduction is part of arithmetic cost. Merely allowing exact
but unreduced numerator/denominator strings would not imply a small-state
claim: repeated naive fraction additions can retain avoidable denominator
products.

Known row values and expert values must actually be available through the
permitted interface. Their compact encodings do not bound the work required
to produce them. The theorem concerns the particular fixed set of numeric
statistics, not arbitrary query text, proofs, a used-ID registry, copies of
past exports or the whole delayed-pool state.

## 2. Polynomial magnitudes give logarithmic root depth

Let `D_t` be the slope range. The new decision-coordinate magnitude is at
most `gamma_a w_t D_t`, while a valid Lipschitz bound is
`gamma_a w_t A D_t^2/(4 eta_t)`. These are polynomial in `t+1` under the
hypotheses, since `1/eta_t=O(t+1)` up to a fixed multiplier. The inherited
expert and tent coordinates have polynomial magnitude and Lipschitz bounds
under the same fixed-dimension assumptions.

Write `M_t` for any polynomial feature-coordinate supremum. Since
`|y-p|<=1`, the crude bound

```math
|R_{t-1,j}|\le\sum_{s<t}M_{s,j}
```

is polynomial. This does not require the desired bit bound or a prior
small-tolerance conclusion. It avoids a circular appeal to successful root
search. Substituting the polynomial magnitudes and feature Lipschitz bounds
into the inherited computable score Lipschitz bound

```math
L_{S,t}\le\sum_j
  (|R_{t-1,j}|L_{t,j}+M_{t,j}^2+M_{t,j}L_{t,j})
```

gives a polynomial quantity. If no endpoint is accepted, continuity provides
a root. A dyadic bracket whose length is sufficiently smaller than
`tolerance/L_S` reaches the requested score tolerance, requiring
`O(log(t+1))` bisections. Endpoint reports have constant-size encodings.

This is a sufficient arithmetic root budget. If the actual hard budget does
not fund it, the procedure still needs its declared exhaustion/allowance
behavior; the theorem does not authorize overrunning that budget.

## 3. Active supports introduce only fixed denominator factors

For a dyadic candidate `p=s/2^k` and an active support `S` of size `m<=A`,

```math
q_i(p)=\frac1m+
 \frac{\sum_{j\in S}c_j(p)-m c_i(p)}{m\eta_t}
\qquad(i\in S).
```

The action costs at `p` have denominator dividing a fixed input denominator
times `2^k`. Dividing by `eta_t=eta_0 2^{-h_t}` multiplies by a power of two
and divides by the fixed numerator of `eta_0`. The support divisor `m`
divides the fixed integer `lcm(1,...,A)`. Consequently a fixed integer `C_q`
exists, independent of `t,k` and the chosen support, such that every ideal
mixture coordinate has denominator dividing

```math
C_q2^k.
```

The multiplication by `2^{h_t}` does not create a growing odd denominator.
It also does not increase the required dyadic denominator exponent in this
formula. The bound holds on support boundaries as well, since zero
coordinates and tied support descriptions give the same unique projection.

After the fixed number of products with slopes, weights and scales, one
fixed factor `C_phi` works for all feature coordinates:

```math
\operatorname{denom}(\Phi_{t,j}(p))\mid C_\Phi 2^k,
\qquad
\operatorname{denom}((y-p)\Phi_{t,j}(p))\mid C_\Phi 2^{2k}.
```

Let `K_T` be the largest candidate/report dyadic exponent through time `T`,
including the initial midpoint bit. Adding residual increments therefore
keeps each residual denominator within `C_phi 2^{2K_T}`. The sizes of the
active supports are not multiplied through time: all increments already
share the fixed support factor, and addition uses its common multiple.

The score does contain products `R_j Phi_j` and squares `Phi_j^2`, but their
results only determine a dyadic branch choice. They do not become a new
arbitrary coefficient in the following feature map. A fixed factor and at
most `2^{3K_T+O(1)}` cover score denominators; the variance and allowance
statistics need at most a fixed factor such as

```math
C_\Phi^2 2^{4K_T+O(1)}.
```

Other fixed loss statistics involve only a fixed number of comparable
products. The cumulative smoothing statistic
`sum_t w_t eta_t (A-1)/(4A)` has its own fixed factor and the nested dyadic
denominator `2^{h_T}`. This last condition matters: using harmonic
`eta_t=eta_0/(t+1)` would not retain the same smoothing-sum denominator claim.

## 4. Bit lengths and storage scope

All current numeric magnitudes are polynomial, and their denominators divide
a fixed factor times a power `2^{O(log(T+1))}`. Reduced numerators and
denominators therefore each have `O(log(T+1))` bits. The fixed-dimension
numeric state uses `O(d log(T+1))` bits. Retaining each numeric issue and
settlement record once through time `T` uses `O(dT log(T+1))` bits.

If the dyadic execution sampler uses `b_t=O(log(t+1))` fair bits, its
integer floors, cumulative counts and probabilities have the same order of
numeric size. Its state does not enter the ideal continuous feature map.
An arbitrarily larger chosen sampling precision is an additional resource
parameter and is not covered by the stated logarithmic sampler bound.

No uniform physical CPU or full program-memory bound follows. Rational
sorting, multiplication, comparison and reduction have actual bit costs;
expert production, mathematical computation, checked evidence, identifier
tracking and storage accesses can dominate them. A delayed pool of `K`
live copies multiplies the per-copy state obligation; pending records and
the retained history remain separately charged.

## 5. Where the proof stops

The fixed-factor argument can fail if action coefficients or experts acquire
unbounded new odd denominators, if the smoothing numerator varies without a
fixed divisibility restriction, if non-dyadic residual-dependent exact roots
are used as forecasts, or if residual-dependent ratios enter future feature
coefficients. Continually updated empirical frequencies are not automatically
fixed-denominator inputs. A profile frozen once may be treated as fixed
input, but its initial size and acquisition cost are still real.

Under the hypotheses actually stated above, there is no hidden denominator
recursion from support sizes such as three. This is a restricted numeric
representation result. It does not by itself establish sublinear decision
regret under increasing stakes, adequate label coverage, positive all-in
value of the extra forecasting work, or a hard end-to-end resource guarantee.
