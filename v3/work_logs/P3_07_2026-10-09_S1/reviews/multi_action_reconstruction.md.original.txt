## 1. Exact finite theorem and its hypotheses

Fix `A>=1` action identities and positive scales `gamma_a` that remain fixed
throughout the episode. Before receiving the current checked binary answer
`y_t`, announce finite affine action rows

```math
c_{t,a}(p)=b_{t,a}+d_{t,a}p,
```

a nonnegative finite weight `w_t`, and a positive smoothing parameter
`eta_t`. The rows and parameter may change predictably between rounds, but
the action identities and coordinate meanings stay fixed. Every action
assigned positive execution probability must be feasible within the current
hard budget, including required checking and fallback reserves.

Let the **ideal** action mixture be

```math
q_t(p)=\arg\min_{q\in\Delta_A}
 \left[q\cdot c_t(p)+\frac{\eta_t}{2}\|q\|_2^2\right],
\qquad
\Delta_A=\{q\ge0:\sum_aq_a=1\}.
```

Write `dbar_t(p)=sum_a q_t,a(p)d_t,a`. Include these decision coordinates in
the forecasting feature vector **before the run**:

```math
\Phi_{t,a}(p)=\gamma_a w_t[\bar d_t(p)-d_{t,a}].
```

Other already justified continuous coordinates may be included by direct
sum. Assume the forecast and admitted-feedback process satisfies the
inherited P3-06 potential inequality for this **enlarged** feature vector,
with total residual vector `R_T` and `||R_T||^2<=B_T`. This must be the
potential of the new procedure, not a certificate from an old run to which
the new coordinates were attached retrospectively.

For the issued mixture `q_t=q_t(p_t)`, define
`cbar_t(y)=sum_b q_t,b c_t,b(y)`. Then, simultaneously for every fixed
action identity `a`,

```math
\sum_{t\le T}w_t[\bar c_t(y_t)-c_{t,a}(y_t)]
 \le\sum_{t\le T}w_t\eta_t\frac{A-1}{4A}
       +\frac{\sqrt{B_T}}{\gamma_a}.
```

This is a bound on the ideal fractional mixture loss, including whatever
task and action-execution costs the announced affine rows actually encode.
It does not yet include forecast/selection overhead outside those rows, nor
does it describe every sampled execution of the mixture. Feedback and those
two extensions are handled separately below.

## 2. Projection, first-order inequality and the sharp constant

The objective is strictly convex because `eta>0`, so its minimizer is unique.
Completing the square identifies it as the Euclidean projection of
`-c/eta` onto the simplex. Equivalently, there is a threshold `tau` such that

```math
q_i=\max\{0,(\tau-c_i)/\eta\},\qquad \sum_iq_i=1.
```

For active coordinates, `c_i+eta q_i=tau`; for inactive coordinates,
`c_i>=tau`. These conditions also give the first-order inequality against
the simplex vertex `e_a`:

```math
(c+\eta q)\cdot(e_a-q)\ge0,
\qquad
q\cdot c-c_a\le\eta(q_a-\|q\|^2).
```

For `A>1`, put `x=q_a`. The other coordinates sum to `1-x`, so

```math
q_a-\|q\|^2
 \le x-x^2-\frac{(1-x)^2}{A-1}
 =\frac{A-1}{4A}
   -\frac{A}{A-1}\left(x-\frac{A+1}{2A}\right)^2.
```

Therefore

```math
q\cdot c-c_a
 \le\eta(q_a-\|q\|^2)
 \le\eta\frac{A-1}{4A}.
```

For `A=1`, the gain and the stated constant are both zero.

The constant is sharp. Take `c_a=0`, `c_b=eta/2` for all `b!=a`. The
projection has

```math
q_a=\frac{A+1}{2A},\qquad q_b=\frac1{2A}\ (b\ne a),
```

and both inequalities are equalities. These may be constant loss rows with
all slopes zero. Thus the smoothing gap can persist even when all action
residuals vanish and the potential contributes zero. For `A=2`, the result
reduces exactly to the completed P3-06 `eta/8` smoothing constant. This does
not assert that every term in a later combined regret bound is jointly sharp.

## 3. Continuity, exact rational evaluation and finite root work

The simplex projection is continuous and nonexpansive. For completeness,
apply its two first-order inequalities at inputs `u,v` to their projections
`q,r` and add: `||q-r||^2 <= (q-r) dot (u-v)`. Cauchy then gives
`||q-r||<=||u-v||`. Since `u(p)=-c(p)/eta` is affine, `q(p)` is continuous.

For a support `S` of size `m`,

```math
\tau(p)=\frac{\eta+\sum_{i\in S}c_i(p)}m,
\qquad q_i(p)=\frac{\tau(p)-c_i(p)}\eta\ (i\in S),
```

with zero elsewhere. Consequently `q(p)` is piecewise affine with rational
coefficients for rational rows and `eta`. Its breakpoints are rational where
nonconstant affine support inequalities become equalities. At a rational
`p`, sorting the costs and finding the active threshold gives an exact
rational answer. Tied costs do not make the minimizer ambiguous. Sorting,
division, comparisons and bit growth still have costs; a finite arithmetic
formula alone supplies no input-independent runtime bound.

The mean slope has an especially useful derivative on each support piece:

```math
\bar d'(p)
 =-\frac1\eta\sum_{i\in S}(d_i-\bar d_S)^2\le0,
\qquad \bar d_S=\frac1{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}d_i.
```

If `D=max_i d_i-min_i d_i`, its magnitude is at most
`A D^2/(4 eta)`. Continuity across pieces gives the same global Lipschitz
bound. Each decision coordinate therefore has valid magnitude and
Lipschitz bounds

```math
M_a=\gamma_a wD,\qquad
L_a=\gamma_a w\frac{AD^2}{4\eta}.
```

The inherited score bound can use these values. In particular, continuous
features make the P3-06 score continuous. Its endpoint rules remain valid:
at `p=0`, `S(0)<=0` ensures `2(y-p)S(p)<=0` for either binary answer; at
`p=1`, `S(1)>=0` does the same. Otherwise `S(0)>0>S(1)` gives a root by
continuity. The endpoint mixture need not be a pure action. Rational
sign-preserving bisection need not find an exactly rational root; a capped
implementation records its actual nonnegative allowance as in P3-06.

Smaller `eta` shrinks the smoothing term but increases these Lipschitz
constants and potentially the charged root-search work. It does not by
itself shrink all-in computational cost.

### 3.1 A useful action-block magnitude bound

For fixed positive scales, the action block satisfies the sharper bound

```math
\sum_a\gamma_a^2w^2(\bar d-d_a)^2
 \le w^2D^2\left(\sum_a\gamma_a^2-\min_a\gamma_a^2\right).
```

To see this, hold the slopes fixed. The left side as a function of `dbar`
is convex on `[min d,max d]`, so its maximum occurs at an endpoint. At that
endpoint one action's difference is zero; all others have magnitude at most
`D`. This yields the bound. It is sharp over slopes and mixtures by putting
all mixture mass on an extreme-slope action with minimum scale and all other
slopes at the opposite extreme. With common `gamma`, it becomes
`(A-1)gamma^2 w^2 D^2`, reducing to the P3-06 two-action bound when `A=2`.

The resulting variance contribution has another factor at most `1/4` from
`p(1-p)`. Other feature blocks and actual numerical allowances still belong
in `B_T`. A normalized sublinear conclusion needs both
`sum w_t eta_t=o(sum w_t)` and `sqrt(B_T)=o(sum w_t)`; bounded dimensions
alone establish neither condition under arbitrary stakes or allowances.

## 4. The residual identity gives fixed-action regret

Because the issued `q_t` is held fixed when comparing the two binary outcomes,

```math
\bar c_t(y_t)-c_{t,a}(y_t)
 =\bar c_t(p_t)-c_{t,a}(p_t)
  +(y_t-p_t)(\bar d_t(p_t)-d_{t,a}).
```

The residual coordinate is exactly

```math
R_{T,a}=\gamma_a\sum_{t\le T}
 w_t(y_t-p_t)(\bar d_t(p_t)-d_{t,a}).
```

Summing the identity, applying the sharp smoothing inequality round by round,
and using `R_T,a<=sqrt(B_T)` proves the theorem in §1. This imports the
P3-06 potential guarantee rather than reproving that completed result.
Varying `gamma_a` between rounds would break the displayed unweighted
coordinate identity unless a new weighted comparison were explicitly derived.

All fixed action identities are covered simultaneously. Thus the best fixed
identity may be designated retrospectively. The result does not compare
with an action selected using the outcome separately on each round, nor with
every adaptive compute-then-act policy. With changing tables it is regret
on the announced sequence of rows and contexts. If a counterfactual always-buy
policy would produce different future caches, queries or information, that
full policy comparison needs a separate trajectory/accounting contract.

## 5. What BUY_EXACT actually assumes

A valid specialization has guess-0 and guess-1 rows, a stated fallback row,
and a BUY_EXACT action that, at a known charged cost `k_t`, is guaranteed to
produce a checked resolution and then the prescribed correct action. With
zero task loss for a correct action and an outcome-independent tariff,

```math
c_{t,\mathrm{BUY}}(p)=k_t,
```

so its slope is zero. Any checking, acquisition and completion charges needed
to make this guarantee true belong in `k_t`. With known outcome-dependent
terminal losses, the two known endpoint totals can instead give an affine
row. The guarantee is structural; it does not require a free uncertain
success-probability oracle.

An unknown partial-success computation generally has additional outcomes,
failure costs and dependence on the current baseline's errors. Those cannot
be inferred from the scalar truth forecast alone. Known conditional expected
costs could support a further expected-cost construction, but their
identification, uncertainty and actual-execution noise would be additional
premises. The present pathwise binary-row theorem does not supply them.

A known upper bound on solver cost is also different from an actual flat
tariff. Replacing an action's actual cost by its upper bound can give a sound
surrogate loss but change the comparator. For example, constant action rows
`(2,10)` with `eta=1` select the first action purely, while actual costs
`(2,1)` make its regret to BUY equal to one. The surrogate smoothing bound
is `1/8` with zero slope residual. It cannot be claimed as an actual-cost
regret bound against the cheaper real buyer without adding the discrepancy.

All charged work outside action rows remains outside the theorem until it
is added explicitly: feature construction, forecasting, projection,
root search, model/profile acquisition, input admission, storage, rounding,
random bits, final selection and verification. For an all-in comparator `a`,
add the controller's corresponding overhead minus that comparator's overhead.
If that difference grows linearly, a sublinear action-row regret statement
does not imply sublinear all-in regret. The same hard resource constraints
apply even when a monetary price is zero.

## 6. Deterministic answers and missing feedback

The inherited potential result needs no IID truth law or accurate expert.
It applies to arbitrary deterministic mathematical answers once the correct
version-matched labels are actually admitted through the required feedback
process. It does not let the learner update on an unresolved answer.

If every query receives admitted full feedback before the next forecast,
the single-copy theorem applies directly. With delayed labels, the inherited
idle-copy wrapper gives a bound over settled subsequences with the copy sum
`sum_k sqrt(B_k)` in place of `sqrt(B_T)`. Its actual copy and pending-label
costs remain relevant. Action-dependent resolution is not automatically a
full-feedback learning protocol.

For a concrete boundary, use guess rows `(p,1-p)`, fallback `1/10`, BUY
`1/5`, and `eta=1/20`. Every fresh zero-state action-feature forecaster issues
`p=1/2` and chooses fallback purely. If only buying supplies feedback, all
these queries remain unresolved. On eight evaluator-later queries whose
answers are all one, total task loss is `4/5` and fixed guess-1 loses zero.
The settled potential has no observations and says nothing about that
whole-cohort regret. Improperly attaching the all-issued smoothing term to
zero settled potential would give only `3/40`, contradicting the actual
`4/5`. This is a feedback-scope witness, not a hardness claim about a query
generator or a failure of the properly scoped P3-06 theorem.

Resolving all previously unbought queries for training can restore a feedback
premise, but that computation and checking must be paid. Permanent censoring
instead leaves an explicitly limited settled-population statement. A bandit
or importance-weighted update would require a separate theorem; it is not
obtained by hiding the missing rows.

## 7. Bounded dyadic rounding: exact total-variation constant

An exact rational ideal mixture is not automatically executable with a finite
worst-case fair-bit budget. For example, three equal costs give probability
`1/3` to each action. Every output probability of a sampler using at most `b`
fair bits is an integer multiple of `2^{-b}`, so this mixture cannot be
sampled exactly by that primitive at any finite worst-case cap. A separately
declared finite categorical primitive is another possible contract.

To use exactly `b` fair bits, put `D=2^b` and round the **execution mixture**.
Let

```math
n_i=\lfloor Dq_i\rfloor,\quad f_i=Dq_i-n_i,\quad
R=D-\sum_i n_i=\sum_i f_i.
```

`R` is an integer with `0<=R<=min(A-1,D)`. Increment the `R` largest
fractional parts, with a fixed tie rule, and divide the resulting counts by
`D` to obtain `nu`. Then

```math
\operatorname{TV}(\nu,q)
 =\frac{R-\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Top}_R}f_i}{D}
 \le\frac{R(A-R)}{AD}.
```

The identity sums positive rounding errors. The inequality uses that the
average of the `R` largest fractions is at least their common overall
average `R/A`. Equality holds when all fractions equal `R/A`.

The root's proposed universal bound is therefore valid:

```math
\operatorname{TV}(\nu,q)\le\frac{\lfloor A^2/4\rfloor}{AD}.
```

There is a sharpness qualification at small `D`. The exact worst case for
fixed `A,D` is

```math
\kappa(A,D)=\max_{0\le R\le\min(A-1,D)}\frac{R(A-R)}{AD}
 =\frac{r_*(A-r_*)}{AD},\qquad
r_*=\min(D,\lfloor A/2\rfloor).
```

It equals the proposed constant when `D>=floor(A/2)`; otherwise it equals
`1-D/A`. Every feasible `R` attains its stated bound: choose nonnegative
integer floors summing to `D-R` and set all fractional parts to `R/A`.
For example, `A=4,D=1` has exact worst-case TV `3/4`, while the more general
formula gives the valid but unattainable bound one.

Sampling a uniform integer in `[0,D-1]` with exactly `b` fair bits and using
the cumulative integer counts implements `nu` exactly. Count the rounding,
sorting, bit acquisition and selection work. A zero-bit grid `D=1` simply
executes its deterministic rounded action.

### 7.1 Keep the continuous ideal mixture in the forecasting features

The rounded map generally jumps at rounding boundaries. It must not silently
replace `q` inside the continuous-feature argument. A concrete score witness
uses two rows `c_0(p)=p`, `c_1(p)=1-p`, `eta=1/2`, `D=1` and residual
`R=(1,1)`, with unit scales and weight. Rounding chooses action 0 for
`p<=1/2` and action 1 above it. The rounded-feature score is

```math
S(p)=\begin{cases}4-4p,&p\le1/2,\\-4p,&p>1/2.\end{cases}
```

Thus `S(0)>0>S(1)` but no root exists: the score jumps from two to minus
two. The algebraic allowance formula can still record the resulting error,
but the intermediate-value and arbitrarily small root-tolerance argument
does not apply. Retaining ideal `q` in the features avoids this problem.

For any actual binary outcome, let `Cbar_t` bound the action cost range
`max_a c_t,a(y)-min_a c_t,a(y)`. The standard zero-sum difference calculation
gives

```math
\nu_t\cdot c_t(y)-q_t\cdot c_t(y)
 \le\bar C_t\operatorname{TV}(\nu_t,q_t)
 \le\bar C_t\kappa(A,D_t).
```

Therefore executing the rounded mixture adds at most
`sum_t w_t Cbar_t kappa(A,D_t)` to the ideal mixed-loss bound. This deterministic
allowance is separate from both smoothing and random sampling noise.

## 8. Actual sampled actions and the selected-feedback trap

Suppose the current action sample has conditional distribution `nu_t`, with
fresh fair bits independent of the current mathematical answer and other
current outcome-generating mechanisms. Prices, rows, weights, the mixture,
and the query are fixed before this current sample. Define

```math
Z_t=w_t[c_{t,A_t}(y_t)-\nu_t\cdot c_t(y_t)].
```

Then `E[Z_t | the pre-sample history]=0`, using the fixed/nonanticipating
mathematical outcome. If deterministic announced caps `r_t` satisfy
`w_t Cbar_t<=r_t` on every allowed history, bounded conditional exponential
moments give, at a fixed finite horizon, with probability at least `1-delta`,

```math
\sum_t Z_t\le
 \sqrt{\frac12\log(1/\delta)\sum_t r_t^2}.
```

This follows by multiplying the conditional bounds
`E[exp(s Z_t)|past]<=exp(s^2 r_t^2/8)` and minimizing the resulting
exponential tail. An arbitrary realized random variance sum cannot simply
replace the deterministic cap without a justified self-normalized result.

On a horizon for which the potential statement applies, every fixed action
simultaneously satisfies the realized sampled-cost bound

```math
\sum_t w_t[c_{t,A_t}(y_t)-c_{t,a}(y_t)]
 \le\sum_t w_t\eta_t\frac{A-1}{4A}
   +\frac{\sqrt{B_T}}{\gamma_a}
   +\sum_t w_t\bar C_t\kappa(A,D_t)
   +\sqrt{\frac12\log(1/\delta)\sum_t r_t^2}.
```

Add any all-in overhead difference described in §5. There is no additional
union bound over action identities: the sampling-noise sum is the same for
every comparator. The pathwise mixed bound is applied to the actual issued
trace, while the final term controls its common random-action deviation.

**Critical selected-feedback caveat.** If the analyzed subset consists only
of observations settled by the sampled action, its sampling noise is not
generally centered. Take constant costs fallback `0`, BUY `1`, and `eta=2`.
The ideal and exact dyadic mixture is `(3/4,1/4)`, with mean cost `1/4`.
Suppose only BUY produces an admitted label. On every settled row the actual
cost is one, so its sampling difference is `3/4`. Per issued row,

```math
E[Z_t]=0,\qquad
E[1\{\text{settled}\}Z_t]=\frac14\frac34=\frac3{16}>0.
```

The settled-only sum is therefore not the martingale sum above. This remains
true even though the current random bits are independent of mathematical
truth. Valid options include a full issued horizon with the required actual
feedback guarantee, an exogenously/predictably selected scoring cohort under
proved conditions, or a separate selection correction. The inherited
settled-subsequence potential by itself does not fix this extra sampling
selection problem.
