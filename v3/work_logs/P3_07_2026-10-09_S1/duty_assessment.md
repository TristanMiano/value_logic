## Nontrivial positive, negative and null findings

The positive paired result is conditional continuation value with explicit
coverage and acquisition. In the saved high-stakes profile, full computation
has a positive lower expected gain, while its setup bill defeats the short
64-query amortization. The whole-procedure audit includes rebuilding that
profile inside each episode and still gives a positive expected improvement
over fallback. This is a bounded instance of assessing a reasoner's own
procedure, without assuming every inner interval is correct.

The ordinary controls are decisive for interpretation. At high and asymmetric
stakes, exact arithmetic is cheaper than the audited controller; the analytic
class profile further reduces procurement from 1,692,957 sampled units to
64,581. The sampled controller's asymmetric decisions are safe by their lower
bounds but not exact-catalogue optimal. This separates safety relative to a
specified baseline from best-action selection.

More data produce one actual policy change, under expensive storage; the
other five price cases have unchanged selected roles. Those unchanged rows
remain reported. Same proper-score accuracy gives different action value in
the explicitly supplied signal experiments. The wider initial forecasts are
the same across repricing cases, so a change in task stakes alone can change
which computation is worthwhile without improving forecast accuracy.

The fixed-size acquisition witness needs 45 observations for symmetric
three-quarter sign identification, beyond the 128-request maximum payoff.
The new tent-envelope proof shows that no adaptive pure-information stopping
rule helps at that horizon under the supplied two-law model. A longer horizon
permits positive prior expected value, but retains negative conditional value
under the bad law. These findings distinguish three different duties: a valid
sign certificate, safe abstention, and Bayesian expected-value control.

The four-action extension proves a conditional mathematical bridge but its
actual full-feedback resource account loses to always buying the exact service.
The dependency/repair example also reverses a check-only apparent gain. These
are explanations supported by exact costs and stated models, rather than
unexplained null results.
