## Question and construction

Can a small wrapper bind a named action's regret query to the complete
supplied catalogue, original loss expressions and versions, common unit,
selected action, nonnegative tolerance and current source? Can the existing
finite kernel refine this task certificate while its truth coordinate and
individual cost levels remain unresolved?

The wrapper will compile the maximum of local positive differences
`resid(selected_loss, other_loss)` over every other supplied action. A
one-action catalogue compiles to zero. Here the local kernel convention is
`resid(f,g)=max(0,f-g)`; translating to phase-two native `res` reverses the
arguments. Boolean kernel coordinates are answer indicators, not native
zero-for-true formula-loss values.

Compilation, validation, byte comparisons, hashing and threshold comparison
are wrapper boundary work outside the core transaction allowance. The request
and compiled expression must fit the finite core's admission caps. The wrapper
will reject changed action/catalogue/compiled-expression data before certifying.
Source revisions use the kernel's source identity; changed objectives require
a newly validated wrapper request. Identity checking will explicitly not
authenticate arbitrary fabricated JSON.

## Planned fixed inputs and checks

1. One opaque Boolean coordinate, A=`10x`, B=`10x+1`, selected A, tolerance
   zero: initial regret upper endpoint nine, endpoint zero after splitting;
   both marginal loss ranges and the truth remain unresolved.
2. Replace B by `11-10x`: preserve the two marginal ranges but obtain regret
   nine for A on the fully processed source, preventing its zero-tolerance
   certificate.
3. A=`x`, B=`1-x`: neither selected pure action obtains a tolerance below one
   on the full Boolean source.
4. Initially assume `not x` in the preceding case: certify A conditionally,
   withdraw the premise, invalidate the old certificate and reopen its bound.
5. Reject mixed units, absent selected actions, altered catalogue/selection,
   and modified reserved compiled losses. Old certificates must not transfer
   to another catalogue or selected action. Changed cost/objective expressions
   require revalidation; a fresh repriced request must recompute its bound.
6. A one-action catalogue produces zero regret. A detected finite conflict
   produces no certificate. Reject a compiled expression that exceeds the
   core expression cap and reject a negative tolerance.

The dedicated script will save its full deterministic input catalogue and
method/wrapper/evaluator/plan hashes in a fresh
`task_certificate_attempt_1` directory before importing the wrapper or running
the first case. Expected small-case values will also be checked with a
separate exact scalar evaluator over both Boolean assignments. Those complete
tables are evaluator-only; they are not wrapper or kernel input.
