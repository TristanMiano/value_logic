## What the research establishes

complete policies on a fixed finite modular-query service. A simultaneous
coordinate event supports signed constant repricing and selection of a lower
expected-gain policy. It handles common sunk costs correctly: paying more to
acquire a profile cannot rationally change the continuation comparison, but
that earlier payment remains part of total performance. Fixed policy behavior,
bounded features, request-law assumptions, hard budgets and source/generator
bindings remain explicit.

The price of additional uncertainty reduction is substantive. In the saved
storage-expensive case the controller remains at fallback for sample counts
128, 256 and 512, then changes its decision at 1024. The high-stakes case has
a positive lower continuation gain at that checkpoint but fails to repay
sample acquisition over only 64 requests. Constant repricing is supported by
the same acquired coordinates; arbitrary new features, new behavior or
unjustified distribution change are separate obligations.

Two bounded audits assess the reasoner's own named procedure. One starts with
the already acquired profile. The other rebuilds a profile inside each fresh
episode and prices that construction, with a separate outer audit bill. The
whole procedure's lower gain against fallback is positive, while charged
exact computation remains much cheaper. This is finite program assessment,
not an unrestricted claim of self-knowledge or recursive trust.

The acquisition companion separates reliable sign identification from prior
expected value. Its two nearby laws require 45 fixed observations for the
declared symmetric identification target. An exact tent-envelope proof bounds
the expected terminal gain of one observation by H/400. At H=128, the observation

The forecast companion supplies sharp quadratic smoothing and finite-bit
