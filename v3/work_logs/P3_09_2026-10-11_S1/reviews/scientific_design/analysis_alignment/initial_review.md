## Judgment

One concrete action-effect calculation is wrong for its intended interpretation: the generic asymmetric-stakes pairing subtracts losses measured under different stake vectors. Several quantitative protocol promises also lack their corresponding computed endpoint or eligibility predicate. These should be resolved prospectively before freezing the analyzer. They do not require new policy experiments.

Their order is not automatically an order on every primitive tariff category.

This review binds the initial captured sources. Parent-owned repairs after the capture require their own additive readback; the findings below are not allegations about unseen later versions.

## 1. Asymmetric stakes: a concrete false improvement

Protocol section 3 defines action loss using the current false-positive and false-negative stakes, normalized by their maximum. Its A3 question asks whether the known-cost readout helps decisions. In `broker_analysis`, the `stakes_reference` relation uses the same generic field subtraction as other pairings: the reference's already normalized `action_loss_mean` minus the current record's mean. Those two records have different stake vectors and can have different normalizers.

The first synthetic observation has two positions and exactly the same actions in both records: one false positive and no false negatives. Both bills are 100 units. Under symmetric stakes `(1,1)`, normalized loss is `1/2`. Under asymmetric stakes `(1/2,3/2)`, it is `1/6`. The analyzer reports a positive difference of `1/3`, while its action-path equality is true. There has been no decision improvement. Both action paths evaluated under the current asymmetric stakes have loss `1/6`, so the valid paired action difference is zero. Because costs also agree, the valid priced-objective difference is zero at every common price. This is an arithmetic interpretation defect, not a statement about any final policy outcome. [Probe evidence](initial_probe_results.json)

The existing score schema already supplies `false_positives`, `false_negatives` and `horizon`. The repair is to evaluate both action paths with the **same chosen current stakes and normalizer**, retain the original self-stakes means under clearly descriptive names, and calculate the priced difference with one common normalized price. Exact complete horizons and the same public request set are required. A changed acquisition history remains an actual paired policy comparison; it must not be described as a pure final-action readout intervention. The `probability_cost` coupling can support that narrower intervention only when its forecast/acquisition signatures agree.

The analyzer's whole-configuration catalogue groups rows by the complete stakes vector and uses each controller's normalized native price. That part does not have this cross-stakes subtraction defect. The error is in the `stakes_reference` paired endpoint. [Analyzer: `broker_analysis`](initial_source/v3/experiments/p309_analyze.py), [adapter: `_metrics`, `score`](initial_source/v3/experiments/p309_broker.py)

## 4. Resource categories and economic scope

The certificate adapter reports `producer=total-consumer` and `consumer`, which form a partition. It also reports output bytes, retained serialized byte-periods and source procurement as **overlapping diagnostics**. The score says this explicitly in `category_convention` and retains the disjoint original tariff counts separately as `tariff_category_totals`. The analyzer aggregates only the five-coordinate `category_totals` and labels their order `componentwise_resource_dominance` without carrying that convention into the aggregate. [Certificate `_invoice_totals`](initial_source/v3/experiments/p309_certificates.py), [certificate analysis](initial_source/v3/experiments/p309_analyze.py)

The observer example makes the limit concrete. One record uses 90 consumer native-call units; another uses 100 consumer opcode units, with every other reported stage/diagnostic coordinate zero. The analyzer correctly marks the first record below the second in its reported five-coordinate vector. Yet it has 90 native-call units against zero, and a primitive repricing that doubles native calls makes its cost 180 against 100. Thus this predicate does not establish dominance at all nonnegative prices of the primitive tariff categories. The example demonstrates a field-interpretation limit; it is not a counterexample to componentwise ordering on the explicitly supplied five coordinates. [Probe](initial_probe_results.json)

Retain the aggregate convention, do not sum the five overlapping fields as a bill, and use a separately named raw-tariff predicate if making a claim about all primitive prices. Missing raw categories should be compared as zero, and a missing/incomplete invoice must prevent a complete vector claim. Consumer-only and total-unit gains remain different claims. No exchange rate joins the CNF tariff to the certificate tariff.
