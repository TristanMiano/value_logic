## Shared positive affine calibration does not rescue the robust values

The initial sum-to-four argument uses known loss units: the two specialized
loss rows sum to the constant four row. Their exact expected losses must sum
to four, while their robust maxima sum to eight.

For all four admitted calibration conventions, a stronger sign argument
handles the shared nuisance parameters explicitly. Let the observed vector
be `v = s L p + b 1`, with one normalized law p, shared s positive and shared
b unrestricted. Known units are the canonical subcase s=1, b=0; a known
component is fixed to its canonical value in the corresponding partial mode.
The fallback row is the constant three row. Therefore

\[
v_{\mathrm{fallback}}
-\frac{v_{\mathrm{take\_p}}+v_{\mathrm{take\_r}}}{2}
=(3s+b)-(2s+b)=s>0.
\]

The recorded robust vector `(4,4,3)` instead has this contrast equal to −1.
It cannot be an expected-loss observation vector for these same rows under
any of the four shared positive calibration conventions. The conclusion
does not depend on choosing a particular p. The positivity of s matters;
unshared row calibrations would be a different, unsupported observation map.

Q5's recovery theorem applies only when the exact shared observation premises
hold **and** the requested target meets the theorem's appropriate fiber/rank
criterion. Some valid expected-loss maps identify only selected targets or
no nonconstant targets. The theorem is not a claim that arbitrary alternative
encodings cannot carry probability information; it characterizes the admitted
expected-loss service. The robust example lies outside that service.

## General repair ranks and the deployed incumbent format

ordered vector. Sourcewise transformation by a positive scalar a_s and a
same-dimension constant vector b_s preserves every lexicographic comparison:
the first differing coordinate retains its sign. It therefore preserves that
source's complete minimizing set, including all ties. The numerical rank-one
and rank-two example in the initial note is the scalar special case.

The separately deployed current-incumbent receiver has one scalar rank tier.
Its sublevel invariance `r(x)<=r(w)` under positive affine transformation is
conditional on a **checked feasible incumbent w**, the same feasible domain,
and an admitted transformed encoding. The scalar deployed grammar is not
silently expanded to the full vector semantics. Changed bytes, arithmetic
