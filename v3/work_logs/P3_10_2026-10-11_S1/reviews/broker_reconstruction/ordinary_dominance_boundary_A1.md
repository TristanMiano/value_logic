## Claim that the saved records support

For each symmetric family, one distinct ordinary configuration has strictly smaller **realized normalized action loss W** and strictly smaller **mean complete terminal cold bill per scheduled request C/T** than each of the four named candidates. Consequently, for those already executed paths,

\[
J_c(\lambda)-J_o(\lambda)
= (W_c-W_o)+\lambda\bigl(\overline{C_c/T}-\overline{C_o/T}\bigr)>0
\quad\text{for every }\lambda\geq0.
\]

This is a comparison of the frozen **terminal scalar objective**. The three witnesses do not dominate the candidate forecasts or deliver the same optional report service. In fact, all three have **worse pre-purchase Brier means** than their family's candidate forecasts. ## Zero-price ties and the practical threshold

At λ=0, each of the twelve candidate-versus-witness comparisons remains **strict** because a>0; there is no such tie. Strict is not synonymous with the frozen 0.01 practical cutoff: the gate-uniform action gap **7/768 ≈ 0.0091145833** is below that cutoff at zero price. The other listed a coefficients exceed 0.01. This does not authorize positive-price economic claims while the global cost gate is false. [1]

Zero-price **catalogue** ties are separate. On mixed and gate, the zero-loss minimum is attained by the four exact solver/cache configurations and the two zero-price combined controllers. On cheap-null, those six plus both bounded DPLL fallbacks tie at zero in the native catalogue; the fixed-record catalogue additionally includes the hashed and linear combined paths originally run at raw price 1/10000, giving ten ties. The mixed and gate witnesses above need not themselves be zero-price minima. All these exact ties are retained in the existing 54-row table. [1–3]

Within the candidates, each cold/cached pair has the same forecasts and actions, so they **tie in J at λ=0**. At λ>0 the cached path is worse, since all six aggregate cached/cold bills—and all 36 individual paired bills—are strictly larger. The executed uniform ordinary mirror agrees exactly with the cached uniform kernel in forecast/action paths, complete bills, report endpoints and report fees, so that identity tie persists for **every λ**. The other declared ordinary-identical kernel interpretations likewise preclude strict exclusive-kernel superiority, but are unnecessary for the distinct-witness inequalities above. [1]

## What the accounting includes

The frozen terminal cost is exactly **common source invoice + local episode invoice**. The local account includes the declared advice, computation, acquisition, receipt/check/cache, action, output and retention operations. The common source fee is **66,076 units per actual broker execution**, present in all 336 reconstructed records. It is already included in C, rather than removed from the ordinary or candidate bill. Within a family, the common source/T term cancels in the difference b. The account is the declared word tariff; supplier generation, orchestration/import/JSON audit encoding, private scoring and analysis are declared outside it. It is not physical runtime or a universal deployment-cost model. [1–2]

Optional reporting has its **own additional source and execution invoice** and is explicitly excluded from the frozen terminal J. The three ordinary witnesses request no optional report. Both candidate providers request reports with the following positive equal fees per T; their cached/cold mathematical reports and fee means agree. [1–2]

## Forecast boundary and reviewer conclusion

The ordinary witnesses' pre-purchase Brier means are **1/4** on mixed and cheap-null, and **149978993387/549755813888 ≈ 0.2728101997** on gate. Candidate uniform/ticket issued Brier means are respectively **0.1499566628/0.1836569774**, **0.0781949530/0.1459710597**, and **0.2613602713/0.2306965507**. Thus every one of the twelve terminal comparisons reverses direction on this forecast metric: the candidate has better Brier than its selected witness. A solver can produce a correct paid terminal action after an uninformative pre-purchase forecast; that action does not retroactively improve the recorded forecast. [1–2]
