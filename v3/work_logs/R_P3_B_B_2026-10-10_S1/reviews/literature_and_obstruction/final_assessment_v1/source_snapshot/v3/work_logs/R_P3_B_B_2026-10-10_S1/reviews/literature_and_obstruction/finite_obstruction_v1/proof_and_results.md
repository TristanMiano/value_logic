## 2. Exact inherited tree theorem and its hypotheses

Fix $`n\ge2`$ Boolean coordinates, no hard constraints, no soft rank rows, the all-zero feasible incumbent, rank cutoff zero, and requested upper bound zero. Define XOR by the inherited grammar as $`u\oplus v=\neg\mathrm{eq}(u,v)`$. Let $`P_n`$ be the forward left fold and $`Q_n`$ the reverse left fold over all coordinates, with

```math
D_n=P_n-Q_n.
```

The true difference is identically zero. Nevertheless, under the concrete inherited local interval and feature-collection rules, every `BandProof` and every `PortfolioProof` with `choices=()` must have exactly

```math
2^n\ \mathrm{leaves},\qquad 2^n-1\ \mathrm{splits},\qquad
2^{n+1}-1\ \mathrm{visited\ nodes}.
```

These are unfolded tree size and receiver-traversal counts. The declared nested tree record has no reference instruction and serializes those occurrences separately. **The theorem does not lower-bound the number of distinct Python tuple objects:** a caller can alias child tuples, but the receiver still visits both occurrences with their respective cells and the nested serialization still expands them. It is not a lower bound on general proof systems or shared DAG evidence.

The source hypotheses are load-bearing: this exact XOR syntax with two distinct fold expressions; the accepted `collect`/`form` and `interval` rules; no hard or soft premises; zero cutoff and bound; exhaustive single-bit splits; and no available reuse choice. Additional hard/rank context, a larger bound, useful admitted history, rewritten identical syntax, a different proof calculus or a new shared evidence language can change the result.

### Proper-box induction

On a complete assignment, either parity fold receives its exact Boolean value. On any box leaving at least one of its coordinates free, a fold receives interval $`[0,1]`$. This follows inductively because an equality of an uncertain Boolean interval and another uncertain interval or Boolean singleton receives $`[0,1]`$, and Boolean negation preserves that interval. Every fold contains all coordinates.

The `BandProof` collector treats the two outer negated equality expressions as distinct opaque atoms for $`n\ge2`$. Their difference is enclosed by $`[-1,1]`$ on every proper box. Thus a zero-bound loss leaf cannot apply. A hard exclusion has no hard row, and a rank exclusion requires the false strict inequality $`0>0`$. Only a split remains.

For the stronger fresh portfolio system, `form` expands Boolean negation. Writing the two distinct outer equality features as $`E_P,E_Q`$, it collects the target as $`E_Q-E_P`$. With no hard or soft rows, `context_rows` supplies only the zero rank row and pairs $`x_i-b_i`$, $`b_i-x_i`$ for assigned coordinates. Arbitrary admissible nonnegative multipliers cannot change either opaque equality coefficient. Every linear combination of the assigned-bit rows has interval exactly zero on its box. Consequently `conditional_upper` remains exactly one on every proper box, for **all valid multiplier tuples**, not just those found by a producer heuristic.

All accepted leaves must therefore be singleton boxes. Exhaustive splitting gives the complete partition into the $`2^n`$ assignments. A full finite binary tree with that many leaves has the claimed split/node counts. Conversely, all singleton loss/direct leaves are valid, so the lower bound is attained. The proper-box argument covers every valid split order; the executed producers used their declared ascending order.

The finite code retains its actual caps: at most ten bits, 2,047 visited proof nodes, inherited expression depth/node restrictions, row/rational restrictions and the separate ADD/wire caps. The asymptotic theorem concerns the **uncapped mathematical rule schema**, retaining the same inference semantics but not the finite admission limits. No cap-lifted CPython implementation was created or executed. The diagnostic stops at the prospectively selected dimensions 2 through 8; it does not extrapolate by running an enlarged source.

## 4. Polynomial size of the actual recorded evidence schema

The stronger constructive statement is valid with explicit hypotheses. For this parity family, under one fixed complete variable order and the unchanged memoized Apply/cache-insertion recording rules, a full fresh certificate has $`O(n^2)`$ node facts, $`O(n^2)`$ Apply facts, $`O(n)`$ expression bindings, and $`O(n^2\log n)`$ encoded bytes. This is a proof-representation bound for the uncapped rule schema, including the actual construction trace, not just the final zero root.

To reconstruct it, consider a fold prefix of $`j`$ distinct coordinates. Its reduced parity diagram has $`2j+1`$ nodes. The next bit diagram has three nodes. The ordinary `Apply(eq, prefix, bit)` visits at most the Cartesian product of the two input node sets, hence at most $`3(2j+1)`$ distinct node-pair facts for that call. Its Boolean equality result is the complemented parity of $`j+1`$ coordinates and has $`2j+3`$ nodes. Applying subtraction from the constant one to negate it uses at most $`2j+3`$ node pairs. This reasoning holds for any fixed variable order, because cofactors are existing nodes of the two input diagrams.

Thus each new XOR contributes at most $`8j+6`$ Apply facts before accounting for cache reuse, which can only reduce the count. Summing both forward and reverse folds over $`j=1,\ldots,n-1`$ is quadratic. Scaling the final reverse parity by minus one is linear in its diagram size. The final addition is bounded by the product of two linear-size diagrams, hence quadratic. The no-hard/no-soft current-goal construction adds only a constant number of Apply calls. For example, $`12n^2+10n+10`$ is a conservative quadratic upper bound from these separate-call bounds; no claim that this constant is optimal is needed.

Every new Apply fact creates at most one new result node, while input bit construction adds at most $`n`$ decision nodes and the fixed terminals. Therefore the total constructed and recorded node table is also quadratic. Memoized cache insertions, rather than every recursive call or a traversal of the final root, are the recorder's admitted proof facts. No uncharged algebraic identity is substituted for those facts in this argument.

There are $`n`$ bit-expression bindings, two equality/negation constructors per added coordinate in each fold, and the final scaling and addition. The concrete family therefore has $`5n-2`$ expression bindings for $`n\ge2`$, consistent with every executed row. Full prefix expression keys are repeated in the wire: a prefix key has $`O(j\log n)`$ encoded length, so summing them costs $`O(n^2\log n)`$ bytes. Node variables and node IDs take $`O(\log n)`$ bytes in ordinary positional encoding because there are only quadratically many constructed nodes. Each node/Apply row has a fixed number of those fields. The terminal values remain in the fixed set $`\{-1,0,1\}`$, so arbitrary growing rational encodings do not enter this family.

The complete current frame, all-zero witness and fixed order add only $`O(n\log n)`$ bytes for the declared sparse metadata. The complete source context is a fixed record, or contributes its explicitly added byte length if changed. **The bound requires that metadata/source payloads do not themselves contain arbitrary larger dimension-dependent data.** A fixed number of duplicated cache/log tables in retained serialization changes constants, not the stated order, for this single fresh proof.

This argument concerns finite proof facts and their encoding. It is not a general compilation-time theorem, a bound on native heap use, a validation of every Python dictionary/parser/serialization cost, or a promise that the deployed finite-cap service accepts arbitrary $`n`$. On unrelated inputs, ADDs and intermediate Apply construction may be exponential. A small final root alone would not establish any of the construction or evidence bounds proved here.

### The explicit five-node receiving proof

With choices ordered left then right, identity replacement maps, positive scale one, and old bound/cutoff zero, the exact tree is:

```python

    ('reuse', 0, ((),), (), ()),
    ('split', 1,
        ('hard', 0),
        ('reuse', 1, ((),), (), ())))
```

At $`p=0`$, the left old hard goal has upper bound zero with empty multipliers. At $`q=1`$, the right old hard goal does too. The mapped old ranks are zero. Identity substitution makes the receiving difference correction exactly zero in edits 0–4 and exactly one half in edit 5, meeting the corresponding requested-bound allowance. At $`p=1,q=0`$, the current hard formula at index zero is identically false, independent of all parity coordinates. The unchanged portfolio receiver therefore checks two reuse leaves, one hard exclusion and two splits, exactly five nodes, for every edit.

### What the necessity claim means

Each old domain is necessary for covering the whole current sublevel **by these two fixed identity-mapped old domain predicates**, allowing genuine current hard/rank exclusions. The left-only point cannot be covered using only the right predicate, and the right-only point cannot be covered using only the left. As both are actually current-feasible and at the cutoff, a sound hard/rank exclusion cannot remove them. This argument does not depend on the partition order or a producer's chosen multiplier search.

This is not a claim that every possible proof needs two certificates. Direct current proofs, the accepted ADD DAG language, another admitted domain, or different replacement maps can change the available proof. The ordinary arm with verified ADD admission followed by the same portfolio kernel has the identical reusable-domain interpretation. Nothing here establishes a unique mechanism advantage or a general certificate lower bound.
