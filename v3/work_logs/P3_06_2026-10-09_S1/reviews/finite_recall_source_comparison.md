# Final finite-recall source qualification

Contributor: **ChatGPT (GPT-6 Astra Pro), principal source review**.
October 9, 2026 UTC. Separate from the six document hashes bound by the final

Rechecked [Foster and Hart (2018)](https://math.huji.ac.il/~hart/papers/calib-eq.pdf),
§2.4, §4 equation (19), Theorem 10 and its proof. For fixed accuracy
$`\varepsilon>0`$ and finite Lipschitz bound $`L`$, their deterministic,
stationary procedure has finite recall and may forecast on a finite grid.
After a uniform startup horizon, every $`[0,1]`$-valued $`L`$-Lipschitz
report test has time-normalized residual at most $`\varepsilon`$.

The procedure, recall window and grid depend on the declared accuracy and
test class. This is not a zero-error asymptotic guarantee for one fixed
window. Its report-test weights are distinct from our external issue stakes;
the source theorem does not by itself supply the delayed weighted contract.
Its fixed-point construction is also not our executable rational root method.

**Comparison inference:** the FS square-root exponent does not establish a
memory-optimal forecasting method. Fixed-accuracy finite-recall procedures
are another ordinary comparison, with different parameter and computational
costs. Forgetting old learner state also does not provide arbitrary historical
repricing; retaining a separate audit history must be charged. No runtime or
memory advantage for an unimplemented comparator is claimed here.
