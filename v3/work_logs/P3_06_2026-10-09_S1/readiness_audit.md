**Acceptance judgment.** The positive task result is the finite update and
its exact conditional inequalities; the negative comparisons determine their
meaning. The scalar is useful as a common input to declared affine queries,
but neither its format nor expert-relative regret establishes accurate
mathematical anticipation. The saved controls test that distinction directly.
The exact solver wins on accuracy on this cheap domain, while the action and
repricing results identify which additional contracts remain valid even when
prediction quality is inferior. These facts satisfy this task's construction-
or-obstruction criterion without presuming the paid-computation answer or a

The preserved addendum's central potential identity survives. Its inference
to expert regret and calibration concerns one issued scalar, so it avoids
the separate report-distribution/mean gap. Exact numerical allowances remain
part of the theorem even when they are small in observed runs.

Several stronger interpretations do not survive the comparison. The ordinary
Brier aggregator has lower Brier loss than both polynomial variants in all
four 128-query development cases; exact arithmetic has zero loss. The capital
method improves both polynomial variants on the checked 32-query prefixes,
but does not dominate the aggregator or match the exact solver. It is a
stronger ordinary construction with different resource costs and different
certificates. The established FS method gives a stronger fixed-Lipschitz
calibration exponent than the proposed growing-tent extension.

The finite-bound analysis supplies some genuine strength without claiming
predictive superiority: every generic Brier bound improves the coarse
unknown-weight envelope, and retaining the actual forecast-action gap gives
a negative certified upper regret in the shortcut decision case. Other
individual expert and bin bounds remain looser than elementary alternatives.
The recorded numerical allowance barely enlarges these fixed-tape bounds;
a new run at different tolerance could change its forecasts and variance.

The BRIA comparison is now exact at two levels. The sparse witness separates
vanishing averages from coverage. CF-16 strengthens this with summable scalar
