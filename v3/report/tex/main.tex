\section{Abstract}\label{abstract}

A bounded reasoner often needs an action before it has a complete
mathematical answer, a fully adequate model, or a reliable estimate of
the benefit of further computation. This report synthesizes the third
phase of Value Logic around five questions about logical uncertainty,
counterfactuals, fallible models, learning usefulness, and probability
information in values. The principal assessed contribution is an exact
whole-range algorithm for a future minimum of affine responses,
conditioned on retained interval-valued optimum receipts over
independent rational source polytopes. With \(n\) source blocks and
\(k\) receipts, it uses at most \(2n2^k\) local linear programs and
\(O(n3^k+n2^k\log(n2^k))\) further comparisons, returns at most \(n2^k\)
intervals, and supports rational certificates and source witnesses.
Preserving gaps enables decisions that an interval hull cannot justify.
Selected secondary results provide sharp counterfactual bounds from
compressed ridge information, task-relative correction of fallible
polynomial models, and exact bounds on the benefit of further
computation or observation from partial decision information. Finite
forecasting and probability-identification results connect these
services to established learning and decision theory. The outcome is a
useful mathematical and executable synthesis with a bounded contribution
claim, rather than a general learner of mathematical truth.
Complete-cost superiority over the strongest conventional alternatives
remains unestablished.

\section{1. The problem and the contribution}\label{sec-introduction}

The organizing question is practical: \textbf{what can a reasoner safely
do with the information it has, and what additional information would be
worth acquiring?} An unresolved theorem, a partially executed optimizer,
and a fallible predictive model create different kinds of uncertainty.
Treating them as interchangeable obscures both useful decisions and
failure modes. Phase three instead develops explicit contracts between
evidence, retained information, computation, and action.

The original questions are addressed in order: (Q1) how to use
unresolved mathematics; (Q2) how to evaluate logical and model
counterfactuals; (Q3) whether several useful but fallible models help;
(Q4) how to refine usefulness estimates and price reasoning, including
the comparison with Logical Induction; and (Q5) which probability
information values contain. A restricted answer counts as progress when
it supplies a concrete guarantee or capability and makes its remaining
obligations visible. It need not solve the broadest interpretation of
the question.

\begin{definition}[Evidence and decision contract]\label{def-contract}

A contract specifies a source family \(\mathcal X\), an observation map
and the evidence actually received, a target quantity, a finite action
family, losses in declared units, and admissible paid operations. Its
compatible source set \(\mathcal X_H\) contains the sources satisfying
the received evidence. A certificate is conditional on this contract. A
source witness proves compatibility; it does not prove that the witness
is the actual source. An empty compatible set is a conflict to
investigate, not permission to infer arbitrary useful conclusions.

\end{definition}

Three distinctions recur. A mathematical certificate is different from a
forecast; knowing an action is optimal is different from knowing its
loss is small; and refining a calculation about a fixed source is
different from observing the actual source. The results below make those
distinctions operational. They also explain the relation to the phrase
\emph{value logic}: losses, computational costs, dependence, and
revision determine which information matters. Conventional robust
optimization, abstract interpretation, numerical analysis, and decision
theory already embody many of these concerns. The scientific question is
the explicit additional result, not whether an algorithm has been given
an unconventional label.

The principal assessed contribution, called \textbf{V19-R/v1}, is a
specialized whole-range synthesis. It combines familiar local linear
programming and subset dynamic programming into an exact answer to a
particular retained-information problem. Its mathematical confirmation
and contribution assessment were accepted at that bounded scope.
Secondary results were selected here for their relevance to the five
questions, not because they passed that same review. In the compact map
below, \textbf{A} denotes the assessed V19 claim; \textbf{D} denotes a
development derivation with scoped executable evidence; \textbf{S}
denotes an established result reconstructed or adapted in the project.
These are different evidence categories, not a numerical ranking of
scientific importance.

\section{2. Q1: useful decisions before mathematics is
resolved}\label{sec-q1}

\textbf{Strongest supported answer.} A bounded reasoner can maintain
sound outer information about unresolved answers, sometimes certify an
action before resolving them, and distinguish uncertainty from
inconsistency. It can also recover exact nonconvex numerical ranges from
retained computations in the V19 class. These are finite conditional
services. They do not provide probabilities for arbitrary unresolved
mathematical sentences or the broad learning properties of Logical
Induction \citep{li}.

\subsection{2.1. Interrupted reasoning must preserve unvisited
possibilities}\label{sec-covers}

For a finite list of versioned Boolean questions, let \(y\in\{0,1\}^m\)
denote their intended answers. Received hard constraints define a finite
feasible set \(F(H)\). An implementation maintains a cover
\(A(H)\supseteq F(H)\), for example a union of cubes whose coordinates
are \(0\), \(1\), or unresolved. If every hard receipt is true of \(y\),
then \(y\in A(H)\). Evaluating a loss by sound interval operations on
this cover yields a conditional bound on its actual value.

The invariant survives a split that replaces one cube by both children
and a pruning step justified by a checked contradiction. It does not
survive silently discarding an unfinished branch. For example, visiting
only \(x=0\) for loss \(100x\) does not establish the range \([0,0]\)
while \(x=1\) remains possible. Likewise, withdrawing a premise requires
restoring an appropriate cover or marking the dependent result stale.
These are applications of the established abstraction principle
\citep{abstract}; the project supplies typed evidence, finite
operations, and versioned decision use.

Finite shrinking feasible sets eventually stabilize, but stabilization
time is not generally observable. A computable rule that always
recognized completion of arbitrary computable future receipt streams
would decide whether a further constraint encoding a halting event will
ever arrive. This obstruction does not prevent a decision certificate
from becoming stable earlier: if every remaining answer supports the
same action, later truthful narrowing preserves that action's warrant.
\href{https://github.com/TristanMiano/value_logic/blob/main/v3/derivations/03_logical_uncertainty.md}{The
finite construction} and its
\href{https://github.com/TristanMiano/value_logic/blob/main/v3/derivations/03_refinement_extensions.md}{refinement
extension} keep these claims separate from a general theory of
arithmetic belief.

\subsection{2.2. Retained optimum values can constrain a new
computation}\label{sec-range}

Suppose earlier computations returned intervals for optimum values,
while the underlying numerical parameters were not retained exactly.
Reusing each interval independently discards the fact that the same
hidden parameters generated all the receipts. V19 asks for the complete
future scalar range under that shared-source condition.

Let \(n\geq1\). Independently uncertain vectors \(x_i\) lie in supplied
nonempty compact rational polytopes \(B_i\), with possibly different
positive dimensions. Independence here means a product uncertainty set,
not probabilistic independence. Let \(g_{ji}\) and \(f_i\) be rational
affine forms. The \(k\) retained receipts and future readout are

\begin{equation}
a_j\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq n}g_{ji}(x_i)\leq b_j
 \quad(j\in K=\{1,\ldots,k\}),\qquad
 V(x)=\min_i f_i(x_i).
 \label{eq-source}
\end{equation}

Every old lower bound applies to every block; each old upper bound needs
at least one witnessing block. For a receipt subset \(T\subseteq K\),
form

\begin{equation}
Q_i(T)=B_i\cap\bigcap_{j\in K}\{g_{ji}\geq a_j\}
              \cap\bigcap_{j\in T}\{g_{ji}\leq b_j\}.
 \label{eq-slice}
\end{equation}

If this slice is nonempty, let \(\ell_i(T)\) and \(u_i(T)\) be the
minimum and maximum of \(f_i\) on it, with attaining points. Its affine
image is the entire interval between them. Empty slices are unavailable,
rather than assigned invented endpoints.

For a block index set \(I\) and receipt subset \(S\), define the
bottleneck

\begin{equation}
U_I(S)=\max_{\bigsqcup_{i\in I}T_i=S}\ \min_{i\in I}u_i(T_i),
 \label{eq-bottleneck}
\end{equation}

where partitions with an empty slice are omitted. Set \(U_I(S)=-\infty\)
when no feasible partition exists;
\(U_\varnothing(\varnothing)=+\infty\) and \(U_\varnothing(S)=-\infty\)
for nonempty \(S\).

\begin{theorem}[Exact anchored range, V19-R/v1]\label{thm-range}

Under Equation \ref{eq-source}, the entire attainable set is

\begin{equation}
V(\mathcal X_H)=
 \bigcup_{i,T}
 \left[\ell_i(T),\ \min\{u_i(T),U_{[n]\setminus\{i\}}(K\setminus T)\}\right],
 \label{eq-range}
\end{equation}

omitting unavailable slices and intervals with upper endpoint below
lower endpoint. The set has at most \(n2^k\) closed intervals before
merging. It can be computed using at most \(2n2^k\) local rational LP
solves and \(O(n3^k+n2^k\log(n2^k))\) additional comparisons, with
\(O(n2^k)\) scalar table entries plus input, backpointers, and local
certificates. Every requested attainable rational value has a
reconstructable rational source. An empty union certifies inconsistency
of the supplied premises.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

Take a compatible source. Assign each receipt to one block witnessing
its upper bound, obtaining a partition \((T_i)\). Choose an anchor \(i\)
attaining the future minimum \(q\). Then
\(\ell_i(T_i)\leq q\leq u_i(T_i)\). Every other block has future value
at least \(q\), so the bottleneck of this outside partition is at least
\(q\). Thus \(q\) belongs to the displayed interval.

Conversely, take \(q\) in a displayed interval. Recover an outside
partition attaining its bottleneck and put every outside block at a
local maximum. Their future values are at least \(q\). In the anchor
slice, interpolate its minimum and maximum points to attain exactly
\(q\). Convexity preserves the slice constraints. Every old lower bound
holds, every upper bound has its assigned witness, and the new minimum
is \(q\). Rational interpolation preserves rationality. Compactness
supplies attainment, including singleton images. The empty-set
conventions handle \(n=1\) and \(k=0\).

Prefix and suffix tables compute Equation \ref{eq-bottleneck} by
splitting each subset:
\(P_i(S)=\max_{T\subseteq S}\min\{P_{i-1}(S\setminus T),u_i(T)\}\). The
sum of the numbers of splits is \(\sum_{S\subseteq K}2^{|S|}=3^k\) per
layer. Combining a prefix and suffix gives each outside table in another
\(3^k\) scan. Sorting and merging the at most \(n2^k\) intervals gives
the remaining term. LP bit complexity, rational number lengths,
certificate bytes, and source reconstruction arithmetic are additional
costs, not unit-cost consequences of this comparison count.
\end{proof}

The certificate covers \textbf{every indexed local slice}. For a bounded
slice it supplies feasible extremizers and matching nonnegative dual
multipliers; for an empty slice it supplies a Farkas witness. The
verifier reconstructs constraints from the original input, checks the
rational identities, and recomputes the union. Omission of a slice is
not evidence of emptiness. This division makes the optimizer an
untrusted producer while retaining explicit trust in input
interpretation and checker implementation.

The principal proof and certificate contract were reconstructed in the
bounded confirmation. Twelve Lean units checked selected finite-set,
order, recurrence, and witness-combination lemmas. They do not formalize
all rational LP existence, bit complexity, parsing, Python execution, or
semantic premises. The development audit in Section 8 supplies
additional finite implementation evidence; it is not an exhaustive
program-correctness proof.

\subsection{2.3. Why the whole range can matter}\label{sec-gap}

A positive-cost example uses independent local variables and two
response vectors

\begin{equation}
x_1=(10+2t+u,\ 11-t+v),\qquad
 x_2=(10+s+u',\ 12-2s+v'),
 \label{eq-positive-example}
\end{equation}

with \(0\leq t,s\leq1\) and \(0\leq u,v,u',v'\leq1/10\). Each
coordinate-minimum receipt lies in \([10,10.1]\). Neither block can
witness both upper bounds: before thickening, the least possible maximum
of its two excess coordinates is \(2/3>1/10\). The two split assignments
give, for the future minimum total response,

\begin{equation}
R=[21,21.2]\ \cup\ [21.9,22.2].
 \label{eq-positive-range}
\end{equation}

For the first assignment the excess total ranges from \(1\) to \(1.2\);
for the reverse it ranges from \(1.9\) to \(2.2\). Independence permits
both local extrema and interpolation. Hence a later band \([21.4,21.6]\)
contradicts the retained model, while its interval hull admits that
band. The same gap certifies \(|1/(V-21.5)|\leq10/3\). This is a
model-criticism and numerical-stability consequence, not a probability
claim.

Whole-range computation is most relevant to repeated or nonmonotone
queries. For a single forbidden band, direct feasibility can be cheaper.
For a convex scalar loss, the worst loss on a compact scalar set occurs
at an extreme endpoint; internal holes add nothing to that particular
minimax duty. Further, an explicit range may itself be exponentially
large: the preserved hypersimplex construction produces
\(\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\) isolated values with two blocks and
growing local dimension. This limits explicit output, not all succinct
representations or single-value queries.

\subsection{2.4. Relationship to the strongest
alternatives}\label{sec-range-prior}

Inverse optimal-value inference is established \citep{inverse}.
Mixed-integer formulations already encode active pieces of
piecewise-affine models \citep{mip}. Berger and Sankaranarayanan combine
compatible-subset search, covering, and LP infeasibility certificates to
fit piecewise-affine models \citep{berger}. Goodman supplies general
semiring outside recurrences \citep{goodman}. V19's outside bottlenecks
are a specialization of that machinery. The proposed contribution is the
\textbf{anchored continuous scalar image with its receipt-parameter
bound, complete certificate, and source recovery}, not a new LP theorem,
convolution primitive, or generic inference principle.

A substantive distinction from fitting one model is preserving all
value-bearing slices. With \(x_1,x_2\in[0,1]\), receipt
\(\min(x_1,x_2)=0\), and future \(\min(-x_1,-x_2)\), the correct image
is \([-1,0]\). Keeping only each block's maximally constrained receipt
slice retains \(\{0\}\) and loses valid values. The empty-subset slice
matters. A conventional solver can retain it and perform the same
synthesis; an identical implementation necessarily ties. A distinct MIP
solver, direct band-feasibility algorithm, or structural shortcut is
instead a genuine competitor whose resources can differ.

The
\href{https://github.com/TristanMiano/value_logic/blob/main/v3/report/claims.md}{close-comparison
map} includes inverse interval LP, bounded-error identification,
max-plus set estimation, and exact set propagation. Some potentially
close full texts remained inaccessible in the assessed review; their
abstract-level scope is recorded rather than treated as a novelty
clearance. The accepted claim is a useful moderately novel synthesis
under that explicit uncertainty. It supplies neither universal
superiority nor a new physical model-identification method.

\section{3. Q2: counterfactual meaning and retained
information}\label{sec-q2}

\textbf{Strongest supported answer.} Counterfactual questions become
tractable when the changed object, unchanged commitments, dependency
routing, and selection rule are explicit. Old evidence can then be
transported under a numerical and domain-coverage bridge. N16 gives a
stronger analytic answer for one numerical edit problem: the sharp
edited ridge value compatible with a retained response block. None of
these results determines a uniquely correct semantics for every false
mathematical antecedent.

\subsection{3.1. A numerical value does not define an
intervention}\label{sec-semantics}

Consider a fixed function with \(f_0(h_0)=0\), calls \(A=f_0(h_0)\) and
\(B=f_0(h_0)\), and loss \(2+A-2B\). Conditioning on \(A=1\) has an
empty ordinary target. Intervening only on the occurrence \(A\) gives
loss \(3\). Replacing the function by an explicitly chosen \(f_1\) with
\(f_1(h_0)=1\), and redirecting both calls, gives loss \(1\).
Redirecting only the actor's call again gives \(3\). A predictor of the
old function does not automatically become a predictor of its
replacement.

The finite request interface therefore records an operation tag,
original antecedent, candidate grammar, hard commitments, permitted
exceptions, ranking, tie policy, interpretation map, and consumer loss.
Structural interventions follow familiar modified-equation semantics
\citep{halpern}. Ranked repairs select all minimum-ranked alternatives
unless an explicit policy breaks ties. The finite counterpossible
adapter instead uses paired positive/negative support in a Belnap--Dunn
propositional fragment, allowing named atomic normality exceptions while
preserving connective rules. Its states are not ordinary arithmetic
worlds. Nonvacuous counterpossible semantics has substantial prior
literature \citep{counterpossible}; this adapter is an explicit limited
choice, not a philosophical settlement.

\begin{proposition}[Domain coverage plus a numerical bridge]\label{prop-transport}

Let \(S_1\) be the nonempty family of currently selected alternatives.
For every \(y\in S_1\), suppose some valid old proof gives
\(d_{0,i}(x)\leq B_i(x)\) and a warranted interpretation gives
\(d_1(y)\leq\alpha_i d_{0,i}(x)+\delta_i(x,y)\), with \(\alpha_i>0\). If
\(I(y)\) lists all applicable pairs, then

\begin{equation}
\max_{y\in S_1}d_1(y)
 \leq\max_{y\in S_1}\min_{(i,x)\in I(y)}
       \{\alpha_iB_i(x)+\delta_i(x,y)\}.
 \label{eq-transport}
\end{equation}

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

Apply the bridge to each valid old inequality at its actual domain
point, take the best valid upper bound for that point, and then maximize
over the currently selected family. Positivity preserves the inequality.
Coverage of every current alternative is indispensable; preserving the
old proof file alone does not provide it.
\end{proof}

The minimum selects a proof, not an action allowed to observe hidden
\(y\). For instance, proof allowances \((0,10)\) and \((10,0)\) on two
cases combine to a uniform allowance \(0\), while choosing a proof after
first taking its worst case yields \(10\). This is an established
dependency-aware operation with a useful retention consequence. Ordinary
proof systems can preserve the same support information.

\subsection{3.2. N16: what a compressed solve tells us about an edited
solve}\label{sec-n16}

Let \(M\) be an unretained finite real matrix, \(W\) an isometry with
\(k\) columns, and \(c\) a unit vector. Retain the strict response block
\(C=W^T(I+MM^T)^{-1}W\), where \(0\prec C\prec I\). An edit \(N\) has
the same shape as its source \(M\) and satisfies
\(\|N-M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq e\), with \(e>0\). The target is the
reoptimized ridge value

\begin{equation}
F(N;Wc)=\min_z\{\|Wc-Nz\|^2+\|z\|^2\}
        =c^TW^T(I+NN^T)^{-1}Wc.
 \label{eq-ridge}
\end{equation}

The information class ranges over \textbf{all finite compatible sources
and edits}, with unrestricted finite source dimensions. This differs
from choosing one robust solution before an edit of a completely known
matrix. Acquisition of \(C\), the isometry interface, and the edit
warrant are substantive inputs.

Put \(E=e^2\), \(h=(E+\sqrt{E^2+4E})/2\), \(a=1+t\), and \(d=t-E-E/t\).
Define \(L_t(C)=C(aI-dC)^{-1}\) and \(U_t(C)=aC(I+dC)^{-1}\).

\begin{theorem}[Sharp retained-block endpoints, developmental N16-C/v1]\label{thm-n16}

The infimum and supremum of Equation \ref{eq-ridge} over the stated
information class are

\begin{equation}
\underline F=\max_{t\geq h}c^TL_t(C)c,
 \qquad
 \overline F=\inf_{t\geq h}c^TU_t(C)c,
 \label{eq-n16}
\end{equation}

where the upper family includes its limit \(1\) at infinity. Each
endpoint is attained by a finite compatible source and edit; the sources
may differ. A construction uses at most \(2k\) input and output
coordinates. Sharpness is for the specified scalar direction \(c\), not
a simultaneous least or greatest matrix envelope.

\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Inverse compression]\label{lem-compression}

For every positive definite real matrix \(A\) and isometry \(W\),
\(W^TA^{-1}W\succeq(W^TAW)^{-1}\).

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}

For any vector \(c\), maximize \(2(Wc)^Tz-z^TAz\) over all \(z\) to
obtain \(c^TW^TA^{-1}Wc\). Restricting \(z\) to \(Wu\) gives the maximum
\(c^T(W^TAW)^{-1}c\). Restriction cannot increase the maximum; validity
in every direction gives the matrix order.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Proof explanation for Theorem \ref{thm-n16}.} The ridge normal
equations give Equation \ref{eq-ridge} and the quadratic dual
\(F(N;v)=\max_w(2v^Tw-w^T(I+NN^T)w)\). Lemma \ref{lem-compression}
supplies the required compression inequality. Young's inequality applied
in both directions to \(N=M+(N-M)\) gives

\begin{equation}
NN^T\preceq aMM^T+(1+1/t)EI,
 \qquad MM^T\preceq aNN^T+(1+1/t)EI.
 \label{eq-young}
\end{equation}

Invert the positive expressions and compress them using the dual
inequality. Because \(d\geq0\) and \(a-d>0\), the resulting bounds are
precisely \(L_t(C)\) and \(U_t(C)\). This proves validity for every
compatible source, rather than only for a tested matrix.

Sharpness needs more. At an interior optimum, choose the canonical
source \(M=(C^{-1}-I)^{1/2}\) and a rank-one edit in the old
residual/solution directions. Differentiating the scalar endpoint makes
Young's equality condition agree with the new normal equations. At the
cap \(t=h\), a hidden second moment may be required. With \(H=I-C\),
\(s=c^TCc\), and \(m_0=c^TC^2c\), a block contraction

\begin{equation}
S_\tau=\begin{pmatrix}C&\sqrt{\tau CH}\\\sqrt{\tau CH}&H\end{pmatrix},
 \quad 0\leq\tau<1,
 \quad c^TW^TS_\tau^2Wc=m_0+\tau(s-m_0)
 \label{eq-dilation}
\end{equation}

realizes the needed moment while preserving \(C\). Each eigenvalue block
has positive determinant \((1-\tau)\lambda(1-\lambda)\) and trace \(1\),
so \(0\prec S_\tau\prec I\) and it corresponds to a finite source. The
endpoint derivative conditions select moments \(s(1+h)/(2+h)\) for the
lower cap and \(s/(2+h)\) for the upper cap, each in \([m_0,s)\) when
that branch applies. The corresponding rank-one edits satisfy the new
normal equations. The remaining upper branch is cancellation, giving
value \(1\).
\href{https://github.com/TristanMiano/value_logic/blob/main/v3/report/claims.md\#n16}{Appendix
A's N16 entry} links the complete branch calculations, including
boundary equality and the later smaller-witness construction. This is a
reconstructed developmental theorem, not a consequence of V19's review.

The pointwise bounds are already useful without optimizing \(t\) or
proving sharpness. Their operator monotonicity permits truthful rational
brackets \(C_-\preceq C\preceq C_+\): evaluate \(c^TL_t(C_-)c\) and
\(c^TU_t(C_+)c\) exactly. The rational checker verifies these finite
algebraic conditions and a strict threshold. It does not authenticate
the source, isometry, bracket truth, or norm warrant. Its finite bit
limits also prevent an unconditional completeness promise for
arbitrarily tiny strict gaps.

For a concrete decision, take \(C=\mathrm{diag}(1/1601,9/10)\),
\(c=(1,1)/\sqrt2\), and \(e=3\). Brackets \(C\pm10^{-4}I\) with rational
parameter \(t=100\) certify an upper value approximately
\(0.58311909<0.6\). The exact calculation can use the rational matrix
\(cc^T\). A different source preserving the entire compared old
optimizer and residual admits an edit with value \(1\); the extra block
information excludes it. This establishes a capability from retained
information. Three scalar polarization queries suffice to acquire the
displayed block, but one suitably targeted scalar query can settle this
particular threshold too. Thus the example supplies no
minimal-acquisition or speed claim.

Robust least squares already supplies the worst-residual machinery
\citep{robustls}, and operator interpolation supplies a strong exact
finite SDP comparator \citep{operator}. The project's Gram reduction
uses two positive semidefinite matrices to express the same
unknown-source and edit constraints; it attains the same endpoint
values. N16's possible increment is an explicit one-parameter solution
and finite equality construction. A subsequent ordinary
hidden-moment/minimax derivation made that increment easier to
reconstruct and potentially more routine. The last scoped contribution
estimate remained 55--80\%, central 65\%, with novelty as the principal
blocker. This historical subjective assessment is not a probability that
the displayed inequality is true, nor a calibrated confidence interval.
N16 remains a worthwhile extracted result without being promoted to
V19's status.

\section{4. Q3: task-relative use and correction of fallible
models}\label{sec-q3}

\textbf{Strongest supported answer.} A model can fail as a complete
account while supplying correct, useful consequences for particular
tasks. Multiple models can supply complementary predictions, proof
domains, or correction structure. The investigation provides exact
restricted mechanisms for doing so. It does not establish a general
advantage of a Value Logic ensemble over equally informed model
averaging, robust selection, or correction methods. Bayesian model
averaging already treats model uncertainty explicitly \citep{bma};
task-relative permission and repair are additional contracts, not
exclusive ideas.

\subsection{4.1. Correct the requested output instead of requiring the
whole model to be true}\label{sec-correction}

Let \(A(x)\) be an expensive true vector output and \(B(x)\) a cheap
fallible model, with residual \(F(x)=A(x)-B(x)\) over a field. Suppose a
full-column-rank matrix \(U\in\mathbb K^{n\times d}\) spans every
residual. Choose \(d\) linearly independent rows \(J\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact correction from a retained error space]\label{prop-correction}

For every input in the warranted domain,

\begin{equation}
A(x)=B(x)+U(U_J)^{-1}\bigl(A_J(x)-B_J(x)\bigr).
 \label{eq-correction}
\end{equation}

Thus \(d\) paid true coordinates, together with model evaluation and
matrix arithmetic, suffice for the complete corrected output. A contrast
\(w^TB(x)\) is already exact whenever \(w^TU=0\).

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

Write \(F(x)=Uz\). Restriction to \(J\) gives \(F_J=U_Jz\); invert this
equation and substitute. The contrast assertion follows by annihilating
the residual space.
\end{proof}

The algebra is ordinary skeleton/cross reconstruction, with low-rank
approximation as a strong ancestor \citep{cross}. The difficult
operational premise is acquiring a warranted residual space without
making every future true evaluation. For fixed polynomial residuals of
known total degree at most \(D\), sample from a grid of \(q>D\) field
elements per coordinate. At any incomplete observed span \(S\), a linear
functional annihilating \(S\) but not the full residual range produces a
nonzero polynomial. The Schwartz--Zippel bound gives
\(\Pr(F(X)\in S)\leq D/q\). Stopping after \(h\) consecutive
nonexpanding probes at a rank stage gives a false-completion bound at
most \(n(D/q)^h\), by a union bound over at most \(n\) incomplete
stages. On the single success event, \textbf{all future inputs and all
later chosen annihilating contrasts} are protected. This is structural
scope, not a posterior probability that the model is true.

If the residual also has a declared feature representation
\(F(x)=E\phi(x)\), keep feature/error pairs \(\Phi\) and \(Y\). A new
feature in the old span has error \(Y\alpha\) when
\(\Phi\alpha=\phi(x)\), so it needs no new truth query. Otherwise
acquire the \(d\) anchors in Equation \ref{eq-correction} and enlarge
the feature span. Starting at rank \(r\), at most \(M-r\) such purchases
determine an \(M\)-dimensional feature map. Under only these coefficient
and output-space constraints, fewer than \(d\) independent anchor
coordinates cannot identify an arbitrary new direction: an unobserved
perturbation \(Uz\lambda\) can preserve the old pairs and queried
coordinates while changing the answer.

The constructed \(n=128\), \(M=35\), \(d=4\) example starts from seven
full samples of feature rank seven. Its total is
\(128\cdot7+4(35-7)=1008\) true-coordinate outputs, compared with
\(128\cdot35=4480\) for full-output interpolation, a \textbf{77.5\%
reduction in that oracle component}. The model, feature evaluation,
factorization, storage, numerical conditioning, and shared batch
operations still need pricing. A conventional two-sided interpolation
implementation matches the count. Exact finite-field tests support the
algebra; they do not validate a real application's access tariff or turn
a probabilistic polynomial-scope event into zero-error certainty.

\subsection{4.2. Revision preserves only warranted
scope}\label{sec-model-revision}

A useful repair rule is explicit: if a new residual satisfies
\(F'(x)=LF(x)+G(x)\) and a separately warranted subspace \(T\) contains
every \(G(x)\), then \(\mathrm{image}(LU)+T\) contains every new
residual. The correction construction can be rebuilt on that space while
retaining the original common event. Arbitrarily retraining \(B\)
supplies no such guarantee. Similarly, partial mathematical labels may
support a restricted equation or action even while the whole feature
model remains inadequate, but the population, selection procedure, and
confidence event must remain the ones actually justified.

The wider portfolio includes joint incomplete-evidence certificates,
nonlinear approximate summaries, query-guided proof selection, and
protected model scopes. Their strongest comparisons sometimes removed an
apparent gain: established scenario bounds beat one proposed elementary
envelope; per-task selection matched a marginal ensemble guarantee; and
interpolation defeated an overly favorable private-validation probe
count. These outcomes favor \textbf{task-relative adequacy with explicit
correction costs} as the useful synthesis. They do not support the claim
that maintaining several fallible models is inherently better than all
conventional methods.
\href{https://github.com/TristanMiano/value_logic/blob/main/v3/report/portfolio.md}{Appendix
B} preserves these directions and their blockers.

\section{5. Q4: learning usefulness, stopping computation, and buying
observations}\label{sec-q4}

\textbf{Strongest supported answer.} Finite loss forecasts can improve
under checked feedback, and partial evidence can bound what more
computation or an observation might buy. The project gives executable
adaptations and sharp restricted decision bounds. It does not match
Logical Induction's general language-wide guarantees or demonstrate a
generally efficient metareasoner.

\subsection{5.1. A cost forecast is a forecast, with an explicit error
allowance}\label{sec-forecast}

For a versioned binary answer \(y_t\), a known affine loss
\(b_t(a)+d_t(a)y_t\) has forecast \(b_t(a)+d_t(a)p_t\). The scalar
\(p_t\) is learned, not admitted as a hard receipt. The finite
implementation adapts Vovk's K29-star defensive forecasting
\citep{vovk}. With continuous predictable features \(\Phi_t(p)\), put

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 R_{t-1}&=\sum_{s<t}(y_s-p_s)\Phi_s(p_s),\\
 S_t(p)&=\langle R_{t-1},\Phi_t(p)\rangle
             +\tfrac{1-2p}{2}\|\Phi_t(p)\|^2,\\
 A_t&=2\max\{0,(1-p_t)S_t(p_t),-p_tS_t(p_t)\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq-forecast}
\end{equation}

Choose a favorable endpoint or search for a root; an interrupted root
search retains the actual allowance \(A_t\). For binary \(y\), the
identity \((y-p)^2=p(1-p)+(1-2p)(y-p)\) proves

\begin{equation}
\|R_T\|^2\leq
 B_T:=\sum_{t\leq T}p_t(1-p_t)\|\Phi_t(p_t)\|^2+\sum_{t\leq T}A_t.
 \label{eq-potential}
\end{equation}

It follows by expanding one potential increment and telescoping. Here
\(p_t,q_{t,i}\in[0,1]\), the finite weights \(w_t\geq0\) and expert
values are available before the current answer, and \(\alpha,\beta>0\)
are fixed feature scales. Expert features \(\alpha w_t(q_{t,i}-p)\)
yield weighted Brier regret at most \(2\sqrt{B_T}/\alpha\) to each
supplied expert. Calibration features \(\beta w_tb_j(p)\) yield
residuals at most \(\sqrt{B_T}/\beta\) in the declared continuous bins.
These are meaningful finite guarantees with charged computation and
truthful feedback. They neither discover arbitrary mathematical
structure absent from the experts nor supply a universal convergence
rate. Zero-allowance potential control and concatenated features are
established machinery; the adaptation is the bounded rational
calculation, versioning, feedback, and loss interface.

Paid selective feedback adds observable performance estimates: only
issued forecasts, purchased checked labels, and actual selection
probabilities enter the reports. In the binary setting, Brier loss and
prospective action-error probability differ by the public term
\(p_t(1-p_t)\). This lets appropriately centered estimators share one
unknown residual. Conditional bounded-moment arguments then yield scoped
horizon-specific bounds. The fixed query tape, positive purchase
probabilities, frozen block state, and fresh-bit assumptions matter.
Deterministic pseudorandom test paths check formulas, not coverage
frequencies. The
\href{https://github.com/TristanMiano/value_logic/blob/main/v3/derivations/07_observable_performance.md}{supporting
derivations} retain the exact sampling contract.

\subsection{5.2. A sharp bound can make further calculation
unnecessary}\label{sec-stopping}

Fix bounded losses for a finite action family. Partition a scalar outer
range into finitely many cells on which every loss is constant, denoting
the loss of action \(a\) in cell \(j\) by \(v_{ja}\). Let \(K\) be the
known attainable cells and \(P\) the possible cells. The true nonempty
support \(J\) satisfies \(K\subseteq J\subseteq P\). Its robust loss is
\(W_a(J)=\max_{j\in J}v_{ja}\). Define \(l_b=\max_{j\in K}v_{jb}\), or
\(-\infty\) when \(K\) is empty.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp computational gap from a partial cell signature]\label{thm-gap}

For at least two actions, the largest possible robust suboptimality of
incumbent \(a\) under this information is

\begin{equation}
\Gamma_a=\max\left\{0,\ \max_{b\ne a}\max_{j\in P}
          \bigl[v_{ja}-\max\{l_b,v_{jb}\}\bigr]\right\}.
 \label{eq-gap}
\end{equation}

For one action it is zero. Direct computation uses
\(O(|P|\,|\mathcal A|^2)\) scalar work. The bound is sharp for the
signature, whose permitted supports may be more numerous than those
realizable by the original source model.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

For any admissible support choose a cell \(j\) attaining \(W_a(J)\).
Every competitor satisfies \(W_b(J)\geq\max(l_b,v_{jb})\), giving the
upper bound. Conversely, for a maximizing pair \((b,j)\) choose
\(J=K\cup\{j\}\). Then \(W_b(J)=\max(l_b,v_{jb})\) and
\(W_a(J)\geq v_{ja}\), attaining a positive maximum; if the bound is
zero every admissible support has zero suboptimality. No enumeration of
all possible supports is needed.
\end{proof}

For known cell losses \((9,0)\) and \((0,10)\), and one possible
additional cell \((12,13)\), separate worst-loss intervals give a
suboptimality upper bound of \(2\) for action A. Equation \ref{eq-gap}
gives \(0\): the actual worst-loss vector is either \((9,10)\) or
\((12,13)\), both favoring A. B is better in the first known cell, so
statewise dominance alone does not explain the result. Retaining joint
information about the losses makes the decision complete before the
values are complete.

If a computation only refines knowledge of this fixed source and fixed
action class, has no reuse or other benefit, and costs \(c\) in the same
loss units, then \(c\geq\Gamma_a\) rules out a strict net improvement in
this robust criterion. A positive \(\Gamma_a\) only bounds possible
benefit; it does not recommend purchase. Optimality also does not imply
safety: losses \((0,10)\) and \((10,0)\) make both actions
minimax-optimal on the known two-cell support, while either can incur
actual loss \(10\).

The continuous analogue replaces the cell maximum by a supremum over an
outer set and retains known inner-set maxima. For piecewise-linear
losses, endpoints and original knots alone can miss a maximum:
additional intersections where a competitor crosses its known lower
maximum are required. The preserved example has a missed maximum
\(13/4\) at \(q=3/4\), versus \(1\) on the insufficient candidate set.
This refinement is separately developed, not imported into the finite
theorem without its compactness and continuity assumptions.

\subsection{5.3. Observation value needs a different policy class and a
named baseline}\label{sec-observation}

Computation selection is an established metalevel decision problem
\citep{hay}. A new truthful observation can change which action is taken
in the actual state; it is therefore not bounded by a fixed-model
optimization gap. If actions have losses \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\) on two
known states, both have minimax loss \(1\) and computational gap zero. A
perfect signal permits loss zero. Robust information discovery studies
exactly this distinction \citep{paradiso}.

For a deterministic truthful signal \(s(j)\) and unrestricted
deterministic action choice on each signal branch, define

\begin{equation}
B(J)=\min_a W_a(J),\qquad
 Q(J)=\max_{s:J_s\ne\varnothing}\min_a\max_{j\in J_s}v_{ja}.
 \label{eq-observation}
\end{equation}

\(B(J)-Q(J)\) is the ideal information value relative to the
\textbf{best constant action}. Relative to an arbitrary incumbent, the
relevant total improvement is instead

\begin{equation}
U_a=\max_{K\subseteq J\subseteq P,\ J\ne\varnothing}
             \{W_a(J)-Q(J)\}.
 \label{eq-incumbent}
\end{equation}

This includes improvement from selecting a better constant action. For
each threshold \(t\) among the finite loss entries, a branch action
\(h\) is legal when it has loss at most \(t\) on every known cell in
that branch. Its allowed set is \(A_{sh}(t)=\{j\in P_s:v_{jh}\leq t\}\).
Discard a threshold if a branch has no legal action or all allowed sets
are empty. Put \(M_a(t)=\max_{s,h\text{ legal},j\in A_{sh}(t)}v_{ja}\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact incumbent-aware observation bound]\label{prop-observation}

Under the preceding signal and policy contract,

\begin{equation}
U_a=\max_t\{M_a(t)-t\}.
 \label{eq-observation-threshold}
\end{equation}

It takes \(O(|V|\,|P|\,|\mathcal A|)\) scalar work for one incumbent,
where \(|V|\) is the number of distinct loss entries. An observation and
its required policy calculation costing at least \(U_a\) cannot strictly
improve this single-use robust criterion net of the fee.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}

For any support use \(t=Q(J)\) and its optimal action in each nonempty
branch; known cells in empty branches are absent. Every cell of \(J\)
lies in an allowed set, so \(W_a(J)\leq M_a(t)\). Conversely choose a
legal branch option and cell attaining \(M_a(t)\), choose a legal option
in every other branch, and take the union of these options' allowed
sets. It contains \(K\), is nonempty, and permits a contingent policy
with worst loss at most \(t\). Its incumbent loss is at least
\(M_a(t)\). The two inequalities establish equality.
\end{proof}

The baseline distinction is substantive. With safe/hazardous losses
\((0,1)\) and \((10,1)\) for fast/fallback, and no known attainable
cell, the ideal value \(\max_J(B-Q)\) is zero for every nonempty
possible support. Yet the incumbent fallback can improve by \(1\) if
only the safe cell is attainable. Rejecting every positive-fee
observation using the ideal bound would therefore be wrong. The
implemented wrapper uses Equation \ref{eq-incumbent} and reports the
ideal baseline separately.

\subsection{5.4. Root accounting and statistical
warrants}\label{sec-accounting}

For a complete policy \(\pi\) compared with a fallback \(b\), retain
paired expected task/resource savings \(d_{\pi,b}\). Simultaneous
coordinate bounds \(l_j\leq\mathbb E d_j\leq u_j\) give, at price vector
\(\lambda\), the lower saving
\(L=\sum_{\lambda_j\geq0}\lambda_jl_j+\sum_{\lambda_j<0}\lambda_ju_j\).
For \(H\) future uses, subtract still-unpaid policy overhead from \(HL\)
to choose a continuation. Already incurred acquisition and selection
costs remain in the root bill even though they should not distort the
continuation choice. An unsuccessful assessment branch still paid them.
Complete policies retain shared uncertainty across branches;
independently minimizing every branch requires an additional
rectangularity assumption.

Statistical source bands require their own coverage guarantee. If a
single simultaneous event of probability at least \(1-\delta\) contains
the actual source in every selected version, all sound deterministic
consequences of it inherit that event. With absolute loss bounded by
\(\gamma\) on that event and always in \([0,M]\), one obtains
\(\Pr(\text{loss}>\gamma)\leq\delta\) and
\(\mathbb E[\text{loss}]\leq\gamma+\delta(M-\gamma)\). Here \(\gamma\)
is an absolute bound, not the suboptimality \(\Gamma_a\). Fixed-time
confidence intervals do not justify arbitrary adaptive stopping;
confidence-sequence methods provide the appropriate established contract
\citep{howard}. This project does not implement a general statistical
source-calibration layer.

\section{6. Q5: probability information in values}\label{sec-q5}

\textbf{Strongest supported answer.} Known expected-loss values can
encode all, some, or none of a probability law. The amount depends on
payoff semantics and the admitted source family. A decision can be
identifiable when the full law and even its absolute action costs are
not. Conversely, optimization values and computational invoices need not
be probabilities at all.

Let \(p\in\mathcal P\subseteq\Delta_n\) be an admitted law and observe
\(y=Lp\), where the loss rows of \(L\) are known. The fiber
\(\mathcal F(y)=\{p\in\mathcal P:Lp=y\}\) expresses the unresolved
information. A target is recoverable precisely when it is constant on
every relevant fiber; an empty fiber signals incompatible premises.

\begin{theorem}[Expected-loss identification on the full simplex]\label{thm-identification}

Put \(A=[\mathbf1^T;L]\). A linear target \(Cp\) is determined by \(Lp\)
for every \(p\in\Delta_n\) if and only if \(\ker A\subseteq\ker C\),
equivalently every row of \(C\) is in the row span of \(A\). In that
case \(C=a\mathbf1^T+BL\) and the decoder is \(Cp=a+By\). Full-law
recovery requires and is implied by \(\mathrm{rank}A=n\).

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

The row-space identity supplies the displayed decoder. Conversely, if
\(Ad=0\) but \(Cd\ne0\), sufficiently small opposite perturbations of
the strictly positive uniform law, \(p_\pm=\mathbf1/n\pm td\), remain in
the simplex. They have identical observations and different targets,
defeating any decoder, linear or otherwise. The normalization row is
essential.
\end{proof}

On a convex restricted family, replace the ambient space by its actual
affine direction space. On a nonconvex catalogue the literal fiber
criterion remains valid but the ambient rank condition need not be
necessary: one linear measurement can distinguish finitely many known
candidate laws. Convexifying the family before conditioning can
introduce false ambiguities. This is the same structural reason that V19
keeps a nonconvex image rather than automatically replacing it by its
hull.

For a binary event with probability \(p\), a known expected cost
\(v=c_0+(c_1-c_0)p\) recovers \(p\) whenever \(c_1\ne c_0\). If the
stakes are unknown, the same number need not identify a probability. A
realized cost is also not an expected cost. Proper scoring rules give an
established loss-based incentive to report distributions
\citep{scoring}; they do not make every utility or receipt a
probability.

For an explicit task-only result, retain \(y=p_1\) on three states and
losses \(c_A=(0,1,2)\), \(c_B=(1,0,1)\). Their expected costs are
\(1-y+p_3\) and \(y+p_3\). Their difference \(1-2y\) is known, so A is
optimal for \(y>1/2\) and B for \(y<1/2\), although neither absolute
cost nor the full law is identified. More generally, a guaranteed Bayes
action exists on a fiber exactly when the intersection of its pointwise
optimal-action sets is nonempty. A minimax action can exist without this
common Bayes optimum; the consumer must name the intended criterion.

The discovery portfolio also studied information carried by query counts
and invoices. Exact parity rates and approximate nonlinear summaries
show that the permitted observation channel can matter as much as the
scalar's name. Their private-read, reset, and public-count restrictions
are load-bearing; direct transmission of a summary or shared randomness
can erase a separation. They are retained as interface results with
application and novelty obligations, not generalized into a claim that
money or computation time always encodes useful probabilities.

\section{7. A worked reasoning episode: partial evidence, action, and
revision}\label{sec-episode}

The following example connects the numerical source, partial
computation, stopping decision, and evidence reuse. \textbf{The
source-to-action portion is already implemented} in the developmental
\texttt{DecisionService}. The final model-edit step below is a
mathematical composition with a separately proved transport rule; the
current wrapper does not implement automatic cross-version transport.

There are two blocks \((x_i,y_i)\in[0,1]^2\). Their sums lie in
\([1.19,1.21]\) and \([1.79,1.81]\), respectively. Receipts require
\(\max_i x_i\geq.99\) and \(\max_i y_i\geq.99\). Equivalently, the
minimum of the corresponding negative coordinates lies in \([-1,-.99]\),
fitting Equation \ref{eq-source}. The future scalar is
\(q=\min_i(.5x_i-1.5y_i)\). A fast action loses \(10\) when \(q\) is in
the hazard band \(H=[-5/4,-6/5]\) and zero otherwise; fallback loses
\(1\) everywhere. These are declared mathematical losses, not measured
deployment outcomes.

\textbf{Partial evidence.} Initially the implementation has outer range
\([-3/2,1/2]\) and no known source. It reports a decision conditional on
nonemptiness, conservatively choosing fallback. Fallback's sharp
possible improvement is \(1\); the best-constant-action information
bound is nevertheless zero, illustrating the baseline distinction in
Section 5.3. A new observation costing at least \(1\) cannot improve
this incumbent net of that fee under the stated single-use policy
contract. A cheaper observation is not automatically worth buying.

\textbf{A computation choice.} The controller performs two passes of
exact box propagation over the eight indexed local slices. Neither block
can witness both receipts because its sum would be at least \(1.98\).
Only two split assignments survive. Assigning the \(x\) receipt to the
first block gives its \(y\) coordinate in \([.19,.22]\); assigning the
\(y\) receipt gives its \(x\) coordinate in the same interval. The
second block's free coordinate is in \([.79,.82]\). The resulting outer
range is

\begin{equation}
O=[-281/200,-11/8]\ \cup\ [-221/200,-43/40],
 \label{eq-episode-range}
\end{equation}

which misses \(H\). The saved run records 134 row visits, 28
coordinate-bound updates, and zero optimizer calls. A tailored ordinary
calculation derives the same answer. The chosen propagation route is a
demonstrated operation, not a claim that it was the optimal computation
to purchase. A later complete range certificate agrees with this union,
but the partial service correctly does not label its current state exact
without corresponding inner coverage.

\textbf{An action before completion.} An original-source witness
\(((1/5,1),(1,4/5))\) passes the base and receipt checks and gives
\(q=-7/5\). It establishes nonemptiness. The outer exclusion then
certifies fast with worst loss zero and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{fast}}=0\).
The action needs neither every local LP optimum nor an exact
identification of \(q\). Under the fixed-model, single-use stopping
contract, further paid calculation cannot improve its robust action
loss. A second witness \(((1,1/5),(4/5,1))\) gives \(q=-11/10\) and
preserves additional attainable information for later questions. The
implementation binds evidence to a copied problem digest, rejects a
wrong digest and an infeasible source without changing state, and binds
the loss/signal request separately. These checks prevent a valid bound
from being silently applied to a different problem.

\textbf{A subsequent model edit.} Suppose a later version changes a
local slice from \(Ax\leq b\) and readout \(c^Tx+d\) to \(A'x\leq b'\)
and \(c'^Tx+d'\), with the same indexed coordinates and a warranted new
finite box \(l\leq x\leq u\). An old nonnegative multiplier \(z\)
remains useful even if it is no longer dual-feasible for the new
objective:

\begin{equation}
c'^Tx+d'\leq d'+z^Tb'
       +\sum_r\max\{(c'-A'^Tz)_r l_r,(c'-A'^Tz)_r u_r\}.
 \label{eq-dual-reuse}
\end{equation}

Insert and subtract \(z^TA'x\), use the new inequalities, and maximize
the remaining linear residual on the new box to prove it. A
corresponding lower bound follows with a nonnegative lower multiplier.
Rebuilding all indexed outer slices from these corrected bounds
preserves a sound range enclosure. An old empty slice must be
reconsidered unless its infeasibility certificate still works. An added
source block, changed coordinate identity, unwarranted new box, or
changed loss semantics is outside this reuse step and requires a new
binding or proof.

Thus the fast action may be retained only if the rebuilt outer range
still misses \(H\) and the new nonempty source duty is met. Otherwise
the old action certificate becomes stale and fallback or new acquisition
is appropriate. This last paragraph is a \textbf{conditional
mathematical composition} of established dual-residual bounding with the
range interface. The repository tests the transport component
separately; it has not executed this exact revision as one end-to-end
controller episode. No statistical coverage layer, N16 solve, or
automatic model-learning loop is silently attached to the implemented
example.

\section{9. Accomplishment, remaining gaps, and next
direction}\label{sec-conclusion}

Phase three produced a concrete positive contribution and a connected
set of mechanisms for acting with incomplete mathematics. Its strongest
result makes retained optimum receipts into an exact, certifiable
future-range service with explicit parameter dependence. The wider
synthesis shows how incomplete ranges can settle actions, how a stopping
bound differs from observation value, how numerical information survives
warranted edits, how a globally fallible model can be corrected for a
task, and when values identify probabilities or only decisions. These
are useful restricted answers to all five questions. They advance the
organizing goal without requiring a new foundation for every form of
mathematical uncertainty.

The largest gaps are now specific. First, the mechanisms assume truthful
structural or statistical warrants whose acquisition can be expensive.
Second, the strongest conventional methods often supply the same
mathematics, leaving the contribution at the level of a specialized
theorem or synthesis rather than an exclusive computational capability.
Third, full-service economics and external transfer are unresolved.
Finally, general learning of unresolved mathematical truth, automatic
selection of counterfactual semantics, and a complete adaptive
model-revision controller remain beyond what was established.

The priority is \textbf{one concrete repeated-decision application of
retained partial results}, with real source access, changing readouts,
and prices. It should compare direct queries, generic MIP/disjunctive
methods, structural shortcuts, and the specialized range service under
identical information and full accounting. This tests whether the useful
information structure survives a natural acquisition regime. In
parallel, a tightly bounded specialist comparison could resolve N16's
remaining analytic novelty question; another large random numerical
panel would be less informative. The preserved portfolio offers
alternatives if either access economics or novelty fails.

The recommendation is \textbf{ready to proceed to P3-D's final audit and
author decision at the stated bounded scope}. This is a readiness
recommendation for a completed synthesis, not a declaration that phase
three is complete, that every secondary result is confirmed, or that the
broadest five questions have been solved.
