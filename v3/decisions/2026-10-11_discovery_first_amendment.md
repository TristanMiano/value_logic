Status: **prospective research direction adopted; discovery execution unstarted**.

## Author direction and reason

The author identifies a recurring allocation and advancement problem: a narrow
candidate is selected with too little generative search, then receives extensive
testing. Completing that test, including an adverse result, becomes a reason to
advance. Sessions end near their minimum durations, and recurrence is not
recommended despite the continuing desire for a useful positive construction.

The author requests substantially more hypothesis generation and derivation,
cheap early elimination, fewer and longer discovery tasks with floors of four
hours or more, and frequent recurrence over potentially tens or hundreds of
hours. Expensive adversarial challenges should follow discovery of candidates
with an estimated 80–90% prospect of rigorously establishing a meaningful
positive result. Open-ended search has no mandatory scientific stopping point.

The operational correction is to change both effort allocation and advancement.
Replacing Research90 with Research240 while retaining completion after one
candidate's rejection would not implement this direction.

## Prospective decisions

1. Select **P3-SEARCH-01**, one continuing discovery program across the five
   phase-three questions. Its candidates are revisable branches within that
   program. The first cycle, **P3-SEARCH-01-C001**, is selected and unstarted,
   with a fresh **240-minute D+L+E floor**, a **360-minute central / 480-minute
   high first-cycle forecast**, and no total program ceiling.
2. Make generation, derivation and constructive development the default.
   Initial purpose allocation is **75% generation/development, 20% cheap
   screening, 5% research synthesis/readiness**, reviewed approximately over
   two discovery cycles. These are planning priorities, not demonstrated
   optimal proportions or retrospective accounting quotas.
3. At a floor or a candidate rejection, normally **continue or recur** in the
   same program. Preserve unfinished commitments across sessions. Research
   cycles may substantially exceed their floors. A pause records its actual
   reason and leaves the program open; it does not establish scientific success.
4. Introduce **P3-POS01**, the outstanding requirement for a materially useful
   positive construction, mechanism, guarantee or demonstrated resource/capability
   benefit connected to the author's objectives. Neither a rejected hypothesis
   nor the already supported modest P3-N01 scope discharges it.
5. Defer **P3-11** until a candidate meets the evidence and forecast criteria in
   the [amended protocol](../RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md). The 80% minimum intended
   readiness level and 90% preferred level concern a defined positive
   verification event. They require an honest, evidence-backed forecast;
   unassessed confidence remains unassessed. No elapsed duration guarantees
   discovery or validates a probability estimate.
6. Freeze the promoted candidate and its confirmation procedure at that later
   boundary. Discovery remains adaptable. Empirical confirmation needs fresh or
   appropriately separated evidence; mathematical confirmation needs a valid
   proof under explicit assumptions. Retain all relevant negative and inconclusive
   outcomes, including unsuccessful confirmation attempts.
7. Routine continuation and recurrence within the selected program do not need
   a new permission request after each cycle. The author retains control over
   redirection, pausing and phase gates. Research recurrence here means continued
   work across cycles and resumptions; this decision supplies no calendar cadence.

The [discovery program](../discovery/P3_SEARCH_01.md) makes the first cycle,
search directions, continuation rules and promotion evidence concrete. This
amendment selects that next work; it does not claim that its four-hour research
commitment has already begun or been fulfilled.
